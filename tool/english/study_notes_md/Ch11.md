## Chapter Content (English)

## Conditionals & Wish/If Only Clauses

**Definition:** **Conditionals and Wish Clauses** (ቁርጠኛ ምሉእ ሓሳባትን ናይ ትምኒት ሓረጋትን) are syntactic structures used to express hypothetical, imaginary, or real situations and their corresponding results. Conditionals establish a cause-and-effect relationship based on a specific condition, while Wish/If Only clauses express regret or desire for a different reality.

English conditional sentences consist of an 'if' clause (the condition) and a main clause (the result). The system is categorized into four primary types. The Zero Conditional ($$\text{If} + \text{Present Simple}, \text{Present Simple}$$) expresses scientific facts, general laws of nature, and absolute certainties. The First Conditional ($$\text{If} + \text{Present Simple}, \text{will} + \text{V}_{\text{base}}$$) describes real, highly probable future situations. A major L1-induced error on exams is inserting *will* in the conditional 'if' clause (e.g., writing "If it *will* rain tomorrow, we will stay home" instead of "If it *rains*..."). This error occurs because Tigrinya morphologically codes the future prefix "ክ-" in both the condition and the result clauses (e.g., "እንተ ዝናብ ክዘንብ እዩ... ክንጸንሕ ኢና"). Students must learn that the 'if' clause in a real future condition must strictly remain in the Simple Present.

The Second Conditional ($$\text{If} + \text{Past Simple}, \text{would} + \text{V}_{\text{base}}$$) is used for unreal, imaginary, or highly improbable situations in the present or future. For formal, academic English exams, a critical subjunctive rule applies: the verb **were** must be used for all grammatical persons instead of *was* (e.g., "If I *were* you, I would accept the scholarship offer"). Tigrinya uses a similar past-conditional particle "እንተዝ-... ነይሩ" to express hypothetical states. However, because Tigrinya does not have a formal subjunctive verb shift like "was" to "were", students often fall into the trap of choosing "was" on matriculation exams, which is considered a grammatical error in formal English.

The Third Conditional ($$\text{If} + \text{Past Perfect}, \text{would have} + \text{V}_3$$) describes unreal or hypothetical past situations, often expressing regret or criticism for actions that cannot be changed. Students often make the mistake of using "would have" in both clauses (e.g., writing $$\times \text{If I would have studied, I would have passed}$$). The 'if' clause must strictly use the Past Perfect ($$\text{had} + \text{V}_3$$). Mixed Conditionals connect an unreal past condition to an ongoing present result ($$\text{If} + \text{Past Perfect}, \text{would} + \text{V}_{\text{base}}$$, e.g., "If I *had studied* computer science in college, I *would be* a software engineer today"). This is a highly tested, advanced grammar structure on national exams.

Regret and desire are expressed using **Wish** and **If Only** clauses, which require a shift into an "unreal" past tense. Present regrets (ጣዕሳ ንህልው ኩነታት) require the Past Simple ($$\text{Subject} + \text{wish} + \text{Subject} + \text{Past Simple}$$, e.g., "I wish I *knew* the answer now" — meaning I do not know it). Past regrets (ጣዕሳ ንሕሉፍ ኩነታት) require the Past Perfect ($$\text{Subject} + \text{wish} + \text{Subject} + \text{Past Perfect}$$, e.g., "I wish I *had prepared* for the exam yesterday"). To express a complaint or a desire for future change, English uses *would* ($$\text{Subject} + \text{wish} + \text{Subject} + \text{would} + \text{V}_{\text{base}}$$, e.g., "I wish you *would stop* making noise"). Tigrinya speakers often struggle with these tense shifts because Tigrinya uses non-past verb structures combined with particles like "ምነወየኒ" or "ምተመነኹ" to express present wishes.

Advanced conditional structures can also undergo **inversion** (ገላቢጦሽ መዋቕር), where the word "if" is omitted and the auxiliary verb is placed before the subject. First conditional inversion uses *should* ($$\text{Should you see him} + \dots$$), second conditional inversion uses *were* ($$\text{Were I you} + \dots$$), and third conditional inversion uses *had* ($$\text{Had I known} + \dots$$). These inverted forms are common on university entrance exams. To solve these questions quickly, students should use the **Clause-Match Diagnostic Strategy**: first, identify the verb form in the provided clause; second, match it to the corresponding conditional formula; and third, if the introductory "if" is missing, look for an inverted auxiliary verb at the front of the clause.

| Conditional Type | 'If' Clause Pattern | Main Clause Pattern | Tigrinya Concept Equivalent |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Zero Conditional** | $$\text{If} + \text{Present Simple}$$ | $$\text{Present Simple}$$ | ሓቂ/ተፈጥሮኣዊ ሕጊ (Scientific fact) |
| **1st Conditional** | $$\text{If} + \text{Present Simple}$$ | $$\text{will} + \text{V}_{\text{base}}$$ | ክኸውን ዝኽእል መጻኢ (Possible future) |
| **2nd Conditional** | $$\text{If} + \text{Past Simple}$$ | $$\text{would} + \text{V}_{\text{base}}$$ | ዘይጭቡጥ ህልው/መጻኢ (Hypothetical present) |
| **3rd Conditional** | $$\text{If} + \text{Past Perfect}$$ | $$\text{would have} + \text{V}_3$$ | ሕሉፍ ጣዕሳ/ዘይተፈጸመ (Past regret) |
| **Mixed Conditional** | $$\text{If} + \text{Past Perfect}$$ | $$\text{would} + \text{V}_{\text{base}}$$ | ሕሉፍ ምኽንያት ህልው ውጽኢት (Past cause, present result) |

> **💡 Did You Know?**: The word *unless* means "if... not" ($$\text{unless} = \text{if} + \text{not}$$) and is used to introduce negative conditions! Because *unless* already contains a negative meaning, you must never use a negative verb in the *unless* clause. For example, "Unless you study, you will fail" is correct, but "Unless you *don't* study..." is a double negative error! This is a very common trap on matriculation exams!

> **🧠 Pro-Tip**: To easily solve **inverted third conditional** questions (e.g., "Had I known the truth, I ________ differently"), remember the **Had-Would Have** rule! Whenever a sentence starts with **"Had I/he/she..."** followed by a past participle ($$\text{V}_3$$), it is an inverted third conditional. The other clause must strictly contain **would have + V3** ($$\text{would have} + \text{V}_3$$). Spotting this starting pattern allows you to find the correct option instantly! 

### 📝 Topic Challenge (Moderate to Hard)

1. Choose the grammatically correct sentence.
A. If I would have known you were in the hospital, I would have visited you.
B. If I had known you were in the hospital, I would have visited you.
C. If I had known you were in the hospital, I had visited you.
D. Had I known you were in the hospital, I would visit you.
*Answer: B. This is a Third Conditional sentence. The 'if' clause must use the Past Perfect ("had known") and the main clause must use "would have + V3" ("would have visited"). Option A incorrectly uses "would have" in the 'if' clause, and Option D has a tense mismatch in the result clause.*

2. Fill in the blank: "If I ________ you, I would apply for that regional scholarship immediately."
A. was
B. were
C. am
D. had been
*Answer: B. This is a Second Conditional sentence. In formal academic English, the subjunctive "were" must strictly be used for all subjects instead of "was".*

3. Choose the correct verb form: "I failed the physics test yesterday; I wish I ________ harder during the weekends."
A. studied
B. have studied
C. had studied
D. would study
*Answer: C. This sentence describes a past regret ("yesterday"). Past regrets using "wish" strictly require the Past Perfect tense ("had studied"), making C the correct option.*

4. Complete the sentence: "________ you encounter any difficulties with the registration process, please contact the administrative office."
A. Had
B. Should
C. Were
D. Unless
*Answer: B. This is an inverted First Conditional sentence. "Should you encounter..." is equivalent to "If you encounter...". Option A is for third conditional inversion, and Option C is for second conditional inversion.*

5. Choose the correct option: "If Dawit had studied computer science in college, he ________ a software engineer now."
A. would have been
B. would be
C. will be
D. is
*Answer: B. This is a Mixed Conditional sentence. The condition is in the past ("had studied"), but the result is in the present ("now"). This past-present connection requires "would + V_base" ("would be") in the result clause.*

## Review Exercises (30)

### 1. If the temperature ________ below zero degrees Celsius, water automatically turns into ice.

*Type:* MC  |  *Topic:* Zero Conditional

- will drop
- drops
- dropped
- would drop

**Answer:** drops

**Explanation:**

**Step 1: Analyze the grammatical structure**
This sentence describes a scientific fact (general truth), requiring the Zero Conditional structure: $$\text{If} + \text{Present Simple}, \text{Present Simple}$$.

**Step 2: Identify the matriculation trap**
Students often choose 'will drop' (Option A) because they assume all conditionals require 'will' in the result. However, for absolute scientific certainties, both clauses must strictly remain in the Present Simple. 'The temperature' is singular, so 'drops' (Option B) is correct.

**Step 3: Tigrinya Explanation (ትርጉም)**
እዚ ምሉእ ሓሳብ ተፈጥሮኣዊ ሓቂ (scientific fact) ስለ ዝገልጽ፡ ኣብቲ ዜሮ ደረጃ ቁርጠኛ (Zero Conditional) እዩ ዝስራዕ። ቀመሩ ድማ ድሕሪ 'If' ዘሎ ሓረግ ይኹን እቲ ስዓቢ ሓረግ ክልቲኦም ብ Simple Present ክጸሓፉ ኣለዎም። ስለዚ ምስቲ ነጸላ ዋና ባእታ ዝሰማማዕ ግሲ 'drops' (Option B) እዩ።


### 2. If I ________ you, I would accept the administrative job offer without any hesitation.

*Type:* MC  |  *Topic:* Second Conditional Subjunctive

- was
- were
- am
- had been

**Answer:** were

**Explanation:**

**Step 1: Analyze the grammatical structure**
This is a Second Conditional sentence expressing an unreal, hypothetical situation in the present ('I would accept').

**Step 2: Identify the matriculation trap**
In formal academic English, the subjunctive **were** must be used for all subjects (including I, he, she, it) in Second Conditional 'if' clauses. Choosing 'was' (Option A) is a common colloquial trap on exams. Option B is correct.

**Step 3: Tigrinya Explanation (ትርጉም)**
እዚ ምሉእ ሓሳብ ዘይጭቡጥ ህልው ኩነታት (Second Conditional) የርእየና። ኣብዚ ወግዓዊ ኣጸሓሕፋ እንግሊዘኛ ድሕሪ 'If' ዘሎ ዋና ባእታ 'I' እንተኾነ እኳ፡ እቲ ግሲ 'was' ናብቲ ንኹሉ ዝኸውን ስዑር ቅጥዒ 'were' (Option B) ክቕየር ኣለዎ (If I were you - ኣነ ኣብ ቦታኻ እንተዝኸውን)።


### 3. Had the rescue team arrived twenty minutes earlier, they ________ the valuable documents from the fire.

*Type:* MC  |  *Topic:* Inverted Conditionals

- would save
- would have saved
- had saved
- will have saved

**Answer:** would have saved

**Explanation:**

**Step 1: Analyze the grammatical structure**
The sentence begins with the inverted auxiliary 'Had' ('Had the rescue team arrived...'), which is an inverted Third Conditional structure ($$\text{Had} + \text{Subject} + \text{V}_3 \dots$$). The main clause must use the formula: $$\text{would have} + \text{V}_3$$.

**Step 2: Identify the matriculation trap**
Students are often confused by the lack of 'if' at the beginning of the sentence. Recognizing 'Had... arrived' as an inverted conditional makes the correct choice 'would have saved' (Option B) clear.

**Step 3: Tigrinya Explanation (ትርጉም)**
እዚ ምሉእ ሓሳብ ተገላቢጦሽ ሳልሳይ ደረጃ ቁርጠኛ (Inverted Third Conditional) እዩ። 'Had the rescue team arrived' ዝብል ሓረግ ልክዕ ከም 'If the rescue team had arrived' ማለት እዩ። እቲ ስዓቢ ሓረግ ድማ ብግድን 'would have + V3' ክኸውን ስለ ዘለዎ ቅኑዕ መልሲ 'would have saved' (Option B) እዩ።


### 4. I am extremely busy with my exam preparation now; I wish I ________ more free time to rest.

*Type:* MC  |  *Topic:* Wish Clauses

- have
- had
- had had
- would have

**Answer:** had

**Explanation:**

**Step 1: Analyze the grammatical structure**
The context 'now' and 'I am extremely busy' indicates a present regret (unreal present). Present regrets using 'wish' require the Past Simple tense in the subordinate clause ($$\text{Subject} + \text{wish} + \text{Subject} + \text{Past Simple}$$).

**Step 2: Identify the matriculation trap**
Students often choose 'have' (Option A) because they see 'now'. However, because the wish is about an unreal present situation (I wish I had time, but I don't), the Past Simple 'had' (Option B) is correct.

**Step 3: Tigrinya Explanation (ትርጉም)**
እቲ ተዛራቢ 'ሕጂ ስራሕ ጸቒጡኒ ኣሎ' ብምባል ብዛዕባ ህልው እዋን ዘለዎ ጣዕሳ (present regret) ይገልጽ። ኣብ እንግሊዘኛ ንህልው ትምኒት ክንገልጽ ከለና 'wish + Past Simple' ክንጥቀም ስለ ዘለና፡ እቲ ግሲ 'had' (Option B - ምሃለወኒ ነይሩ) ይኸውን። 'have' ምጥቃም ጌጋ እዩ።


### 5. Aster ________ sick today if she hadn't eaten that contaminated street food yesterday.

*Type:* MC  |  *Topic:* Mixed Conditionals

- would not have been
- would not be
- will not be
- had not been

**Answer:** would not be

**Explanation:**

**Step 1: Analyze the grammatical structure**
This is a Mixed Conditional sentence. The condition is in the past ('if she hadn't eaten... yesterday'), but the result is in the present ('today'). This past-present connection requires the formula: $$\text{If} + \text{Past Perfect}, \text{would} + \text{V}_{\text{base}}$$.

**Step 2: Identify the matriculation trap**
Students often blindly choose 'would not have been' (Option A) because they see 'hadn't eaten' in the 'if' clause. However, the present result marker 'today' requires the mixed conditional form 'would not be' (Option B).

**Step 3: Tigrinya Explanation (ትርጉም)**
እዚ ምሉእ ሓሳብ ዝተሓዋወሰ ቁርጠኛ (Mixed Conditional) እዩ። እቲ ምኽንያት ኣብ ሕሉፍ እዩ (yesterday) እቲ ውጽኢት ግን ኣብ ህልው እዋን እዩ (today)። እዚ ሕሉፍን ህልውን ዝጥርንፍ መዋቕር ድማ 'If + Past Perfect, would + V_base' ስለ ዝኾነ፡ ቅኑዕ መልሲ 'would not be' (Option B - ሎሚ ሕማም ኣይምሃለዋን) እዩ።


### 6. Unless you ________ studying English grammar daily, you will not achieve a high score on the ESSLCE.

*Type:* MC  |  *Topic:* Unless Conjunction

- don't start
- start
- started
- will start

**Answer:** start

**Explanation:**

**Step 1: Analyze the grammatical structure**
The conjunction 'Unless' means 'if... not' and carries a negative meaning. The First Conditional formula requires: $$\text{Unless} + \text{Present Simple}, \text{will not} + \text{V}_{\text{base}}$$.

**Step 2: Identify the matriculation trap**
Because *unless* is already negative, you cannot use a negative verb like 'don't start' (Option A) in the *unless* clause. This double-negative trap is highly tested on exams. Option B 'start' is correct.

**Step 3: Tigrinya Explanation (ትርጉም)**
'Unless' (እንተዘይኮይኑ) ባዕሉ ኣሉታዊ (negative) ትርጉም ዝሓዘለ ቃል እዩ። ስለዚ ኣብ ውሽጢ እቲ ሓረግ ድርብ ኣሉታዊ (double negative) ምእንታን ከይፍጠር፡ 'don't' ክንጥቀም የብልናን። እቲ ግሲ ብኣዎንታዊ ቃና 'start' (Option B) ክጸሓፍ ኣለዎ።

### 8. ________ you see the program supervisor tomorrow, please tell him that I have finished the report.

- Were
- Should
- Would

**Answer:** Should

**Explanation:**

- stopped
- would stop

**Step 2: Identify the matriculation trap**

እዚ ምሉእ ሓሳብ ተገላቢጦሽ ካልኣይ ደረጃ ቁርጠኛ (Inverted Second Conditional) እዩ። 'Were I to be...' ዝብል ሓረግ ልክዕ ከም 'If I were...' (ኣነ እንተዝኸውን) ማለት ስለ ዝኾነ፡ ቅኑዕ መልሲ 'to be' (Option C) እዩ።


### 26. Complete the sentence with the correct conjunction: 'We will not complete the science project on time ________ everyone works harder.'

*Type:* WORKOUT  |  *Topic:* Unless Conjunction

- if
- unless
- provided that
- as long as

**Answer:** unless

**Explanation:**

**Step 1: Analyze the grammatical structure**
The main clause 'We will not complete... on time' is negative, and the conditional clause 'everyone works harder' is positive. The meaning requires a negative condition: 'if they don't work harder'. This is expressed by 'unless' ($$\text{unless} = \text{if} + \text{not}$$).

**Step 2: Identify the matriculation trap**
Students often choose 'if' (Option A), but 'if everyone works harder' would mean we won't complete the project because they work harder, which is logically incorrect. Option B 'unless' is correct.

**Step 3: Tigrinya Explanation (ትርጉም)**
እቲ ትርጉም 'ነፍሲ ወከፍ ሰብ እንተዘይጽዒሩ፡ ነቲ ፕሮጀክት ኣብ እዋኑ ክንውድኦ ኣይንኽእልን' ዝብል እዩ። 'እንተዘይኮይኑ' (if not) ንምግላጽ ድማ 'unless' (Option B) ክንጥቀም ኣለና። 'if' ምጥቃም ትርጉም የባላሽዎ።


### 27. Complete the Third Conditional sentence: 'If you ________ (study) the grammar modules, you would have passed the exam.'

- would have studied
- has studied

**Explanation:**

The main clause uses 'would have passed' ($$\text{would have} + \text{V}_3$$), which indicates a Third Conditional structure. The 'if' clause must strictly use the Past Perfect tense ($$\text{If} + \text{Past Perfect}$$).

### 28. Complete the Future Wish sentence: 'I wish you ________ (stop) complaining about the weather; there is nothing we can do about it.'

*Type:* WORKOUT  |  *Topic:* Wish Clauses

- stop
- stopped
- would stop
- had stopped

**Answer:** would stop

**Explanation:**

The speaker is expressing annoyance and a desire for someone else's behavior to change in the future. This requires the structure: $$\text{Subject} + \text{wish} + \text{Subject} + \text{would} + \text{V}_{\text{base}}$$.

### 29. Complete the Second Conditional sentence: 'If they had enough funding, they ________ (build) a new laboratory.'

*Type:* WORKOUT  |  *Topic:* Second Conditional

- will build
- would build
- would have built
- built

**Answer:** would build

**Explanation:**

**Step 1: Analyze the grammatical structure**
The 'if' clause uses 'had' ($$\text{Past Simple}$$), which indicates a Second Conditional structure. The main clause must use 'would' followed by the base verb: $$\text{would} + \text{V}_{\text{base}}$$.

**Step 2: Identify the matriculation trap**

### 30. Complete the sentence with the correct inverted conditional form: '________ you study hard, you will pass the national examination.'

*Type:* WORKOUT  |  *Topic:* Conditionals

- Should
- Provided that
- Unless
- Had

**Answer:** Provided that

**Explanation:**

**Step 1: Analyze the grammatical structure**
The sentence expresses a positive condition: 'if you study hard, you will pass'. This is equivalent to 'provided that' or 'as long as'.

**Step 2: Identify the matriculation trap**
Option A 'Should' is for first conditional inversion, but here there is no inverted verb order ('you study', not 'study you'). Option B 'Provided that' is the correct conjunction to introduce a positive condition.
