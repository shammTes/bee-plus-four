## Chapter Content (English)

## Active and Passive Voice

**Definition:** **Active and Passive Voice** (ጡሩፍን ጥንሳዊን መዋቕር ምሉእ ሓሳብ) refers to the grammatical system that determines whether the subject of a sentence performs the action expressed by the verb ($$\text{Active Voice}$$) or receives the action of the verb ($$\text{Passive Voice}$$).

In English, voice is a critical syntactic feature. Active Voice ($$\text{Subject} + \text{Verb} + \text{Object}$$) places the doer of the action in the primary subject slot. Passive Voice ($$\text{Subject} + \text{be} + \text{V}_3 + [\text{by } \text{agent}]$$) shifts the focus to the receiver of the action, converting the active object into the passive subject. The tense of the active verb strictly dictates the form of the auxiliary *be* that must be introduced in the passive. For example, simple present *writes* becomes *is written*, simple past *wrote* becomes *was written*, present progressive *is writing* becomes *is being written*, and present perfect *has written* becomes *has been written*. Contrast this with Tigrinya, where the passive voice is formed morphologically by adding the prefix "ተ-" (te-) directly to the verb root (e.g., "ሰበረ" [he broke] becomes "ተሰብረ" [it was broken]). Because Tigrinya lacks an auxiliary verb equivalent to *be* for forming passives, Tigrinya speakers frequently omit the *be* auxiliary in English (e.g., writing "The bridge constructed in 2018" instead of "The bridge *was* constructed in 2018"), which is a highly penalized error on national exams.

Agent omission (ምጉዳፍ ፈጻሚ ተግባር) is a core stylistic feature of the passive voice. In academic, scientific, and journalistic contexts, the agent (expressed via the prepositional phrase $$\text{by} + \text{agent}$$) is routinely omitted when the performer of the action is obvious, unknown, or unimportant (e.g., "The thief was arrested" [by the police], or "The chemical experiment was conducted" [by the researchers]). In Tigrinya, active sentences with plural, unspecified third-person pronouns are often used where English prefers a passive with an omitted agent (e.g., "ሰረቕዎ" [they stole it] vs. "It was stolen"). This L1 preference can make students write active sentences in English when a passive structure with agent omission would be stylistically and grammatically superior.

When passive voice is applied to ditransitive verbs (ግሶች ክልተ ተቐበልቲ ዘለዎም) — verbs that take both a Direct Object (DO) and an Indirect Object (IO), such as *give, offer, send, show, teach, lend* — two passive pathways are syntactically valid. Pathway A places the Indirect Object (the person receiving the item) in the subject position: $$\text{IO} + \text{be} + \text{V}_3 + \text{DO}$$ (e.g., "The student was awarded a prestigious scholarship"). Pathway B places the Direct Object (the item being given) in the subject position: $$\text{DO} + \text{be} + \text{V}_3 + \text{to/for} + \text{IO}$$ (e.g., "A prestigious scholarship was awarded *to* the student"). National matriculation exams frequently test Pathway B, presenting distractors that omit the essential preposition *to* or *for*, trapping students who translate directly from Tigrinya without inserting the required preposition.

Impersonal reporting structures (ዘይውልቃዊ ናይ ጸብጻብ ጥንሳዊ መዋቕር) are advanced passive constructions used with verbs of opinion, belief, or saying (*say, believe, think, report, claim, rumor, estimate*). There are two primary structures to express these ideas: Structure 1 utilizes an introductory 'It' pattern: $$\text{It} + \text{be} + \text{V}_3 + \text{that} + \text{Clause}$$ (e.g., "It is believed that the suspect escaped"). Structure 2 utilizes a subject-infinitive pattern: $$\text{Subject} + \text{be} + \text{V}_3 + \text{to-infinitive}$$ (e.g., "The suspect is believed to have escaped"). If the reported action occurred *prior* to the reporting, a perfect infinitive must be used in Structure 2: $$\text{Subject} + \text{be} + \text{V}_3 + \text{to have} + \text{V}_3$$ (e.g., "The suspect *is believed to have fled* the country last night"). This is one of the highest-yield, most heavily tested grammar items on national university entrance exams.

Passives in progressive and perfect tenses require precise auxiliary matching. Progressive passives require the insertion of the progressive auxiliary **being** ($$\text{be} + \text{being} + \text{V}_3$$, e.g., "The classroom *is being cleaned* right now"). Perfect passives require the perfect aspect auxiliary **been** ($$\text{have/has/had} + \text{been} + \text{V}_3$$, e.g., "The document *has been signed*"). Tigrinya speakers often confuse *being* and *been* due to phonetic similarities and L1 vowel-system interference. On matriculation exams, always double-check the aspect: if the action is currently in progress, look for *being*; if the action has been completed, look for *been*.

To succeed on national exams, candidates should apply the **Inanimate Subject Probe Strategy**: when analyzing a sentence completion or error identification item, first identify the subject. If the subject is an inanimate object (e.g., *bridge, report, document, road*) that cannot physically perform the action of the verb, the sentence *must* be passive. Once a passive requirement is identified, scan the sentence for time-markers (e.g., *yesterday, tomorrow, recently, right now*), match the time-marker to its corresponding tense, and select the option that correctly implements the $$\text{be} + \text{V}_3$$ construction.

| Active Tense/Pattern | Passive Form/Pattern | Tigrinya Concept Equivalent / Note |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $$\text{S} + \text{writes} + \text{O}$$ | $$\text{S} + \text{is written} + [\text{by } \text{agent}]$$ | ይጸሓፍ (Tigrinya uses passive prefix 'ተ-') |
| $$\text{S} + \text{is writing} + \text{O}$$ | $$\text{S} + \text{is being written} + [\text{by } \text{agent}]$$ | ይጸሓፍ ኣሎ (Progressive passive requires 'being') |
| $$\text{S} + \text{wrote} + \text{O}$$ | $$\text{S} + \text{was written} + [\text{by } \text{agent}]$$ | ተጻሒፉ (Simple past passive requires 'was/were') |
| $$\text{S} + \text{has written} + \text{O}$$ | $$\text{S} + \text{has been written} + [\text{by } \text{agent}]$$ | ተጻሒፉ ኣሎ (Perfect passive requires 'been') |
| $$\text{S} + \text{will write} + \text{O}$$ | $$\text{S} + \text{will be written} + [\text{by } \text{agent}]$$ | ክጸሓፍ እዩ (Future passive requires 'be') |

> **💡 Did You Know?**: In English legal, scientific, and academic writing, the passive voice is highly preferred because it emphasizes objective facts and processes rather than the individuals performing them. For example, in a lab report, scientists write "The solution was heated" instead of "I heated the solution" to keep the focus entirely on the science! This is why reading academic passages requires a strong command of passive syntax.

> **🧠 Pro-Tip**: To solve advanced impersonal reporting questions (e.g., "The suspect is rumored ________ last night"), analyze the timeline. 
- If the rumor is happening **now** about a **present** action, use a simple infinitive ($$\text{to } \text{V}_{\text{base}}$$ or $$\text{to be} + \text{V-ing}$$, e.g., *to be hiding*).
- If the rumor is happening **now** about a completed **past** action (signaled by *"last night"* or *"yesterday"*), always choose the **perfect infinitive** ($$\text{to have} + \text{V}_3$$, e.g., *to have escaped*). This distinction is a classic high-stakes exam filter!

### 📝 Topic Challenge (Moderate to Hard)

1. Choose the grammatically correct passive transformation of: "The university awarded Aster a gold medal yesterday."
A. Aster awarded a gold medal yesterday by the university.
B. Aster was awarded a gold medal yesterday by the university.
C. A gold medal awarded to Aster yesterday by the university.
D. A gold medal was awarded Aster yesterday by the university.
*Answer: B. "Awarded" is past simple. Pathway A is used here, making "Aster" the passive subject, which requires the past auxiliary "was" and past participle "awarded". Option C and D are incorrect because Option C lacks the auxiliary "was", and Option D lacks the required preposition "to" before the indirect object "Aster".*

2. Fill in the blank: "The massive regional construction project ________ by the end of next semester."
A. will have completed
B. will have been completed
C. is completing
D. has been completed
*Answer: B. The subject "project" is inanimate, requiring the passive voice. The future time-marker "By the end of next semester" requires the Future Perfect Passive ("will have been completed").*

3. Choose the correct passive reporting form: "The ancient manuscript ________ by local historians during the war."
A. is believed to destroy
B. is believed to have been destroyed
C. believes to be destroyed
D. is believed to have destroyed
*Answer: B. The reporting is in the present ("is believed"), but the destruction happened in the past ("during the war"). This past action requires a perfect passive infinitive ("to have been destroyed"). Option D is active and incorrect.*

4. Complete the sentence: "Our high school classroom ________ at the moment, so we are studying in the garden."
A. is painting
B. is being painted
C. has painted
D. is painted
*Answer: B. The time-marker "at the moment" indicates an action in progress, and the inanimate subject "classroom" requires the passive voice, leading to the Present Progressive Passive ("is being painted").*

5. Identify the error: "A new modern health center is being construct in our village to provide better medical services."
*Answer: The error is "construct". In passive voice, the verb must always be in its past participle form ($$\text{V}_3$$) after "being". It should be "constructed" instead of "construct".*

## Review Exercises (30)

### 1. The official report on the regional economic development ________ by the committee before the deadline last night.

*Type:* MC  |  *Topic:* Past Simple Passive

- submitted
- was submitted
- has been submitted
- was submitting

**Answer:** was submitted

**Explanation:**

**Step 1: Analyze the grammatical structure**
The subject 'The official report' is inanimate and cannot perform the action of submitting. This requires the passive voice. The time-marker 'last night' indicates the Simple Past tense, requiring the past passive form ($$\text{was/were} + \text{V}_3$$).

**Step 2: Identify the matriculation trap**
Students often choose the active form 'submitted' (Option A) due to direct translation, or 'has been submitted' (Option C), which is present perfect and cannot be paired with the definite past time-marker 'last night'.

**Step 3: Tigrinya Explanation (ትርጉም)**
'The official report' (እቲ ወግዓዊ ጸብጻብ) ህይወት ኣልቦ ስለ ዝኾነ፡ እቲ ምሉእ ሓሳብ ብጥንሳዊ መዋቕር (passive) ክጸሓፍ ኣለዎ። እቲ ናይ ግዜ መመልከቲ 'last night' (ትማሊ ምሸት) ስለ ዝኾነ ድማ ምስኡ ዝኸይድ Past Simple passive 'was submitted' (Option B - ቀረበ) ቅኑዕ መልሲ ይኸውን።


### 2. A massive amount of money ________ currently ________ to fund the construction of the new school library.

*Type:* MC  |  *Topic:* Present Progressive Passive

- is / raising
- is / being raised
- has / raised
- are / being raised

**Answer:** is / being raised

**Explanation:**

**Step 1: Analyze the grammatical structure**
The subject is 'A massive amount of money' (singular and inanimate), requiring the passive voice. The time-marker 'currently' indicates an action in progress, requiring the Present Progressive Passive ($$\text{is/are} + \text{being} + \text{V}_3$$).

**Step 2: Identify the matriculation trap**
Students often choose 'is raising' (Option A) because they confuse active and passive progressive forms. Since 'money' cannot raise itself, the passive 'is being raised' (Option B) is correct.

**Step 3: Tigrinya Explanation (ትርጉም)**

### 3. The high school teacher ________ a beautiful traditional gift by the students on her graduation day.

*Type:* MC  |  *Topic:* Double-Object Passives

- gave
- was given
- was giving
- has given

**Answer:** was given

**Explanation:**

**Step 1: Analyze the grammatical structure**
This is a double-object passive where the Indirect Object 'The high school teacher' is placed in the subject position: $$\text{IO} + \text{be} + \text{V}_3 + \text{DO}$$.

**Step 2: Identify the matriculation trap**
Students often choose 'gave' (Option A) because they see a person as the subject and assume the active voice. However, the context shows she received the gift, requiring the passive form 'was given' (Option B).
