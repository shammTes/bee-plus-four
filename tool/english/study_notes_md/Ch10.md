## Chapter Content (English)

## Direct and Indirect (Reported) Speech

**Definition:** **Direct and Indirect Speech** (ቃል ብቓሉን ዝተሓበረን ዘረባ) refers to the grammatical mechanisms used to convey the spoken words or thoughts of a speaker, either by quoting them verbatim ($$\text{Direct Speech}$$) or by integrating them into another clause with shifts in tense, pronouns, and time-markers ($$\text{Indirect/Reported Speech}$$).

When converting Direct Speech into Indirect Speech, the most critical grammatical rule is **backshifting** (ድሕሪት ምስጓም እዋን). Backshifting is mandatory when the reporting verb (ግብጻዊ ግሲ) is in the past tense (e.g., *said, told, asked, explained*). Under this rule, tenses must shift back one step into the past: Simple Present shifts to Simple Past ($$\text{Simple Present} \rightarrow \text{Simple Past}$$), Present Continuous shifts to Past Continuous ($$\text{Present Continuous} \rightarrow \text{Past Continuous}$$), and both Simple Past and Present Perfect shift to Past Perfect ($$\text{Simple Past / Present Perfect} \rightarrow \text{Past Perfect}$$). Modals also undergo shifts: *will* becomes *would*, *can* becomes *could*, and *must* becomes *had to*. Tigrinya does not possess a systematic morphological backshifting rule; instead, it reports speech by embedding the direct clause using quotative particles like "ኢሉ" (saying) or "ከምዚ ከም ዝበለ" (that he said this) without altering the tense of the original utterance. This structural difference makes English backshifting a high-frequency source of errors on matriculation exams.

In addition to tense backshifting, reported speech requires temporal and locative deictic shifts (ለውጢ መመልከቲ ቃላት ግዜን ቦታን). Because the time and place of reporting are usually different from the original utterance, spatial and temporal markers must shift: *now* becomes *then*, *today* becomes *that day*, *yesterday* becomes *the previous day / the day before*, *tomorrow* becomes *the following day / the next day*, *here* becomes *there*, and *this* becomes *that*. Tigrinya speakers often carry over direct temporal markers (e.g., using "yesterday" directly in an indirect report), which is a common diagnostic error targeted on the ESSLCE.

Pronoun concord (ምስምማዕ ተውላጠ ስማት) is also required to align the reported utterance with the reporter's perspective. Subject, object, possessive, and reflexive pronouns must shift. For example, if Speaker A says, "My father gave me a laptop," and Speaker B reports it, it becomes, "He said that *his* father had given *him* a laptop." Tigrinya speakers often experience cognitive load during this pronoun shifting because Tigrinya allows mixed direct/indirect reporting styles where pronouns can remain unshifted or align with different honorific systems (e.g., using respectful plurals like "ንስኹም" for singular individuals).

Reporting questions represents another high-yield exam filter. The most critical rule is **No Inversion**: once a question is reported, it becomes a noun clause, losing its question word order and its question mark. The structure must shift from interrogative to declarative ($$\text{Wh-word} + \text{Subject} + \text{Verb}$$). For example, the direct question "Where are you going?" becomes "He asked me where I was going" (not "where was I going"). For Yes/No questions, the complementizers *if* or *whether* must be introduced: $$\text{if/whether} + \text{Subject} + \text{Verb}$$ (e.g., "Are you hungry?" becomes "He asked if I was hungry"). Additionally, the dummy auxiliary *do/does/did* is completely removed in reported questions.

Reporting commands, requests, and warnings involves infinitive clauses ($$\text{reporting verb} + \text{object} + \text{to-infinitive}$$, e.g., "Sit down!" becomes "The teacher told us to sit down"). For negative commands, the negative particle *not* must strictly precede the infinitive: $$\text{not to} + \text{V}_{\text{base}}$$ (e.g., "Don't touch the wire!" becomes "He warned us *not to touch* the wire"). Placing *not* after *to* ($$\times \text{to not touch}$$) is a common syntactic trap on national exams. Suggestion structures can also be reported using a gerund or a *that*-clause with *should* (e.g., "Let's study" becomes "He suggested studying" or "He suggested that we should study").

There are two important exceptions where backshifting is *not* applied: 1) if the reporting verb is in the present, present perfect, or future tense (e.g., "He says he is tired"); 2) if the reported statement expresses an ongoing general truth or scientific fact (e.g., "The teacher said that the earth revolves around the sun"). To succeed on exam items, students must apply the **Reporting Verb Probe Strategy**: first, identify the tense of the reporting verb; second, check for required pronoun and deictic shifts; and third, if reporting a question, confirm that the clause follows a strict declarative word order.

| Direct Speech Pattern | Reported Speech Pattern | Tigrinya Concept Equivalent / Note |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $$\text{Simple Present (write)}$$ | $$\text{Simple Past (wrote)}$$ | Simple Past (Tigrinya does not shift tense) |
| $$\text{Simple Past (wrote)}$$ | $$\text{Past Perfect (had written)}$$ | Past Perfect (Expresses earlier past) |
| $$\text{Present Perfect (has written)}$$ | $$\text{Past Perfect (had written)}$$ | Past Perfect (Expresses completed past) |
| $$\text{Wh-question (Where is he?)}$$ | $$\text{Wh-clause (where he was)}$$ | Declarative word order (No inversion!) |
| $$\text{Negative Command (Don't go)}$$ | $$\text{Infinitive (not to go)}$$ | 'not to' precedes the base verb |

> **💡 Did You Know?**: The difference between the reporting verbs *say* and *tell* is a common exam target! *Say* does not require a personal indirect object (e.g., "He said that he was tired"), whereas *tell* must strictly be followed by a personal object (e.g., "He told *me* that he was tired"). Writing "He said me..." or "He told that..." are major grammatical errors in reported speech!

> **🧠 Pro-Tip**: In reported questions, always look for the word order. If you see a question word (like *where, why, how, what*) followed immediately by an auxiliary verb (e.g., *was, did, had*) before the subject, that option is **immediately incorrect** on your exam! The correct pattern is always: $$\text{Question Word} + \text{Subject} + \text{Verb}$$ (e.g., *where I was*, *what she wanted*).

### 📝 Topic Challenge (Moderate to Hard)

1. Choose the grammatically correct reported speech: "Where did you spend your vacation?" she asked me.
A. She asked me where did I spend my vacation.
B. She asked me where I spent my vacation.
C. She asked me where I had spent my vacation.
D. She asked me where had I spent my vacation.
*Answer: C. Because the reporting verb "asked" is in the past, the simple past "did... spend" must backshift to the past perfect "had spent". Additionally, the reported question must follow declarative word order ("where I had spent"), making C the only correct option.*

2. Fill in the blank: "The chemistry teacher warned us ________ touch the chemical solution with our bare hands."
*Answer: "not to". Negative commands and warnings are reported using "not to" followed by the base verb ("not to touch"). "To not" is grammatically incorrect in formal English.* 

3. Choose the correct option: "The astronomy professor stated that the sun ________ in the east."
A. rose
B. rises
C. had risen
D. is rising
*Answer: B. Even though the reporting verb "stated" is in the past tense, the reported statement describes a scientific fact (general truth). General truths do not undergo backshifting, so "rises" is correct.*

4. Complete the sentence: "Aster said that she ________ her biology assignment the previous day."
A. completed
B. has completed
C. had completed
D. is completing
*Answer: C. The time-marker "the previous day" indicates that the original speech contained the Simple Past ("yesterday"), which must backshift to the Past Perfect ("had completed") in reported speech.*

5. Identify the error: "He told to me that he would arrive on the afternoon flight tomorrow."
*Answer: The error is "told to me". Unlike "said", the reporting verb "told" is transitive and must be followed directly by its personal object without the preposition "to". It should be "He told me...". In addition, "tomorrow" should shift to "the following day" in reported speech.*

### 1. The director asked me ________ to the project meeting scheduled for the next morning.

*Type:* MC  |  *Topic:* Reported Questions

- if I will come
- whether I would come
- whether would I come
- if will I come

**Answer:** whether I would come

**Explanation:**

**Step 1: Analyze the grammatical structure**
This is a reported Yes/No question. It requires the complementizer 'if' or 'whether', followed by declarative word order ($$\text{if/whether} + \text{Subject} + \text{Verb}$$). Because the reporting verb 'asked' is past, the future 'will' must backshift to 'would'.

**Step 2: Identify the matriculation trap**
Option C and Option D incorrectly invert the subject and auxiliary. Option A fails to backshift 'will' to 'would'. Only Option B correctly implements both backshifting and declarative word order.

**Step 3: Tigrinya Explanation (ትርጉም)**
እዚ ዝተሓበረ ናይ 'እወ/ኣይፋልን' ሕቶ (Reported Yes/No question) እዩ። ነዚ ክንቅይር ከለና 'whether' ወይ 'if' ተጠቒምና ስሩዕ ኣዎንታዊ ኣሰራርዓ ($$Subject + Verb$$) ክንሕዝ ኣለና። እቲ ናይ መጀመርታ 'will' ድማ ናብ 'would' ክቕየር ስለ ዘለዎ ቅኑዕ መልሲ 'whether I would come' (Option B) እዩ።

እዚ ናይ መጀመርታ ደረጃ ቁርጠኛ ምሉእ ሓሳብ (Type 1 Conditional) እዩ። ኣብ ዝተሓበረ ዘረባ ክቕየር ከሎ ግን ናብ Type 2 Conditional ይሰጋገር፤ እዚ ማለት እቲ ህልው 'have' ናብ ሕሉፍ 'had' (Option B) ክቕየር ኣለዎ።


### 29. Complete the reported speech: '"I have been researching this disease for years," the doctor said.' 
The doctor said that he ________ researching that disease for years.

*Type:* WORKOUT  |  *Topic:* Backshifting Rules

- has been
- had been
- was
- is

**Answer:** had been

**Explanation:**

**Step 1: Analyze the grammatical structure**
The Present Perfect Continuous 'have been researching' must backshift to the Past Perfect Continuous 'had been researching' when reported with a past reporting verb ('said').

**Step 2: Identify the matriculation trap**
Students often choose 'has been' (Option A) or 'was' (Option C), failing to apply backshifting. Option B 'had been' is correct.

**Step 3: Tigrinya Explanation (ትርጉም)**
እቲ ዋና ዘረባ Present Perfect Continuous ስለ ዝኾነ፡ ምስቲ 'said' ዝብል ሕሉፍ ግሲ ተሓዊሱ ናብ Past Perfect Continuous 'had been' (Option B - ክሰርሕ ከም ዝጸንሐ) ክሰግም ኣለዎ።


### 30. Complete the reported speech: '"Whose jacket is this?" Aster asked.' 
Aster asked whose jacket ________.

*Type:* WORKOUT  |  *Topic:* Reported Questions

- was that
- that was
- is this
- this was

**Answer:** that was

**Explanation:**

**Step 1: Analyze the grammatical structure**
This is a reported Wh-question. It must follow declarative word order ($$\text{Whose jacket} + \text{Subject} + \text{Verb}$$). The demonstrative 'this' must shift to 'that', and the present 'is' must backshift to 'was'.

**Step 2: Identify the matriculation trap**
Option A incorrectly maintains subject-verb inversion ('was that'). Option B 'that was' correctly uses declarative word order and deictic/tense shifts.

**Step 3: Tigrinya Explanation (ትርጉም)**
እዚ ዝተሓበረ ሕቶ እዩ። 'this' ዝብል ቃል ናብ 'that' ክቕየር ከሎ፡ እቲ ግሲ 'is' ድማ ናብ 'was' ክቕየር ኣለዎ። ከምኡ ውን እቲ ኣሰራርዓ ከም ስሩዕ ሓሳብ ስለ ዝኸውን 'that' ቅድሚ 'was' ክስራዕ ኣለዎ (Option B)። 'was that' (Option A) ምጥቃም ጌጋ እዩ።
