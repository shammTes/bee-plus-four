## Chapter Content (English)

## Subject-Verb Agreement (Concord)

**Definition:** **Subject-Verb Agreement** (ስምምዕ ዋና ባእታን ግሲን) refers to the syntactic rule requiring a verb to agree in grammatical number (singular or plural) and person (first, second, or third) with its corresponding subject. 

Subject-Verb Agreement (or grammatical concord) is the foundational cornerstone of English clause structure. While the basic rule dictates that a singular subject matches a singular verb and a plural subject matches a plural verb, advanced syntactic environments introduce significant complexity. In Tigrinya, verb conjugation is highly morphological and inflects not only for person and number but also for gender (e.g., "ንሱ መጺኡ" vs. "ንሳ መጺኣ"). In English, number agreement is primarily marked in the third-person singular of the simple present tense ($$\text{Verb} + \text{-s/es}$$) or through auxiliary verbs (*is/are, has/have, was/were*). This superficial simplicity often leads Tigrinya-speaking students to underestimate the rules, causing them to overlook agreement patterns when dealing with complex, multi-clause sentences.

A major source of errors on national exams is the presence of intervening phrases (ማእከላይ ሓረግ) that stand between the subject and the verb. Phrases such as *as well as, along with, together with, in addition to, accompanied by, including,* and *except* introduce extra information but do not alter the grammatical number of the subject ($$\text{Subject}_{\text{singular}} + [\text{intervening phrase}] \rightarrow \text{Singular Verb}$$). For example, in the sentence "The principal, along with his teachers, *is* attending the workshop," the true subject is the singular "principal". Students often fall into the trap of attraction, matching the verb to the nearest plural noun ("teachers") and selecting "are". Tigrinya-speaking students must train themselves to mentally delete these parenthetical structures to isolate the true subject.

Compound subjects (ድርብ ባእታታት) also present distinct challenges depending on the conjunctions used. Subjects joined by the coordinating conjunction *and* generally form a plural subject and require a plural verb ($$\text{S}_1 + \text{and} + \text{S}_2 \rightarrow \text{Plural Verb}$$). However, if the two nouns represent a single conceptual unit (e.g., *bread and butter, law and order, rice and curry*), they take a singular verb. When correlative conjunctions like *either... or, neither... nor, not only... but also* are used, the "proximity rule" (ሕጊ ቅርበት) applies: the verb must agree with the subject closest to it ($$\text{Neither } \text{S}_1 + \text{nor } \text{S}_2 \rightarrow \text{Verb agrees with } \text{S}_2$$). This is heavily tested on matriculation exams, with distractors designed to trick students who assume all compound structures are plural.

Indefinite pronouns (ዘይተወሰኑ ተውላጠ ስማት) are classified into three strict categories: singular, plural, and variable. Singular indefinite pronouns (*each, every, everyone, someone, nobody, either, neither, one*) must take singular verbs ($$\text{Each} + \text{of the} + \text{Plural Noun} \rightarrow \text{Singular Verb}$$). For example, "Each of the students *has* [not *have*] submitted the paper." Plural indefinite pronouns (*both, few, many, several*) require plural verbs. Variable pronouns, known by the mnemonic **SANAM** (*Some, Any, None, All, Most*), can be either singular or plural. Their number is determined by the object of the preposition in the following phrase (e.g., "Most of the *milk* *is* sour" vs. "Most of the *students* *are* present"). Students must analyze the entire noun phrase to apply these rules correctly.

Collective nouns (እኩብ ስማት - *committee, family, jury, team, audience*) represent groups of individuals. Their verb agreement depends on group dynamics: if the group acts as a single, unified unit, the verb is singular ($$\text{Collective Noun} \rightarrow \text{Singular Verb}$$); if the members act as separate individuals, the verb is plural. For example, "The committee *has* reached a decision" vs. "The committee *are* arguing among themselves." Additionally, students must distinguish between nouns that look singular but are always plural (*police, cattle, clergy*) and those that look plural but are always singular (*news, physics, economics, measles*). Misinterpreting these nouns is a high-frequency error on national exams.

Finally, agreement rules extend to quantities, percentages, and inverted sentences. Plural-appearing nouns representing money, time, distance, or weight are treated as single collective quantities and require singular verbs (e.g., "Ten dollars *is* too expensive," "Five miles *is* a long walk"). In inverted sentences, such as those beginning with *there is/are* or locative phrases (e.g., "Under the bridge *lie* the old documents"), the subject follows the verb. Students must look past the introductory words to find the true subject and ensure proper agreement. Mastery of these advanced concord rules is essential for achieving a high score on the English matriculation exam.

> **💡 Did You Know?**: The word *police* has been treated as a plural collective noun since its introduction into English from French in the 18th century. Unlike other collective nouns like *committee*, *police* can never take a singular verb in standard English. Thus, "The police is coming" is always grammatically incorrect, while "The police are coming" is correct!

> **🧠 Pro-Tip**: To excel on advanced relative clause agreement questions (e.g., "She is one of the girls who..."), look for the word **only**. 
- If the sentence contains **"one of the [plural noun] who..."**, the relative pronoun refers to the plural noun, requiring a **plural verb** ($$\text{who} + \text{Plural Verb}$$).
- If the sentence contains **"the *only* one of the [plural noun] who..."**, the relative pronoun refers to "the only one", requiring a **singular verb** ($$\text{who} + \text{Singular Verb}$$). This distinction is highly prized by exam writers!

### 📝 Topic Challenge (Moderate to Hard)

1. Choose the grammatically correct sentence.
A. The captain, along with his crew members, were rescued from the sinking ship.
B. The captain, along with his crew members, was rescued from the sinking ship.
C. The captain along with his crew members were rescued from the sinking ship.
D. The captain, along with his crew members was rescued from the sinking ship.
*Answer: B. The subject is "The captain" (singular). The intervening phrase "along with his crew members" does not change the subject's number. Commas are also required to set off the parenthetical phrase. This makes B correct.*

2. Fill in the blank: "Neither the laboratory technician nor the senior researchers ________ able to locate the missing blood samples."
*Answer: "were". When using "neither... nor", the verb must agree with the closer subject. Since "senior researchers" is plural, the plural past verb "were" is required.*

3. Choose the correct verb form: "Two hundred miles ________ a long distance to travel for a one-hour interview."
A. are
B. is
C. be
=D. were
*Answer: B. Plural nouns representing units of measurement (distance, money, time) are treated as single quantities, requiring the singular verb "is".*

4. Identify the error in this sentence: "Each of the modern computer systems in the laboratories require regular software updates."
*Answer: The error is "require". The true subject is "Each", which is a singular indefinite pronoun. The verb must be singular, so it should be "requires" instead of "require".*

5. Choose the correct verb form: "Aster is the only one of the applicants who ________ a master's degree in biochemistry."
A. have
B. has
C. having
D. do have
*Answer: B. Because of the word "only" in "the *only* one of", the relative pronoun "who" refers back to the singular "only one". This requires the singular verb "has".*

### 1. One of the most challenging obstacles facing high school graduates ________ the rising cost of higher education.

*Type:* MC  |  *Topic:* One of + Plural Noun

- are
- is
- were
- be

**Answer:** is

**Explanation:**

**Step 1: Analyze the grammatical structure**
The subject of the sentence is 'One' (singular) in the pattern $$\text{One of} + \text{Plural Noun} + \text{Singular Verb}$$. The plural noun phrase 'the most challenging obstacles' is part of a prepositional phrase modifying the subject.

**Step 2: Identify the matriculation trap**
Students often match the verb to the plural noun 'obstacles' which is closest to the verb slot, leading them to choose 'are' (Option A). This is a classic attraction trap in matriculation exams.

**Step 3: Tigrinya Explanation (ትርጉም)**
እቲ ቀንዲ ናይዚ ምሉእ ሓሳብ ዋና ባእታ 'One' (ሓደ - ነጸላ) እዩ። ድሕሪኡ 'of the obstacles' (ካብቶም ዕንቅፋታት) ዝብል ሓረግ ክመጽእ ከሎ፡ እቲ ግሲ ምስቲ 'One' ዝብል ነጸላ ቃል እዩ ዝሰማማዕ። ስለዚ ቅኑዕ መልሲ 'is' (Option B) እዩ።

### 3. Neither the regional program coordinator nor the senior researchers ________ present at the emergency project meeting yesterday.

*Type:* MC  |  *Topic:* Correlative Conjunctions

- was
- were
- is
- are

**Answer:** were

**Explanation:**

**Step 1: Analyze the grammatical structure**
The compound subject is connected by the correlative conjunction 'Neither... nor'. The proximity rule applies, meaning the verb agrees with the closer subject, 'the senior researchers' (plural).

**Step 2: Identify the matriculation trap**
Students may focus on 'the coordinator' (singular) and choose 'was' (Option A). The past time marker 'yesterday' eliminates present auxiliaries 'is' and 'are', leaving 'were' (Option B) as the correct choice.

**Step 3: Tigrinya Explanation (ትርጉም)**
ምሉእ ሓሳብ ብ 'Neither... nor' ክእሰር ከሎ፡ እቲ ግሲ ምስቲ ናብኡ ዝቐረበ ባእታ ይሰማማዕ (Proximity Rule)። እቲ ዝቐረበ ባእታ 'the senior researchers' (ብዙሕ) ስለ ዝኾነን እቲ ግዜ ድማ 'yesterday' (ትማሊ) ስለ ዝኾነን፡ ቅኑዕ መልሲ 'were' (Option B) እዩ።

### 29. Complete the sentence: 'Five miles ________ too far for the primary school children to walk every morning.'

*Type:* WORKOUT  |  *Topic:* Quantities & Measurements

- are
- is
- were
- be

**Answer:** is

**Explanation:**

**Step 1: Analyze the grammatical structure**
'Five miles' is a unit of distance. When representing a single collective distance, units of measurement require a singular verb ($$\text{Quantity of Distance} \rightarrow \text{Singular Verb}$$).

**Step 2: Identify the matriculation trap**
Students are often misled by 'miles' (plural) and select 'are' (Option A). The singular verb 'is' (Option B) is correct.

### 30. Complete the sentence: 'Either Almaz or her older sisters ________ responsible for cleaning the biology laboratory today.'

*Type:* WORKOUT  |  *Topic:* Correlative Conjunctions

- is
- are
- was
- has been

**Answer:** are

**Explanation:**

**Step 1: Analyze the grammatical structure**
The compound subject is connected by 'Either... or'. The proximity rule applies, meaning the verb must agree with the closer subject, 'her older sisters' (plural).

**Step 2: Identify the matriculation trap**
Students may focus on 'Almaz' (singular) and choose 'is' (Option A). The proximity rule requires the plural auxiliary 'are' (Option B) to match the closer plural subject 'sisters'.

**Step 3: Tigrinya Explanation (ትርጉም)**
ምሉእ ሓሳብ ብ 'Either... or' ክእሰር ከሎ፡ እቲ ግሲ ምስቲ ዝቐረበ ባእታ ይሰማማዕ። እቲ ዝቐረበ ባእታ 'her older sisters' (ኣሓታ - ብዙሕ ቁጽሪ) ስለ ዝኾነ፡ ቅኑዕ መልሲ 'are' (Option B) እዩ።
