Q: What should you do first if a chemical splashes into your eyes in the laboratory?
+ Rinse the eyes with plenty of running water (eyewash) for at least 15 minutes and tell the teacher.
- Rub your eyes with your hands.
- Wait to see if the pain stops by itself.
- Cover the eyes with a dry cloth and continue working.
E: Immediate flushing with lots of clean water dilutes and removes the chemical. Rubbing can spread it and damage the eye, and waiting allows more damage. Always report accidents to the teacher.

Q: When heating a liquid in a test tube, you should point the mouth of the tube:
+ away from yourself and other people
- towards your face so you can see clearly
- towards your partner
- straight down to the floor
E: A heated liquid can suddenly boil and spurt out ("bumping"). Pointing the tube away from everyone prevents burns. A test tube pointing down would spill the liquid.

Q: How many protons, neutrons and electrons are in a neutral atom of $${}_{11}^{23}\text{Na}$$?
+ 11 protons, 12 neutrons, 11 electrons
- 11 protons, 23 neutrons, 11 electrons
- 12 protons, 11 neutrons, 12 electrons
- 23 protons, 11 neutrons, 23 electrons
E: The atomic number (11) gives the number of protons. In a neutral atom, electrons = protons = 11.
Neutrons = mass number − atomic number = 23 − 11 = 12.

Q: What is the electron arrangement of a magnesium atom (atomic number 12)?
+ 2, 8, 2
- 2, 10
- 2, 2, 8
- 8, 2, 2
E: Electrons fill shells in order: the first shell holds up to 2, the second up to 8. 12 electrons: 2 in the first, 8 in the second, and 2 in the third shell.

Q: Chlorine has two isotopes: Cl-35 (75%) and Cl-37 (25%). What is its average atomic mass?
+ 35.5 u
- 36.0 u
- 35.0 u
- 37.0 u
E: Average atomic mass = (35 × 0.75) + (37 × 0.25) = 26.25 + 9.25 = 35.5 u.
36.0 u is the simple average of 35 and 37, which ignores how common each isotope is.

Q: Which is the correct formula of aluminium oxide? (Al³⁺ and O²⁻)
+ Al₂O₃
- AlO
- Al₃O₂
- AlO₃
E: The total positive and negative charges must balance. 2 Al³⁺ = +6 and 3 O²⁻ = −6, so the formula is Al₂O₃. (Criss-cross method: the charge of each ion becomes the subscript of the other.)

Q: Balance the equation: __Fe + __O₂ → __Fe₂O₃. What are the coefficients?
+ 4, 3, 2
- 2, 3, 1
- 1, 1, 1
- 4, 2, 3
E: Start with O: 3 O₂ gives 6 O atoms, which needs 2 Fe₂O₃. 2 Fe₂O₃ contains 4 Fe atoms, so we need 4 Fe.
4Fe + 3O₂ → 2Fe₂O₃. Check: Fe 4 = 4, O 6 = 6 ✓. With 2, 3, 1 the oxygen atoms do not balance (6 on the left, 3 on the right).

Q: What is the molar mass of H₂SO₄? (H = 1, S = 32, O = 16)
+ 98 g/mol
- 49 g/mol
- 96 g/mol
- 50 g/mol
E: Molar mass = 2(1) + 32 + 4(16) = 2 + 32 + 64 = 98 g/mol.

Q: How many moles are in 36 g of water? (H = 1, O = 16)
+ 2 mol
- 0.5 mol
- 18 mol
- 36 mol
E: Molar mass of H₂O = 2(1) + 16 = 18 g/mol. Moles = mass ÷ molar mass = 36 ÷ 18 = 2 mol.

Q: Which of the following is a chemical change?
+ burning wood
- melting ice
- dissolving sugar in water
- breaking a glass
E: In a chemical change, new substances are formed. Burning wood produces carbon dioxide, water and ash. Melting, dissolving and breaking change only the state, form or size; no new substance is made.

Q: Which method is best for separating salt from salt water?
+ evaporation
- filtration
- using a magnet
- decanting
E: Salt is dissolved in the water, so it passes through filter paper. Heating evaporates the water and leaves the salt behind. A magnet only separates magnetic materials like iron.

Q: Which piece of equipment is used to measure the volume of a liquid most accurately in a school laboratory?
+ burette or pipette
- beaker
- test tube
- watch glass
E: Burettes and pipettes are designed for accurate volume measurements. Beakers have only rough markings, test tubes are for holding small amounts, and a watch glass is for evaporating small samples.
