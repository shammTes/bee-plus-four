# Authored Grade 12 history top-ups (facts checked against the Grade 12 History textbook where it covers them)
@ hist12-u2
Q: In which year was the State of Israel proclaimed?
+ 1948
- 1917
- 1956
- 1967
E: Israel declared independence on 14 May 1948, when the British mandate over Palestine ended. 1917 is the Balfour Declaration, 1956 the Suez Crisis, and 1967 the Six-Day War.

Q: What event started the Suez Crisis of 1956?
+ Egypt's President Nasser nationalised the Suez Canal.
- Israel was proclaimed as a state.
- The Shah of Iran was overthrown.
- Egypt and Israel signed the Camp David Accords.
E: In July 1956 Nasser nationalised the Suez Canal Company. Britain, France and Israel then attacked Egypt, but pressure from the USA, the USSR and the UN forced them to withdraw. The other events happened in 1948, 1979 and 1978.

Q: In the Six-Day War of 1967, Israel captured which territories?
+ the West Bank, Gaza Strip, Sinai Peninsula and Golan Heights
- only the Suez Canal
- Lebanon and Jordan entirely
- Cyprus and Crete
E: In June 1967 Israel defeated Egypt, Jordan and Syria in six days and occupied the West Bank and East Jerusalem (from Jordan), Gaza and Sinai (from Egypt) and the Golan Heights (from Syria). Sinai was later returned to Egypt after the Camp David agreement.

Q: Who led the Palestine Liberation Organisation (PLO) from 1969 until his death in 2004?
+ Yasser Arafat
- Gamal Abdel Nasser
- Anwar Sadat
- Menachem Begin
E: Yasser Arafat was chairman of the PLO from 1969. Nasser and Sadat were presidents of Egypt, and Begin was prime minister of Israel.

Q: On 1 October 1949, Mao Zedong proclaimed:
+ the People's Republic of China
- the Republic of China in Taiwan
- the end of the Cultural Revolution
- the Great Leap Forward
E: After the communists won the civil war, Mao proclaimed the People's Republic of China in Beijing on 1 October 1949. Chiang Kai-shek's Nationalists retreated to Taiwan. The Great Leap Forward began in 1958, and the Cultural Revolution in 1966.

Q: What was the main aim of Mao's Great Leap Forward (1958–1962)?
+ rapid industrial and agricultural growth through large people's communes
- to open China to Western capitalist investment
- to restore the emperor
- to join the Western alliance NATO
E: The Great Leap Forward tried to industrialise China very quickly, for example with backyard steel furnaces and huge communes. Poor planning and false reports led to a terrible famine in which millions died.

Q: The economic reforms of Deng Xiaoping after 1978 are known as the "Four Modernisations". Which areas did they cover?
+ agriculture, industry, science and technology, and national defence
- religion, art, music and literature
- banking, tourism, fashion and sport
- only heavy industry
E: Deng's Four Modernisations aimed to modernise agriculture, industry, science and technology, and defence. They allowed some private enterprise and foreign investment, which led to fast economic growth.

Q: How did the Korean War (1950–1953) end?
+ with an armistice that left Korea divided near the 38th parallel
- with the unification of Korea under the North
- with the unification of Korea under the South
- with Japan taking control of Korea
E: The war started when North Korea invaded the South in June 1950. After fighting involving UN (mainly US) forces and China, an armistice was signed in July 1953. Korea remained divided near the 38th parallel, and no peace treaty has been signed.

Q: East Pakistan became the independent state of Bangladesh in:
+ 1971
- 1947
- 1962
- 1984
E: After a war in 1971, in which India supported the Bengali independence movement, East Pakistan became Bangladesh. In 1947 Pakistan (East and West) was created by the partition of India.

Q: After Japan surrendered in 1945, the US occupation of Japan was led by:
+ General Douglas MacArthur
- General Dwight Eisenhower
- President Harry Truman in person
- Chiang Kai-shek
E: General MacArthur led the Allied occupation (1945–1952). Under the occupation Japan adopted a new constitution in 1947 that made it a democracy and renounced war.

@ hist12-u4
Q: According to the textbook, what were two of the most immediate goals of the newly independent African states?
+ promoting economic growth and ensuring national unity
- building empires and conquering neighbours
- returning to colonial rule and closing schools
- abolishing all trade and agriculture
E: The textbook states that two of the most immediate goals were promoting economic growth and ensuring national unity, because the colonial borders had joined many different ethnic groups together.

Q: Why did African exports grow by about 6% per year in the 1960s?
+ Oil was cheap and world prices for raw materials such as cocoa, coffee and copper were high.
- African countries stopped exporting raw materials.
- Oil prices were extremely high.
- Europe banned imports from Africa.
E: The textbook explains that in the 1960s oil cost only about $2 a barrel, while prices for Africa's raw materials were high, so exports grew by about 6% a year.

Q: Which country hosted the first festival of African arts and culture in 1966?
+ Senegal
- Kenya
- Egypt
- South Africa
E: The textbook says Senegal hosted the first festival of African arts and culture in 1966, attended by artists, writers, musicians and griots from all over Africa.

Q: In which year was Kwame Nkrumah of Ghana overthrown in a military coup?
+ 1966
- 1957
- 1963
- 1971
E: Nkrumah led Ghana to independence in 1957, but his government's huge spending led to debt and economic decline. He was overthrown in a military coup in 1966, nine years after coming to power.

Q: What was the "Leadership Code" in Nyerere's Tanzania?
+ rules to prevent government and party officials from using their positions to become rich
- a code for the army to take power
- a law allowing officials to own many businesses
- a list of colonial laws that remained in force
E: Under the Arusha Declaration, civil servants and party officials were banned from capitalist activities, and the Leadership Code aimed to prevent corruption. Historians consider Tanzania one of the least corrupt African countries at that time.

Q: About how many people were moved into Ujamaa villages in Tanzania between 1973 and 1976?
+ about five million
- about five thousand
- about fifty thousand
- about fifty million
E: The textbook states that between 1973 and 1976 some five million people were moved into Ujamaa villages, and there were about 8000 such villages by 1977. Much of this "villagization" was compulsory.

Q: Who overthrew Milton Obote in Uganda in 1971 and ruled as a brutal military dictator until 1979?
+ Idi Amin
- Mobutu Sese Seko
- Jomo Kenyatta
- Julius Nyerere
E: General Idi Amin, chief of the army, seized power in 1971. His regime was known for terror and economic ruin. He was overthrown in 1979 when Tanzanian forces and Ugandan exiles invaded, and he fled to Libya.

Q: Patrice Lumumba was:
+ the first prime minister of the independent Congo, murdered in 1961
- the first president of Ghana
- the leader of the Biafra secession
- the founder of Ujamaa in Tanzania
E: Lumumba became the first prime minister of the Congo at independence in 1960. During the Congo crisis he was arrested after Mobutu seized power and was murdered in captivity in January 1961.

Q: How did the oil price rises of the 1970s harm African economies?
+ Oil imports became very expensive while the prices of African exports fell, so countries borrowed and fell into debt.
- They made all African countries rich.
- They lowered the cost of imported machines.
- They had no effect on Africa.
E: Oil prices rose sharply after 1973 (reaching about $34 a barrel by 1980). At the same time African export prices fell; for example, a ton of Tanzanian tea bought far less oil in 1980 than in 1972. Many countries borrowed, and Africa's external debt rose from $6 billion to $38 billion during the 1970s.

Q: Who led the attempted secession of Biafra from Nigeria (1967–1970)?
+ Odumegwu Ojukwu
- Idi Amin
- Kwame Nkrumah
- Patrice Lumumba
E: Colonel Ojukwu declared the Eastern Region independent as Biafra in 1967. Federal forces blockaded the region, causing famine, and Biafra surrendered in 1970 after Ojukwu fled.

Q: Between 1963 and 1989, how often was armed force used to take power in African countries, according to the textbook?
+ on more than 60 occasions
- only once
- never
- on about 5 occasions
E: The textbook states that armed force was used to take power on more than 60 occasions between 1963 and 1989. By the late 1980s, about half of African countries were under military rule.

Q: When did Mobutu Sese Seko finally lose power in Zaire (Congo)?
+ 1997, when Laurent Kabila's forces entered Kinshasa
- 1960, at independence
- 1965, after his coup
- 1971, after Idi Amin's coup
E: Mobutu seized power in 1965 and ruled as a dictator for over 30 years. In May 1997 Laurent Kabila's rebel army, supported by Rwanda, entered Kinshasa and Mobutu fled into exile.
