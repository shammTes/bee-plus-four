# Authored Grade 12 geography top-ups (facts from the Grade 12 Geography textbook)
@ geo12-u2
Q: According to the textbook, why is surface water in Eritrea unreliable for irrigation and hydroelectric power?
+ Rainfall is low, seasonal and irregular, and evapo-transpiration is high.
- Eritrea has too many large permanent rivers.
- Rainfall is heavy throughout the year.
- Surface water is too cold to use.
E: Rainfall in Eritrea is erratic, seasonal and low, and it varies from year to year and place to place. Evapo-transpiration is also high. Together these make surface water unreliable, so small-scale developments like ponds and sub-surface dams are recommended.

Q: Which geological unit in Eritrea contains considerable groundwater and supplies irrigation wells in the Mendefera and Tselma plains?
+ the volcanic traps of the lava plateau
- the basement (crystalline) rocks
- the coral of the Dahlak Islands
- the sand dunes north of Massawa
E: The volcanic traps are the oldest volcanic rocks in Eritrea, found on the plateau mainly south of Asmara. They hold considerable groundwater, and many irrigation wells (for example in the Mendefera and Tselma plains) draw from them. Basement rocks are impermeable, and the coastal sediments have poor groundwater.

Q: Why does most rain become runoff in areas of basement (crystalline) rocks?
+ The rocks are impermeable and poorly weathered, so water cannot sink in easily.
- The rocks are very porous like sand.
- These areas receive no rain at all.
- The rocks absorb all the water.
E: Basement rocks are hard, impermeable and poorly weathered. Water cannot infiltrate, so it runs off. Any groundwater there is usually not perennial, especially where rainfall is low.

Q: Where is the groundwater recharge rate in Eritrea lowest (less than 50 mm per year)?
+ the Eastern Lowlands
- the Central Highlands
- the Eastern Escarpment (Semenawi Bahri)
- the Western Lowlands near the Setit
E: The Eastern Lowlands (below 500 m) receive less than 200 mm of rain a year, have evapo-transpiration above 1900 mm/year, and a recharge rate below 50 mm/year. The Highlands recharge at about 50–150 mm/year.

Q: What is the main source of groundwater recharge in the Eastern Lowlands?
+ flood runoff that originates in the Highlands
- sea water from the Red Sea
- melting snow
- heavy local rainfall
E: Rainfall in the Eastern Lowlands is very low, so most recharge comes from flood water flowing down from the Highlands. Sea water is salty and is not a source of fresh groundwater.

Q: Which is a major water problem in the Eritrean Highlands?
+ groundwater is easily depleted by heavy pumping for irrigation
- too much rainfall all year round
- no siltation of reservoirs
- the water table is always at the surface
E: The textbook lists two major problems in the Highlands: groundwater depletion due to heavy pumping for irrigation, and siltation of surface water developments (dams and ponds).

Q: Which river in Eritrea has the largest annual discharge?
+ Setit
- Barka
- Gash
- Anseba
E: According to the textbook (Table 2.1), the Setit discharges about 8–10 billion m³ per year, much more than the Barka (400–800 million m³) and the Gash (200–400 million m³).

Q: The textbook recommends building ponds and dams especially in the Central Highlands. What is one of the two main advantages of storing rainwater in dams and reservoirs?
+ It protects the soil from erosion.
- It increases evapo-transpiration.
- It makes the water salty.
- It lowers the water table.
E: Storing rainwater has two main advantages: it conserves water for later use (drinking, irrigation, livestock), and it protects the soil from erosion by reducing runoff.

@ geo12-u5
Q: What are the five factors of soil formation?
+ parent material, climate, topography, living things and time
- wind, sea, sun, moon and stars
- fertilizer, ploughing, irrigation, rain and roads
- sand, clay, silt, gravel and stone
E: The textbook lists five factors that account for soil formation: parent material (the rock), climate, topography (relief and slope), living things (plants, animals, bacteria) and time.

Q: Why are soils in the Eritrean lowlands generally saline in the western plain?
+ Because of aridity, soluble salts accumulate in the soil.
- Because sea water floods the western plain every year.
- Because the soils are very rich in humus.
- Because rainfall there is very high.
E: In dry (arid) areas, evaporation is greater than rainfall, so salts are drawn upward and left in the soil. That is why many soils of the western plain are saline or alkaline.

Q: On average, how much soil does Eritrea lose per hectare each year?
+ 15–20 tons
- 1–2 tons
- 500 tons
- no soil at all
E: The textbook states that Eritrea loses on average 15–20 tons of soil per hectare per year, and in some places as much as 100 tons per hectare per year.

Q: Which soil conservation method takes some land out of production, as noted in the textbook?
+ terracing
- contour ploughing
- inter-cropping
- shelter belts
E: Terracing builds step-like ground across a slope to hold soil and water. Its disadvantage is that the terrace walls take up some land that could otherwise be cultivated.

Q: What percentage of Eritrea's land is under forest cover, according to the textbook?
+ less than 1%
- about 10%
- about 30%
- more than 50%
E: The textbook says forests have been destroyed for fuel and other uses, and today less than 1% of the country is under forest cover. This has increased soil erosion and siltation of dams.

Q: About what percentage of Eritrea's land surface is suitable for grazing animals?
+ about 80%
- about 8%
- about 20%
- about 50%
E: The textbook estimates that about 80% of Eritrea's land surface is ideal for grazing. That is why overgrazing is a serious cause of soil degradation.

Q: Which ethnic groups belong to the Nilotic family?
+ Nara and Kunama
- Tigre and Tigrigna
- Saho and Afar
- Bilen and Hidarb
E: According to Table 7.1, the Nilotic family includes the Nara and Kunama. Saho, Afar, Bilen and Hidarb are Hamitic (Kushitic), and Tigre, Tigrigna and Rashaida are Semitic.

Q: Which ethnic group does NOT belong to the Hamitic (Kushitic) family?
+ Tigre
- Bilen
- Saho
- Afar
E: The Hamitic (Kushitic) family includes Saho, Afar, Bilen and Hidarb. The Tigre are a Semitic group.

Q: According to the textbook, who were the earliest inhabitants of Eritrea?
+ the Pygmoid people
- the Semites
- the Hamites
- the Rashaida
E: The Pygmoid people are said to be the earliest inhabitants. They later intermarried with Nilo-Saharan migrants from south-east Sudan; the Nara and Kunama are descendants of these groups.

Q: Between about 1000 B.C.E. and 700 B.C.E., which family migrated to Eritrea from the Arabian Peninsula?
+ the Semitic family
- the Nilotic family
- the Pygmoid people
- the Hamitic family
E: The Semites were the last of the early migrants. They came across the Red Sea from the Arabian Peninsula between 1000 and 700 B.C.E., first settled on the coast, and then moved to the cooler highlands. The Hamites arrived around 2000 B.C.E.

Q: Which of these is listed in the textbook as a contribution of the Semitic families to Eritrean society?
+ terracing, irrigation and the Sabean alphabet
- the printing press and railways
- tea plantations
- the use of electricity
E: The textbook lists the domestication of animals (camel, goats and sheep), terracing, irrigation, housing construction, handcraft, the Sabean alphabet, trade and mining.

Q: The textbook says 41.5% of Eritrea's population is under 15 years old and 6.3% is over 65. What does this tell us?
+ Almost half the population is in the dependent age groups.
- Most people are over 65.
- There are very few children in Eritrea.
- The population is decreasing quickly.
E: 41.5% + 6.3% = 47.8% of the population are dependants (too young or too old to be economically active). This is a very youthful population, typical of a country with high fertility.

Q: In a country, 41.5% of the population is under 15 and 6.3% is over 65. What is the dependency ratio (dependants per 100 people aged 15–64)?
+ about 92
- about 48
- about 52
- about 109
E: Dependants = 41.5 + 6.3 = 47.8%. Working-age population = 100 − 47.8 = 52.2%.
Dependency ratio = $$\frac{47.8}{52.2} \times 100 \approx 91.6$$, so about 92 (the textbook gives 91.5).
48 is the percentage of dependants, not the ratio, and 109 inverts the fraction.

Q: Why is it difficult to give precise population figures for Eritrea, according to the textbook?
+ No official population census has been carried out.
- The population never changes.
- Nobody lives in the lowlands.
- Population is counted only by satellites.
E: The textbook says no official census has been undertaken in Eritrea, so figures (such as 5.1 million in 2009) are estimates from government offices and the Population Reference Bureau.

Q: The textbook gives a sex ratio of 102 for Eritrea. What does this mean?

- There are 102 males for every 100 females.
- 102 babies are born every day.
- 102 people live in each square kilometre.
E: The textbook defines the sex ratio here as the number of females for every 100 males: about 102 females per 100 males.

Q: According to the textbook, which is the smallest ethnic group in Eritrea in terms of population size?
+ Rashaida
- Tigrigna
- Tigre

E: Table 7.4 shows the Rashaida at less than 1% of the population, living in the Northern Red Sea region.

Q: Why are the Buri Peninsula and southern Gash-Barka (Shambiko, Shilalo, Om Hager) attractive for settlement?
+ They have good grazing land for livestock.
- They have the largest factories in Eritrea.
- They are the coldest parts of the country.
- They have no water at all.
E: The textbook names these areas as important grazing lands. People settle there with their livestock because grass and water are available; the Buri Peninsula stays green throughout the year.

Q: Fishing villages such as Hirgigo, Afta, Zula and Thio developed mainly because of:
+ fishing grounds along the Red Sea coast
- gold mines
- cool highland climate
- large rivers with hydroelectric dams
E: The textbook explains that the coast south of Massawa down to Assab has fishing grounds with many fish and lobsters, and many villages have grown around them.

Q: Which zone has the highest population density in Eritrea (about 125 people per km²)?
+ Maekel (Central)
- Debub (Southern)
- Northern Red Sea
- Southern Red Sea
E: The textbook gives about 125 per km² for Maekel, about 44 for Debub, and only about 9–10 for the lowland Red Sea zones.
