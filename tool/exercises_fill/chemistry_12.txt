# Authored Grade 12 chemistry top-ups
@ chem12-u1
Q: What is the H–C–H bond angle in methane (CH₄)?
+ 109.5°
- 90°
- 120°
- 180°
E: The carbon in CH₄ has four bonding pairs and no lone pairs. VSEPR theory says four electron pairs spread out as far as possible, which gives a tetrahedral shape with angles of 109.5°. 120° is for trigonal planar (3 pairs) and 180° is for linear (2 pairs).

Q: Why is the H–O–H bond angle in water (about 104.5°) smaller than the angle in methane (109.5°)?
+ The two lone pairs on oxygen repel the bonding pairs more strongly and push them closer together.
- Water has fewer electrons than methane.
- Oxygen has no lone pairs.
- Hydrogen atoms attract each other.
E: Oxygen in H₂O has 2 bonding pairs and 2 lone pairs (4 pairs, tetrahedral arrangement). Lone pair–bonding pair repulsion is stronger than bonding pair–bonding pair repulsion, so the H–O–H angle is squeezed to about 104.5°. Oxygen does have lone pairs, and the H atoms do not attract each other.

Q: What is the shape of the SF₆ molecule?
+ octahedral
- tetrahedral
- trigonal bipyramidal
- square planar
E: Sulfur in SF₆ has 6 bonding pairs and no lone pairs. Six pairs point to the corners of an octahedron, with all bond angles 90°. Trigonal bipyramidal is for 5 pairs (like PCl₅).

Q: What is the shape of the PCl₅ molecule?
+ trigonal bipyramidal
- octahedral
- square pyramidal
- tetrahedral
E: Phosphorus in PCl₅ has 5 bonding pairs and no lone pairs. Five pairs give a trigonal bipyramid: three Cl atoms in a plane at 120°, and two above and below at 90° to that plane.

Q: What is the hybridization of each carbon atom in ethene (C₂H₄, H₂C=CH₂)?
+ sp²
- sp
- sp³
- sp³d
E: Each carbon in ethene forms three σ bonds (two to H and one to C) and one π bond. Three σ bonds need three hybrid orbitals: sp². The leftover p orbital forms the π bond. The shape around each carbon is trigonal planar (about 120°).

Q: In the sodium chloride (rock salt) crystal, how many Cl⁻ ions surround each Na⁺ ion?
+ 6
- 4
- 8
- 12
E: In NaCl each ion is surrounded by 6 oppositely charged ions at the corners of an octahedron, so the coordination number is 6:6. In CsCl it is 8:8, and in zinc blende (ZnS) it is 4:4.

Q: Why do ionic solids such as NaCl conduct electricity when molten but not when solid?
+ In the molten state the ions are free to move; in the solid state they are held in fixed positions.
- Solid NaCl has no ions.
- Molten NaCl contains free electrons like a metal.
- Heating NaCl changes it into a metal.
E: Electric current needs moving charged particles. In solid NaCl the Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ions are locked in the lattice. When melted (or dissolved in water), the ions can move and carry the current. NaCl does not contain free electrons.

Q: What is the shape of the H₂S molecule?
+ bent (V-shaped)
- linear
- trigonal planar
- tetrahedral
E: Sulfur is in the same group as oxygen. In H₂S, sulfur has 2 bonding pairs and 2 lone pairs, so (like H₂O) the molecule is bent. The angle is about 92°, smaller than in water.

Q: Which type of solid has particles held together by a "sea" of delocalised electrons?
+ metallic solid
- ionic solid
- molecular solid
- covalent network solid
E: In a metallic solid, positive metal ions sit in a lattice surrounded by delocalised (free) electrons. These electrons explain why metals conduct electricity and heat and can be hammered into shapes.
