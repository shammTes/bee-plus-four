Q: Find the vertex of the parabola $$y = (x - 3)^2 + 2$$.
+ (3, 2)
- (−3, 2)
- (3, −2)
- (2, 3)
E: The vertex form is $$y = a(x - h)^2 + k$$ with vertex (h, k).
Here h = 3 and k = 2, so the vertex is (3, 2). Be careful: (x − 3) means h = +3, not −3.

Q: Find the axis of symmetry of $$y = x^2 - 6x + 5$$.
+ x = 3
- x = −3
- x = 6
- x = 5
E: For $$y = ax^2 + bx + c$$, the axis of symmetry is $$x = -\frac{b}{2a}$$.
$$x = -\frac{-6}{2(1)} = 3$$

Q: Find the minimum value of $$y = x^2 - 6x + 5$$.
+ −4
- 5
- 3
- −9
E: The vertex is at x = 3 (from $$x = -\frac{b}{2a}$$).
$$y = 3^2 - 6(3) + 5 = 9 - 18 + 5 = -4$$
Since a = 1 > 0, the parabola opens upward, so −4 is the minimum value.

Q: Solve $$x^2 - 5x + 6 = 0$$.
+ x = 2 or x = 3
- x = −2 or x = −3
- x = 1 or x = 6
- x = −1 or x = 6
E: Find two numbers that multiply to 6 and add to −5: −2 and −3.
$$x^2 - 5x + 6 = (x - 2)(x - 3) = 0$$, so x = 2 or x = 3.
Check: $$2^2 - 5(2) + 6 = 0$$ ✓.

Q: Use the quadratic formula to solve $$x^2 + 2x - 4 = 0$$.
+ $$x = -1 \pm \sqrt{5}$$
- $$x = 1 \pm \sqrt{5}$$
- $$x = -2 \pm \sqrt{5}$$
- $$x = -1 \pm \sqrt{3}$$
E: $$x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a} = \frac{-2 \pm \sqrt{4 + 16}}{2} = \frac{-2 \pm \sqrt{20}}{2} = \frac{-2 \pm 2\sqrt{5}}{2} = -1 \pm \sqrt{5}$$

Q: Find the discriminant of $$2x^2 - 3x + 5 = 0$$ and state the nature of the roots.
+ −31; no real roots
- 49; two real roots
- −31; two equal roots
- 31; two real roots
E: Discriminant $$D = b^2 - 4ac = (-3)^2 - 4(2)(5) = 9 - 40 = -31$$.
Since D < 0, the equation has no real roots (the parabola does not cross the x-axis). 49 comes from adding 40 instead of subtracting.

Q: For what value of k does $$x^2 + kx + 9 = 0$$ have two equal roots (k > 0)?
+ 6
- 3
- 9
- 18
E: Equal roots happen when the discriminant is zero: $$k^2 - 4(1)(9) = 0$$, so $$k^2 = 36$$ and k = ±6. With k > 0, k = 6.
Check: $$x^2 + 6x + 9 = (x + 3)^2$$, which has the double root x = −3.

Q: What are the x-intercepts of $$y = x^2 - 4$$?
+ (−2, 0) and (2, 0)
- (0, −4) only
- (4, 0) and (−4, 0)
- (0, 2) and (0, −2)
E: Set y = 0: $$x^2 - 4 = 0$$, so $$x^2 = 4$$ and x = ±2. The x-intercepts are (−2, 0) and (2, 0). (0, −4) is the y-intercept.

Q: The sum of the roots of $$x^2 - 7x + 10 = 0$$ is:
+ 7
- −7
- 10
- 3
E: For $$ax^2 + bx + c = 0$$, the sum of the roots is $$-\frac{b}{a} = -\frac{-7}{1} = 7$$ and the product is $$\frac{c}{a} = 10$$.
Check: the roots are 2 and 5, and 2 + 5 = 7 ✓.

Q: The product of two consecutive positive integers is 56. Find the smaller integer.
+ 7
- 8
- 6
- 14
E: Let the integers be n and n + 1: $$n(n + 1) = 56$$, so $$n^2 + n - 56 = 0$$.
Factor: $$(n + 8)(n - 7) = 0$$, so n = 7 (n = −8 is not positive).
Check: 7 × 8 = 56 ✓.

Q: A rectangle has a length 3 m more than its width and an area of 40 m². Find its width.
+ 5 m
- 8 m
- 4 m
- 10 m
E: Let the width be w; then the length is w + 3. Area: $$w(w + 3) = 40$$, so $$w^2 + 3w - 40 = 0$$.
Factor: $$(w + 8)(w - 5) = 0$$, so w = 5 (a width cannot be negative).
Check: 5 × 8 = 40 ✓. 8 m is the length.

Q: Which function has a graph that opens downward and has its vertex at (0, 4)?
+ $$y = -x^2 + 4$$
- $$y = x^2 + 4$$
- $$y = -(x - 4)^2$$
- $$y = x^2 - 4$$
E: A negative leading coefficient makes the parabola open downward. $$y = -x^2 + 4$$ has vertex (0, 4). $$y = -(x - 4)^2$$ opens downward but has vertex (4, 0).

Q: Simplify $$\frac{2^5 \times 2^{-2}}{2^3}$$.
+ 1
- 2
- 0
- 1/2
E: Multiply: $$2^5 \times 2^{-2} = 2^{5 + (-2)} = 2^3$$. Divide: $$\frac{2^3}{2^3} = 2^0 = 1$$.
The answer is not 0: any non-zero number to the power 0 equals 1.

+ 9

- 81
