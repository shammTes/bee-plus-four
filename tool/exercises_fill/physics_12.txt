@ phys12-u1
Q: Find the pressure due to water at a depth of 5.0 m. (Density of water = 1000 kg/m³, g = 10 m/s². Ignore atmospheric pressure.)
+ 50 kPa
- 5 kPa
- 0.5 kPa
- 500 kPa
E: Liquid pressure: $$P = \rho g h$$.
$$P = 1000 \times 10 \times 5.0 = 50\,000 \text{ Pa} = 50 \text{ kPa}$$
The other options are wrong by factors of 10; check the powers of ten carefully.

Q: In a hydraulic press, a force of 200 N is applied to a small piston of area 0.01 m². The large piston has an area of 0.5 m². What force is produced on the large piston?
+ 10 000 N
- 4 N
- 1000 N
- 100 N
E: By Pascal's principle the pressure is the same on both pistons: $$\frac{F_1}{A_1} = \frac{F_2}{A_2}$$.
$$F_2 = F_1 \times \frac{A_2}{A_1} = 200 \times \frac{0.5}{0.01} = 200 \times 50 = 10\,000 \text{ N}$$
4 N comes from dividing instead of multiplying by the area ratio.

Q: A block of volume 0.002 m³ is completely under water. Find the buoyant force on it. (ρ water = 1000 kg/m³, g = 10 m/s²)
+ 20 N
- 2 N
- 200 N
- 0.2 N
E: Archimedes' principle: buoyant force = weight of fluid displaced.
$$F_B = \rho_{\text{fluid}} V g = 1000 \times 0.002 \times 10 = 20 \text{ N}$$

Q: A stone weighs 30 N in air and 20 N when fully immersed in water. Find the density of the stone. (ρ water = 1000 kg/m³, g = 10 m/s²)
+ 3000 kg/m³
- 1500 kg/m³
- 2000 kg/m³
- 667 kg/m³
E: Loss of weight = buoyant force = 30 − 20 = 10 N.
Volume: $$V = \frac{F_B}{\rho_w g} = \frac{10}{1000 \times 10} = 0.001 \text{ m}^3$$.
Mass = 30 / 10 = 3 kg, so density = 3 / 0.001 = 3000 kg/m³.
(Shortcut: relative density = weight in air ÷ loss of weight = 30 ÷ 10 = 3.) 1500 uses 30 ÷ 20, which is wrong.

Q: Water flows at 2 m/s through a pipe of cross-sectional area 4 cm². The pipe narrows to 1 cm². What is the speed in the narrow part?
+ 8 m/s
- 0.5 m/s
- 2 m/s
- 4 m/s
E: Continuity equation: $$A_1 v_1 = A_2 v_2$$.
$$v_2 = \frac{A_1 v_1}{A_2} = \frac{4 \times 2}{1} = 8 \text{ m/s}$$
The fluid speeds up where the pipe is narrower. 0.5 m/s wrongly reverses the ratio.

Q: Ice has a density of 900 kg/m³ and water 1000 kg/m³. What fraction of a floating ice block is under the water?
+ 90%
- 10%
- 50%
- 100%
E: For a floating object, fraction submerged = $$\frac{\rho_{\text{object}}}{\rho_{\text{fluid}}} = \frac{900}{1000} = 0.9$$, so 90% is under the water and only 10% is above it.

Q: Water flows through a horizontal pipe. Where the pipe becomes narrower, the water speed increases. According to Bernoulli's principle, the pressure there:
+ decreases
- increases
- stays the same
- becomes zero
E: Bernoulli's principle (for a horizontal pipe): $$P + \frac{1}{2}\rho v^2$$ is constant. If the speed v increases, the pressure P must decrease. This explains, for example, the lift on an aeroplane wing.

Q: Water flows at 3 m/s through a pipe of cross-sectional area 0.02 m². What is the volume flow rate?
+ 0.06 m³/s
- 0.15 m³/s
- 1.5 m³/s
- 6 m³/s
E: Volume flow rate $$Q = A v = 0.02 \times 3 = 0.06 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$$. Every second, 0.06 m³ (60 litres) of water passes any point.

Q: A force of 600 N acts evenly on an area of 0.3 m². What is the pressure?
+ 2000 Pa
- 180 Pa
- 200 Pa
- 0.0005 Pa
E: $$P = \frac{F}{A} = \frac{600}{0.3} = 2000 \text{ Pa}$$. 180 multiplies instead of dividing.

Q: A piece of wood of density 600 kg/m³ and volume 0.05 m³ floats in water (1000 kg/m³). What is the buoyant force on it? (g = 10 m/s²)
+ 300 N
- 500 N
- 200 N
- 30 N
E: A floating object is in equilibrium, so the buoyant force equals its weight.
Weight = $$\rho V g = 600 \times 0.05 \times 10 = 300$$ N.
500 N would be the buoyant force if the wood were completely under water, which is not the case when it floats.

Q: Find the energy of a photon of frequency 5.0 × 10¹⁴ Hz. (h = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s)
+ 3.3 × 10⁻¹⁹ J
- 3.3 × 10⁻²⁰ J
- 1.3 × 10⁻⁴⁸ J
- 7.5 × 10⁴⁷ J
E: $$E = hf = 6.63 \times 10^{-34} \times 5.0 \times 10^{14} = 3.3 \times 10^{-19} \text{ J}$$
$$1.3 \times 10^{-48}$$ comes from dividing h by f, and $$7.5 \times 10^{47}$$ from dividing f by h.

Q: Light with photon energy 3.5 eV falls on a metal whose work function is 2.0 eV. What is the maximum kinetic energy of the emitted electrons?
+ 1.5 eV
- 5.5 eV
- 2.0 eV
- 3.5 eV
E: Einstein's photoelectric equation: $$K_{\max} = hf - \phi = 3.5 - 2.0 = 1.5 \text{ eV}$$.
Adding the values (5.5 eV) is wrong: part of the photon's energy is used to free the electron.

Q: A metal has a work function of 4.14 eV. Find its threshold frequency. (h = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s, 1 eV = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ J)
+ 1.0 × 10¹⁵ Hz
- 1.0 × 10¹⁴ Hz
- 6.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ Hz
- 2.5 × 10¹⁵ Hz
E: Convert: $$\phi = 4.14 \times 1.6 \times 10^{-19} = 6.6 \times 10^{-19} \text{ J}$$.
$$f_0 = \frac{\phi}{h} = \frac{6.6 \times 10^{-19}}{6.63 \times 10^{-34}} \approx 1.0 \times 10^{15} \text{ Hz}$$
$$6.6 \times 10^{-19}$$ is the work function in joules, not a frequency.

Q: Find the de Broglie wavelength of an electron moving at 1.0 × 10⁶ m/s. (h = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s, mₑ = 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ kg)
+ 7.3 × 10⁻¹⁰ m
- 7.3 × 10⁻⁴ m
- 1.4 × 10⁹ m
- 6.0 × 10⁻²⁸ m
E: $$\lambda = \frac{h}{mv} = \frac{6.63 \times 10^{-34}}{9.11 \times 10^{-31} \times 1.0 \times 10^{6}} = \frac{6.63 \times 10^{-34}}{9.11 \times 10^{-25}} \approx 7.3 \times 10^{-10} \text{ m}$$
This is about the size of an atom, which is why electrons can be diffracted by crystals.

Q: Find the wavelength of a photon with energy 2.0 eV. (Use hc = 1240 eV·nm.)
+ 620 nm
- 2480 nm
- 310 nm
- 1240 nm
E: $$\lambda = \frac{hc}{E} = \frac{1240 \text{ eV·nm}}{2.0 \text{ eV}} = 620 \text{ nm}$$ (orange-red light).
2480 nm multiplies by 2 instead of dividing.

Q: In a hydrogen atom, $$E_n = -\frac{13.6}{n^2}$$ eV. What energy is emitted when the electron falls from n = 3 to n = 2?
+ 1.89 eV
- 13.6 eV
- 3.40 eV
- 1.51 eV
E: $$E_3 = -\frac{13.6}{9} = -1.51$$ eV and $$E_2 = -\frac{13.6}{4} = -3.40$$ eV.
Energy emitted = $$E_3 - E_2 = -1.51 - (-3.40) = 1.89$$ eV (red light, about 656 nm).
3.40 eV and 1.51 eV are the sizes of single levels, not the difference between them.

Q: A photon has a wavelength of 400 nm. Find its frequency. (c = 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s)
+ 7.5 × 10¹⁴ Hz
- 1.3 × 10⁻¹⁵ Hz
- 1.2 × 10¹¹ Hz
- 7.5 × 10⁵ Hz
E: $$f = \frac{c}{\lambda} = \frac{3.0 \times 10^8}{400 \times 10^{-9}} = \frac{3.0 \times 10^8}{4.0 \times 10^{-7}} = 7.5 \times 10^{14} \text{ Hz}$$
Remember to convert nm to m (1 nm = 10⁻⁹ m).
