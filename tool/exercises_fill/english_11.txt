Q: Which word has the same vowel sound as "seat" /iː/?
+ meet
- sit
- set
- sat
E: "Seat" and "meet" both have the long vowel /iː/. "Sit" has the short /ɪ/, "set" has /e/, and "sat" has /æ/.

Q: Which word has the same vowel sound as "ship" /ɪ/?
+ fish
- sheep
- shape
- shop
E: "Ship" and "fish" both have the short /ɪ/. "Sheep" has the long /iː/, "shape" has the diphthong /eɪ/, and "shop" has /ɒ/.

Q: Which word has the same vowel sound as "cup" /ʌ/?
+ love
- cop
- cap
- cope
E: "Love" is pronounced /lʌv/, with the same vowel as "cup". The letter "o" can be pronounced /ʌ/ in words like love, come, mother and son. "Cop" has /ɒ/, "cap" has /æ/, and "cope" has /əʊ/.

Q: Which word has the same vowel sound as "food" /uː/?
+ blue
- foot
- good
- book
E: "Blue" has the long /uː/, like "food". "Foot", "good" and "book" have the short /ʊ/, even though some of them are spelled with "oo".

Q: Which word has the same vowel sound as "book" /ʊ/?
+ could
- food
- boot
- moon
E: "Could" is pronounced /kʊd/ (the "l" is silent), with the same short vowel as "book". "Food", "boot" and "moon" have the long /uː/.

Q: Which word has the same vowel sound as "bird" /ɜː/?
+ word
- beard
- bed
- bored
E: "Word" is pronounced /wɜːd/, with the same vowel as "bird", "girl" and "learn". "Beard" has /ɪə/, "bed" has /e/, and "bored" has /ɔː/.

Q: Which word is the odd one out (different vowel sound)? meat, seat, great, heat
+ great
- meat
- seat
- heat
E: "Meat", "seat" and "heat" have /iː/. "Great" has the diphthong /eɪ/, the same sound as in "late". The spelling "ea" does not always give the same sound.

Q: Which word is the odd one out (different vowel sound)? food, mood, blood, rude
+ blood
- food
- mood
- rude
E: "Food", "mood" and "rude" have /uː/. "Blood" is pronounced /blʌd/, with the same vowel as "cup".

Q: The words "ship" and "sheep" are a minimal pair. What is the difference between them?
+ the vowel sound: short /ɪ/ in "ship" and long /iː/ in "sheep"
- the first consonant
- the last consonant
- the number of syllables
E: A minimal pair is two words that differ in only one sound. "Ship" /ʃɪp/ and "sheep" /ʃiːp/ have the same consonants; only the vowel is different.

Q: The weak vowel sound heard in the first syllable of "about" and the last syllable of "teacher" is called:
+ schwa /ə/
- long /iː/
- diphthong /aɪ/
- short /æ/
E: The schwa /ə/ is the most common vowel sound in English. It appears in unstressed syllables, as in about /əˈbaʊt/ and teacher /ˈtiːtʃə/.

Q: Which word has the diphthong /aɪ/, as in "my"?
+ buy
- bay
- boy
- bee
E: "Buy" is pronounced /baɪ/, like "my" and "eye". "Bay" has /eɪ/, "boy" has /ɔɪ/, and "bee" has /iː/.

Q: Which word has the same vowel sound as "car" /ɑː/?
+ heart
- hat
- hurt
- hot
E: "Heart" is pronounced /hɑːt/ in British English, with the same vowel as "car" and "father". "Hat" has /æ/, "hurt" has /ɜː/, and "hot" has /ɒ/.

Q: Which word has the same vowel sound as "door" /ɔː/?
+ caught
- cot
- cut
- coat
E: "Caught" is pronounced /kɔːt/, with the same vowel as "door" and "saw". "Cot" has /ɒ/, "cut" has /ʌ/, and "coat" has /əʊ/.

Q: Direct: "I can swim," said Ruth. Reported: Ruth said that she ____ swim.
+ could
- can
- may
- will
E: In reported speech after a past reporting verb, "can" changes to "could". The pronoun "I" also changes to "she".

Q: Direct: "We are leaving today," they said. Reported: They said that they were leaving ____.
+ that day
- today yesterday
- tomorrow
- now today
E: When speech is reported later, "today" changes to "that day". "Tomorrow" would change the meaning.

Q: Direct: He asked me, "Have you seen my keys?" Reported: He asked me ____.
+ if I had seen his keys
- have I seen his keys
- if had I seen his keys
- if I have seen my keys?
E: A yes/no question is reported with "if/whether" and statement word order. The present perfect "have you seen" moves back to the past perfect "I had seen", and "my keys" becomes "his keys". There is no question mark.

Q: Find the mistake: "She don't like coffee."
+ "don't" should be "doesn't"
- "She" should be "Her"
- "like" should be "likes"
- "coffee" should be "coffees"
E: With he/she/it in the present simple, the negative auxiliary is "doesn't": She doesn't like coffee. After "doesn't", the main verb stays in the base form (like), so "likes" would be wrong.

Q: Find the mistake: "I have seen him yesterday."
+ "have seen" should be "saw"
- "him" should be "he"
- "yesterday" should be "since yesterday"
- "I" should be "Me"
E: We do not use the present perfect with a finished past time such as "yesterday". Use the past simple: I saw him yesterday.

Q: Find the mistake: "He is more taller than his brother."
+ "more taller" should be "taller"
- "is" should be "are"
- "than" should be "then"
- "his" should be "him"
E: "Taller" is already a comparative, so "more" is not needed. Use "more" only with long adjectives (more beautiful), never together with -er.
