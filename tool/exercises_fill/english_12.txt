@ eng12-u1
Q: Which of these is a finite clause?
+ because she works hard
- to work hard
- working hard
- having worked hard
E: A finite clause has a verb that shows tense and agrees with a subject: "she works". The other options use non-finite forms (to-infinitive, -ing participle, perfect participle), which do not show tense.

Q: Identify the non-finite clause: "To succeed in life, you must face challenges."
+ To succeed in life
- you must face challenges
- must face
- challenges
E: "To succeed in life" uses a to-infinitive and has no subject or tense, so it is a non-finite clause. "You must face challenges" is the main (finite) clause.

Q: Identify the type of non-finite clause: "Having finished the exam, the students left the hall."
+ a perfect participle clause
- a to-infinitive clause
- a finite relative clause
- a bare infinitive clause
E: "Having + past participle" (having finished) is a perfect participle clause. It shows that one action was completed before the next (they finished, then they left).

Q: Choose the correct option: "____ by the noise, the baby started to cry."
+ Frightened
- Frightening
- To frighten
- Frighten
E: The baby was frightened (passive meaning), so we use a past participle clause: "Frightened by the noise". "Frightening" would mean the baby frightened something.

Q: Choose the correct option: "____ into a poor family, he understood the value of hard work."
+ Born
- Bearing
- To bear
- Bore
E: "Born into a poor family" is a past participle clause meaning "Because he was born into a poor family". "Born" is the past participle used for birth.

Q: Which sentence contains a dangling (wrong) participle?
+ Walking to school, the rain started to fall heavily.
- Walking to school, I got wet in the rain.
- While I was walking to school, the rain started.
- I walked to school in the rain.
E: In a participle clause, the subject of the main clause must be the one doing the participle action. In the first sentence it sounds as if "the rain" was walking to school. The other sentences are correct.

Q: Choose the finite clause that has the same meaning as "Not knowing the way, we asked a policeman."
+ Because we did not know the way, we asked a policeman.
- Although we knew the way, we asked a policeman.
- If we know the way, we will ask a policeman.
- We asked a policeman to know the way.
E: The participle clause "Not knowing the way" gives a reason. The finite clause "Because we did not know the way" has the same meaning.

Q: In the long sentence "The young farmer, who had lost most of his crops during the drought that hit the region last year, decided to dig a well so that his family would never go hungry again", what is the main clause?
+ The young farmer decided to dig a well
- who had lost most of his crops
- that hit the region last year
- so that his family would never go hungry again
E: To understand a long sentence, find the main subject and main verb first: "The young farmer ... decided". The other parts are a relative clause (who had lost...), another relative clause (that hit...), and a purpose clause (so that...).

Q: In the same sentence, why did the farmer decide to dig a well?
"The young farmer, who had lost most of his crops during the drought that hit the region last year, decided to dig a well so that his family would never go hungry again."
+ so that his family would never go hungry again
- because his well was too deep
- because the region had too much rain
- in order to sell water
E: "So that" introduces purpose. The farmer's purpose was to make sure his family would never go hungry again.

Q: Choose the correct option: "It is difficult ____ a job without experience."
+ to find
- finding to
- find
- found
E: The pattern "It is + adjective + to-infinitive" is common: It is difficult to find a job.

Q: Choose the correct option: "She was the first woman ____ the mountain."
+ to climb
- climbing to
- climbed
- climbs
E: After "the first / the last / the only + noun", we often use a to-infinitive: the first woman to climb the mountain.

Q: Choose the correct option: "I heard someone ____ for help."
+ shouting
- to shouting
- shouted at
- shouts
E: After verbs of perception (hear, see, watch, feel) + object, we use the -ing form (or bare infinitive): heard someone shouting.

Q: A "challenge" is:
+ something difficult that tests your ability
- something very easy
- a kind of reward
- a holiday
E: A challenge is a difficult task or problem that needs effort and skill to overcome.

Q: Which word means "to deal with a difficult problem successfully"?
+ overcome
- avoid
- ignore
- cause
E: To overcome a challenge is to succeed in dealing with it. To avoid or ignore it is to not face it, and to cause it is to make it happen.

Q: A person who keeps trying even when things are difficult is:
+ determined
- lazy
- careless
- hopeless
E: Determined people do not give up easily. They continue trying until they succeed.

Q: Read the text and answer.
"Born with only one arm, Kidane never let his disability stop him. Having trained for years, he became a national cycling champion, inspiring many young people with disabilities to follow their dreams."
Find the perfect participle clause in the text.
+ Having trained for years
- Born with only one arm
- inspiring many young people
- to follow their dreams
E: "Having + past participle" is the perfect participle: "Having trained for years". "Born with only one arm" is a past participle clause, "inspiring..." is a present participle clause, and "to follow..." is an infinitive.

Q: Read the text and answer.
"Born with only one arm, Kidane never let his disability stop him. Having trained for years, he became a national cycling champion, inspiring many young people with disabilities to follow their dreams."
What effect did Kidane's success have on other people?
+ It inspired young people with disabilities.
- It made others give up.
- It stopped other cyclists.
- It caused a disability.
E: The text says his success was "inspiring many young people with disabilities to follow their dreams".

Q: Which sentence contains a non-finite clause used as the subject?
+ Learning a new language takes time.
- I am learning a new language.
- She learned a new language last year.
- They will learn a new language.
E: "Learning a new language" is an -ing (gerund) clause and is the subject of "takes". In the other sentences, "learn" is a finite verb.

Q: Choose the correct option: "The road ____ to the village is being repaired."
+ leading
- leads
- led to
- to leading
E: "Leading to the village" is a present participle clause that shortens the relative clause "which leads to the village".

Q: Choose the correct option: "The letter ____ yesterday contained good news."
+ received
- receiving
- receives
- to receiving
E: The letter was received (passive), so we use the past participle: "the letter received yesterday" = "the letter which was received yesterday".

Q: Which word introduces a subordinate clause of contrast?
+ although
- and
- but also
- or
E: "Although" is a subordinating conjunction that introduces contrast. "And", "or" and "but also" are coordinating words; they do not make a subordinate clause.

Q: Identify the subordinate clause: "When the novel was published, it became very popular."
+ When the novel was published
- it became very popular
- very popular
- it became
E: A subordinate clause cannot stand alone as a complete sentence. "When the novel was published" depends on the main clause "it became very popular".

Q: Choose the correct subordinator: "____ the poem is short, it has a deep meaning."
+ Although
- Because
- So that
- Unless
E: There is a contrast between "short" and "deep meaning", so we use "Although". "Because" gives a reason, "so that" gives purpose, and "unless" gives a negative condition.

Q: Choose the correct subordinator: "She kept reading ____ she finished the whole book."
+ until
- although
- unless
- whereas
E: "Until" means "up to the time when". She continued reading up to the moment she finished.

Q: Choose the correct subordinator: "The play was cancelled ____ the main actor was ill."
+ because
- although
- whereas
- so that
E: "Because" introduces the reason. The actor's illness was the reason the play was cancelled.

Q: Which sentence uses subordination (not coordination)?
+ Since he loved poetry, he joined the literature club.
- He loved poetry, and he joined the literature club.
- He loved poetry, so he joined the literature club.
- He loved poetry; he joined the literature club.
E: "Since he loved poetry" is a subordinate clause introduced by the subordinator "since". The other sentences join two independent clauses with "and", "so" or a semicolon (coordination).

Q: Which word means "a long written story about imaginary people and events"?
+ novel
- poem
- essay
- biography
E: A novel is a long fictional story. A poem uses lines and often rhythm, an essay discusses ideas, and a biography is the true story of a real person's life.

Q: A "biography" is:
+ the story of a person's life written by someone else
- the story of your own life
- a book of poems
- a play for the theatre
E: A biography is written by another person. An autobiography is the story of a person's life written by that person.

Q: The main character in a story is called the:
+ protagonist
- antagonist
- narrator only
- audience
E: The protagonist is the central character. The antagonist opposes the protagonist, the narrator tells the story, and the audience reads or watches it.

Q: The time and place in which a story happens is the:
+ setting
- theme
- climax
- plot
E: The setting is where and when the story happens. The theme is the main idea or message, the climax is the most exciting point, and the plot is the series of events.

Q: The central message or idea of a literary work is its:
+ theme
- setting
- title only
- rhyme
E: The theme is the main idea the writer wants to communicate, for example love, courage or injustice.

Q: Collocation: Which verb goes best with "a decision"?
+ make
- do
- take part
- give up
E: In English we say "make a decision". ("Take a decision" is also used in British English, but "do a decision" is wrong.) Collocations are words that naturally go together.

Q: Collocation: Which verb goes best with "homework"?
+ do
- make
- take
- say
E: We say "do homework", "do the housework", "do an exercise". We "make" things that are created, such as "make a mistake" or "make a plan".

Q: Collocation: Which adjective goes best with "rain"?
+ heavy
- strong
- big
- powerful
E: The natural collocation is "heavy rain". We say "strong wind", but "strong rain" is not natural.

Q: Collocation: Choose the correct phrase.
+ pay attention
- give attention to do
- make attention
- do attention
E: The correct collocation is "pay attention (to)". The other verbs do not combine with "attention" in this way.

Q: Collocation: Which phrase is correct?
+ commit a crime
- make a crime
- do a crime
- take a crime
E: We say "commit a crime". "Commit" is also used with "commit suicide" and "commit an offence".

Q: Collocation: Which phrase is correct?
+ strong coffee
- powerful coffee
- heavy coffee
- mighty coffee
E: In English we say "strong coffee" or "strong tea" (with a lot of flavour). "Powerful", "heavy" and "mighty" do not collocate with coffee, even though their meanings are similar.

Q: Read the poem lines and answer.
"Hold fast to dreams / For if dreams die / Life is a broken-winged bird / That cannot fly." (Langston Hughes)
What literary device is used in "Life is a broken-winged bird"?
+ metaphor
- simile
- alliteration
- onomatopoeia
E: The line says life IS a broken-winged bird, without "like" or "as". That is a metaphor.

Q: Read the poem lines and answer.
"Hold fast to dreams / For if dreams die / Life is a broken-winged bird / That cannot fly." (Langston Hughes)
What is the main message of these lines?
+ Without dreams, life loses its purpose and freedom.
- Birds should be protected.
- Dreams are dangerous.
- Life is always easy.
E: A bird that cannot fly has lost its freedom and purpose. The poet says life without dreams is like that, so we should hold on to our dreams.

Q: Choose the correct subordinator: "____ you read the book carefully, you will not understand the ending."
+ Unless
- Because
- Although
- Since
E: "Unless" means "if ... not": If you don't read the book carefully, you will not understand the ending.

Q: Choose the correct subordinator: "Some readers enjoy poetry, ____ others prefer novels."
+ whereas
- because
- unless
- so that
E: "Whereas" introduces a contrast between two facts: some readers enjoy poetry, but others prefer novels.

Q: The sentence that states the main idea of a paragraph is the:
+ topic sentence
- concluding sentence
- supporting detail
- title
E: The topic sentence states the main idea and is often the first sentence. Supporting sentences give details and examples, and the concluding sentence closes the paragraph.

Q: A basic paragraph has three parts. Which list is correct?
+ topic sentence, supporting sentences, concluding sentence
- title, list, signature
- question, answer, question
- introduction, body, conclusion of an essay only
E: A paragraph has a topic sentence (main idea), supporting sentences (details and examples) and a concluding sentence (summary or final thought). Introduction, body and conclusion describe a whole essay, not one paragraph.

Q: "Unity" in a paragraph means:
+ every sentence is about the main idea
- every sentence is the same length
- the paragraph has only one sentence
- every sentence starts with the same word
E: A paragraph has unity when all the sentences support the one main idea in the topic sentence. Sentences about other topics break unity.

Q: "Coherence" in a paragraph means:
+ the ideas are in a logical order and connected smoothly
- the paragraph is very long
- every sentence is a question
- the writer uses difficult words
E: Coherence means the paragraph is easy to follow: ideas are in a logical order and are connected with transition words (first, then, however, as a result) and pronouns.

Q: Read the paragraph and answer.
"(1) Massawa is a wonderful place for a holiday. (2) It has warm weather and beautiful beaches. (3) Visitors can also see the old Ottoman buildings in the old town. (4) My cousin is studying medicine in Asmara. (5) For these reasons, many people choose to spend their holidays in Massawa."
Which sentence breaks the unity of the paragraph?
+ sentence 4
- sentence 1
- sentence 2
- sentence 5
E: The paragraph is about Massawa as a holiday place. Sentence 4 (about a cousin studying medicine) does not support this main idea.

Q: In the paragraph about Massawa, sentence 1 is the:
+ topic sentence
- concluding sentence
- irrelevant sentence
- supporting detail
E: "Massawa is a wonderful place for a holiday" states the main idea, so it is the topic sentence.

Q: In the paragraph about Massawa, sentence 5 is the:
+ concluding sentence
- topic sentence
- irrelevant sentence
- example
E: "For these reasons, many people choose..." sums up the paragraph, so it is the concluding sentence. "For these reasons" signals a conclusion.

Q: Which transition word shows a result?
+ therefore
- however
- for example
- in addition
E: "Therefore" introduces a result or conclusion. "However" shows contrast, "for example" gives an example, and "in addition" adds information.

Q: Which transition word adds information?
+ moreover
- however
- although
- instead
E: "Moreover" (like "furthermore" and "in addition") adds more information that supports the same idea.

Q: Which transition shows the order of events (sequence)?
+ after that
- however
- similarly
- in contrast
E: "After that", "first", "next", "then" and "finally" show the order of events. The others show contrast or similarity.

Q: Which is the best topic sentence for a paragraph about the benefits of travelling during holidays?
+ Travelling during holidays has many benefits for young people.
- I went to Keren last year.
- Buses are sometimes late.
- Holidays are in summer.
E: A good topic sentence states the main idea clearly and can be supported by details. The first sentence introduces "many benefits", which the paragraph can then explain.

Q: Which sentence is the best supporting detail for the topic sentence "Holidays give students a chance to rest"?
+ After months of study, a break helps students relax and recover their energy.
- Some students like football.
- The school was built in 1960.
- Bananas are rich in potassium.
E: The supporting detail must explain the topic sentence. Only the first option explains how holidays help students rest.

Q: Put the sentences in a logical order to make a coherent paragraph.
(a) Then we visited the Dahlak Islands by boat. (b) Last summer my family went to Massawa. (c) Finally, we returned home tired but happy. (d) First, we spent two days on the beach.
+ b, d, a, c
- a, b, c, d
- d, a, b, c
- c, b, a, d
E: The paragraph starts with the general situation (b), then follows the sequence words: First (d), Then (a), Finally (c).

Q: "Holiday" in British English often means:
+ a period of rest from work or school, often spent travelling
- a working day
- a school exam
- a type of hotel only
E: A holiday is a time when people do not work or go to school. Public holidays are special national days, such as Independence Day (24 May) in Eritrea.

Q: Eritrean Independence Day is celebrated on:
+ 24 May
- 1 September
- 20 June
- 1 January
E: Eritrea celebrates Independence Day on 24 May, the day in 1991 when the EPLF entered Asmara. 20 June is Martyrs' Day, and 1 September marks the start of the armed struggle (1961).

Q: Which pronoun use helps coherence? "Senait visited Keren. ____ enjoyed the market very much."
+ She
- It
- They
- Him
E: Pronouns refer back to nouns and connect sentences. "She" refers back to Senait, the subject. "It" and "they" do not match, and "him" is an object pronoun for a male.

Q: Which concluding sentence best fits a paragraph about the advantages of camping?
+ In short, camping is a cheap and healthy way to enjoy nature.
- Camping tents are made of nylon.
- My uncle has a car.
- However, I like reading books.
E: A concluding sentence sums up the main idea (advantages of camping). The other sentences introduce new, unrelated details.

Q: Read and answer: "Many tourists visit Asmara because of its modernist architecture. ____, the city's cafés and wide streets make it pleasant to walk around."
+ In addition
- However
- As a result
- For instance, but
E: The second sentence adds another reason to visit Asmara, so "In addition" is the best connector. "However" would show contrast, and "As a result" would show a consequence.

Q: Which word is a synonym of "vacation"?
+ holiday
- job
- task
- lesson
E: "Vacation" (mainly American English) and "holiday" (British English) both mean a period of rest away from work or school.

Q: A paragraph that has good unity and coherence is:
+ focused on one idea and easy to follow
- full of different topics
- written without any connectors
- very short only
E: Unity keeps the paragraph focused on one idea; coherence makes it logical and easy to follow.

- on the other hand

Q: A paragraph that explains how something happens step by step is developed by:

- comparison and contrast
- cause and effect only

Q: A paragraph that shows similarities and differences between two inventions is developed by:
+ comparison and contrast
- narration only
- classification only
- definition only
E: Comparison shows similarities and contrast shows differences, for example between a mobile phone and a landline.

Q: Which signal words are typical of a cause-and-effect paragraph?
+ because, as a result, therefore
- first, second, finally
- like, similarly, both
- for example, such as
E: Cause-and-effect paragraphs explain why something happens and what results. "Because", "as a result", "therefore" and "consequently" signal this.

Q: "Seldom" has a similar meaning to:

E: When a negative adverb (seldom, rarely, never, hardly ever) starts a sentence for emphasis, the auxiliary comes before the subject: Seldom have I seen...

Q: An "invention" is:
+ something new that someone has designed or created for the first time
- something found in nature, like a mountain
- a holiday
- a kind of disease
E: An invention is a new device or process created by a person, such as the telephone or the light bulb. Finding something that already existed is a discovery.

Q: Who is usually credited with inventing the telephone (patented in 1876)?

- Thomas Edison
- Isaac Newton
- Albert Einstein
E: Alexander Graham Bell received the first US patent for the telephone in 1876. Edison is known for the light bulb and the phonograph, Newton for laws of motion, and Einstein for relativity.

Q: The difference between "invent" and "discover" is:

- they mean exactly the same
- you discover machines and invent planets
- "invent" is only used for food
E: We invent machines or processes (the printing press). We discover things that already exist in nature (a new star, penicillin's effect).
