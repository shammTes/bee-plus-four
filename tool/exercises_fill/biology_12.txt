Q: Producers in a food chain capture 10 000 kJ of energy. Using the 10% rule, how much energy reaches the secondary consumers?
+ 100 kJ
- 1000 kJ
- 10 kJ
- 9000 kJ
E: About 10% of the energy passes from one trophic level to the next.
Producers 10 000 kJ → primary consumers 10% = 1000 kJ → secondary consumers 10% of 1000 = 100 kJ.
1000 kJ is the primary consumer level, and 10 kJ is the tertiary consumer level.

Q: Which organisms break down dead plants and animals and return nutrients to the soil?
+ decomposers such as bacteria and fungi
- producers such as grass
- herbivores such as goats
- carnivores such as lions
E: Decomposers (mainly bacteria and fungi) feed on dead organic matter and release simple nutrients (such as nitrates and phosphates) back into the soil, where plants can use them again.

Q: A lichen is made of an alga and a fungus living together, and both benefit. This relationship is:
+ mutualism
- parasitism
- commensalism
- competition
E: In mutualism both partners benefit: the alga makes food by photosynthesis, and the fungus gives shelter and holds water and minerals. In parasitism one benefits and the other is harmed, and in commensalism one benefits and the other is not affected.

Q: The maximum population size that an environment can support for a long time is called its:
+ carrying capacity
- birth rate
- population density
- biotic potential
E: Carrying capacity depends on resources such as food, water and space. When a population is larger than the carrying capacity, resources run out and the population falls.

Q: Bare rock is slowly colonised, first by lichens and mosses, then grasses, shrubs and finally trees. This gradual change is called:
+ primary succession
- secondary succession
- eutrophication
- migration
E: Primary succession starts on a surface with no soil (bare rock, new lava). Lichens and mosses are pioneer species that begin making soil. Secondary succession starts where soil already exists, for example after a fire or on an abandoned farm.

Q: Why is a pyramid of numbers sometimes inverted (upside-down), for example one tree supporting thousands of insects?
+ Because a single large producer can feed many small consumers.
- Because energy increases at higher trophic levels.
- Because insects are producers.
- Because trees eat insects.
E: A pyramid of numbers only counts individuals, not their size. One big tree can support many insects, so the producer level is narrower than the consumer level. A pyramid of energy, however, can never be inverted, because energy is always lost between levels.

Q: Excess fertilizer washes into a lake, causing algae to grow rapidly; when they die, bacteria use up the oxygen and fish die. This process is called:
+ eutrophication
- nitrogen fixation
- transpiration
- succession
E: Eutrophication is the over-enrichment of water with nutrients (nitrates and phosphates). The algal bloom and its decomposition reduce dissolved oxygen, which kills fish and other animals.
