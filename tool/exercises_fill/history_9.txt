Q: The famous fossil skeleton called "Lucy" (Australopithecus afarensis), discovered in 1974, was found in:
+ the Afar region of Ethiopia (Hadar)
- the Nile Delta in Egypt
- southern France
- the Gobi Desert in China
E: Lucy was found at Hadar in the Afar region of Ethiopia in 1974. She lived about 3.2 million years ago and walked upright on two legs. The discovery supports the idea that East Africa is a cradle of humankind.

Q: The discovery of a hominid cranium about one million years old at Buya, in the northern Danakil Depression, shows that:
+ the region of Eritrea was inhabited by early humans very long ago
- humans first appeared in Europe
- Eritrea had no prehistory
- the first cities were built in the Danakil
E: The Buya cranium is about one million years old. As part of the East African Rift Valley, Eritrea was inhabited by early humans, which is why the region is important for the study of human origins.

Q: Which period is also called the "New Stone Age", when people began farming and using polished stone tools?
+ the Neolithic period
- the Paleolithic period
- the Bronze Age
- the Iron Age
E: "Neo" means new and "lithic" means stone. In the Neolithic period people domesticated plants and animals and lived in permanent villages. The Paleolithic (Old Stone Age) came before it, and the Bronze and Iron Ages came after.

Q: Which invention, developed in Mesopotamia around 3200 B.C.E., allowed people to keep written records?
+ cuneiform writing
- the printing press
- paper money
- the compass
E: The Sumerians of Mesopotamia developed cuneiform, wedge-shaped marks pressed into clay tablets, to record trade, laws and stories. Writing is one of the main characteristics of civilization.
