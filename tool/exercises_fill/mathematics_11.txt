Q: Solve $$2^{x+3} = 64$$.
+ x = 3
- x = 6
- x = 9
- x = 2
E: Write 64 as a power of 2: $$64 = 2^6$$. Then $$x + 3 = 6$$, so x = 3.
Check: $$2^{3+3} = 2^6 = 64$$ ✓.

Q: Solve $$\log_3(x - 1) = 2$$.
+ x = 10
- x = 9
- x = 7
- x = 5
E: Change to exponential form: $$x - 1 = 3^2 = 9$$, so x = 10.
Check: $$\log_3 9 = 2$$ ✓. The domain condition x − 1 > 0 is satisfied.

Q: Simplify $$\log_2 8 + \log_2 4$$.
+ 5
- 12
- 32
- 6
E: Use the product rule: $$\log_2 8 + \log_2 4 = \log_2 (8 \times 4) = \log_2 32 = 5$$.
(Or: 3 + 2 = 5.) 12 adds 8 + 4 inside the log, which is wrong.

Q: What is the domain of $$f(x) = \log_2(2x + 6)$$?
+ x > −3
- x > 3
- x ≥ −3
- all real numbers
E: The argument of a logarithm must be positive: 2x + 6 > 0, so x > −3. Equality is not allowed because log 0 is undefined.

Q: What is the range of $$f(x) = 3^x$$?
+ all positive real numbers (y > 0)
- all real numbers
- y ≥ 1
- y < 0
E: An exponential function with a positive base is always positive and never reaches 0. As x → −∞, $$3^x \to 0$$, and as x → ∞, $$3^x \to \infty$$. So the range is y > 0.

Q: Solve $$\log x + \log(x - 3) = 1$$ (base 10).
+ x = 5
- x = −2
- x = 5 or x = −2
- x = 10
E: Combine: $$\log[x(x - 3)] = 1$$, so $$x(x - 3) = 10$$ and $$x^2 - 3x - 10 = 0$$.
Factor: $$(x - 5)(x + 2) = 0$$, so x = 5 or x = −2.
x = −2 is rejected because log(−2) is undefined. Answer: x = 5. Check: log 5 + log 2 = log 10 = 1 ✓.

Q: A population of 2000 bacteria doubles every hour. How many bacteria are there after 4 hours?
+ 32 000
- 16 000
- 8000
- 10 000
E: $$N = 2000 \times 2^t$$. After 4 hours: $$N = 2000 \times 2^4 = 2000 \times 16 = 32\,000$$.
8000 assumes it doubles only twice, and 10 000 adds 2000 each hour (linear growth).

Q: If $$\log_a 2 = 0.3$$ and $$\log_a 3 = 0.5$$, find $$\log_a 12$$.
+ 1.1
- 0.8
- 1.5
- 0.9
E: $$12 = 2^2 \times 3$$, so $$\log_a 12 = 2\log_a 2 + \log_a 3 = 2(0.3) + 0.5 = 1.1$$.
0.8 forgets that 2 appears twice in 12.

Q: Find the simple interest on 5000 Nakfa at 6% per year for 3 years.
+ 900 Nakfa
- 300 Nakfa
- 5900 Nakfa
- 1800 Nakfa
E: Simple interest $$I = Prt = 5000 \times 0.06 \times 3 = 900$$ Nakfa.
5900 Nakfa is the total amount (principal + interest), not the interest.

Q: 4000 Nakfa is invested at 5% compounded annually. What is the amount after 2 years?
+ 4410 Nakfa
- 4400 Nakfa
- 4200 Nakfa
- 4500 Nakfa
E: $$A = P(1 + r)^n = 4000 \times 1.05^2 = 4000 \times 1.1025 = 4410$$ Nakfa.
4400 Nakfa is the simple-interest amount (4000 + 2 × 200); compound interest also earns interest on the first year's interest.

Q: A machine costs 20 000 Nakfa and has a scrap value of 2000 Nakfa after 6 years. Using the straight-line method, what is the annual depreciation?
+ 3000 Nakfa
- 3333 Nakfa
- 2000 Nakfa
- 18 000 Nakfa
E: Annual depreciation = $$\frac{\text{cost} - \text{scrap value}}{\text{useful life}} = \frac{20\,000 - 2000}{6} = \frac{18\,000}{6} = 3000$$ Nakfa.
3333 Nakfa forgets to subtract the scrap value (20 000 ÷ 6).

Q: Two partners invest 30 000 Nakfa and 50 000 Nakfa. They share a profit of 16 000 Nakfa in the ratio of their investments. What is the second partner's share?
+ 10 000 Nakfa
- 6000 Nakfa
- 8000 Nakfa
- 12 000 Nakfa
E: Ratio = 30 000 : 50 000 = 3 : 5, total 8 parts. One part = 16 000 ÷ 8 = 2000 Nakfa.
Second partner: 5 × 2000 = 10 000 Nakfa (the first gets 6000 Nakfa).

Q: A shirt is marked at 800 Nakfa and sold at a 15% discount. What is the selling price?
+ 680 Nakfa
- 785 Nakfa
- 120 Nakfa
- 920 Nakfa
E: Discount = 15% of 800 = 0.15 × 800 = 120 Nakfa. Selling price = 800 − 120 = 680 Nakfa.
120 Nakfa is the discount itself, and 920 Nakfa adds it instead of subtracting.
