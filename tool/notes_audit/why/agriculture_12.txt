agri12-u1-q01|Ruminants (cattle, sheep, goats, camels) have a four-chambered stomach; microbes in the rumen ferment the cellulose of roughage, so these animals can live on grass and browse.
agri12-u1-q02|Donkeys are cheap to keep, hardy, need little water and can carry loads on rough, steep paths, so they are the main pack and transport animals in rural Eritrea.
agri12-u1-q03|Roughages are bulky feeds high in fibre and low in energy per kg, such as straw, hay, grass and browse; concentrates (grain, oilseed cake) are low in fibre and rich in energy or protein.
agri12-u1-q04|Prevention is cheaper and more effective than treatment: vaccination, clean housing and water, quarantine of new animals and control of ticks and worms stop diseases before they spread.
agri12-u1-q05|Breeding should depend on body weight, not age: a doe mated too small may have difficult births and stunted growth, so wait until she reaches about two-thirds to three-quarters of adult weight.
agri12-u2-q02|Without tree cover, rain hits bare soil and runs off quickly, washing soil downhill; the silt fills reservoirs and dams, and less water soaks in to recharge groundwater.
agri12-u2-q03|Mangroves are salt-tolerant trees that grow in the shallow, sheltered intertidal zone of coasts and islands, such as along the Red Sea coast and the Dahlak islands.
agri12-u2-q04|An enclosure is a fenced or protected area closed to livestock and cutting; without grazing pressure, grasses, shrubs and tree seedlings can regrow and the soil recovers.
agri12-u3-q01|On the steep highland slopes, once vegetation is removed, heavy rain runs off as sheet flow and forms rills and gullies — water erosion is the main type.
agri12-u3-q02|A contour bund follows the contour (a line of equal height) across the slope, so water is held along it and soaks in instead of running downhill and carrying soil.
agri12-u3-q03|A check dam (stone or brush wall across a gully) slows the flow of water so it drops its silt behind the dam; the gully gradually fills and stops deepening.
agri12-u3-q05|If irrigation water cannot drain away, the water table rises (waterlogging); as water evaporates, dissolved salts are left at the surface (salinity), harming crops.
agri12-u4-q01|Opportunity cost is the value of the best alternative given up when a choice is made — e.g. using a field for maize means losing the income it could have earned from vegetables.
agri12-u4-q02|When more and more of one input (fertiliser, labour) is added to a fixed input (land), each extra unit eventually adds less extra output than the one before.
agri12-u4-q03|Records of inputs, costs, yields and sales show the profit of each enterprise, help plan the next season, and are needed for loans and tax.
agri12-u4-q04|Good management chooses crops and livestock that suit the local climate, soil and water (ecology), which lowers risk and cost and raises yields.
