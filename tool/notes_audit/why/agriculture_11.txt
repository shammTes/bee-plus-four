agri11-u1-q01|Domestication is bringing wild plants and animals under human control and breeding them over generations for useful traits (e.g. wild grasses to wheat, wild goats to herd goats).
agri11-u1-q03|Agronomy is the branch of crop science dealing with field crops — cereals, pulses, oil and fibre crops — their production, breeding and soil management.
agri11-u2-q01|A productive soil is a mixture of mineral particles (sand, silt, clay), organic matter (humus), air and water in the pore spaces, and living organisms such as bacteria and earthworms.
agri11-u2-q03|Local (landrace) varieties have been selected by farmers over many generations in that place, so they often carry genes for drought, pest and disease tolerance suited to local conditions.
agri11-u3-q01|Chickpea is a legume (pulse); bacteria in its root nodules fix nitrogen from the air, leaving the soil richer in nitrogen for the next crop, such as wheat or barley.
agri11-u3-q02|Broadcasting is sowing by scattering seed by hand or machine over the prepared soil surface, then covering it lightly; it is quick but gives uneven spacing.
agri11-u3-q03|In layering a stem is bent down and covered with soil while still attached to the parent plant, which feeds it until roots form; a cutting is removed from the parent before it roots.
agri11-u3-q04|Moist grain in a closed bag stays damp and warm, which is ideal for moulds (some produce toxins) and storage insects; grain must be dried well before storage.
agri11-u3-q05|A nursery is a protected bed where seedlings are raised with care (shade, watering, pest control) until they are strong enough to be transplanted to the field.
