bio12-u1-q08|Meiosis forms gametes, so it happens in the reproductive organs: testes and ovaries in animals, anthers (pollen) and ovules in flowering plants.
bio12-u1-q09|Interphase is the preparation stage between divisions: the cell grows, replicates its DNA, makes new organelles and stores energy; chromosomes are still uncoiled chromatin and the nuclear membrane is intact.

bio12-u1-q12|The genotype is the set of alleles an organism carries (e.g. Tt); the phenotype is the observable trait that results (tall), influenced also by the environment.

bio12-u2-q01|Evolution is the gradual change in the inherited characteristics of populations over many generations, which can produce new species from earlier ones.
bio12-u2-q02|Natural selection can only act on differences that are inherited; if all individuals were identical, none would survive better than the others, so variation is the raw material of evolution.
bio12-u2-q03|Organisms produce more offspring than resources can support, so they compete (struggle for existence); those with inherited traits best suited to the environment survive and reproduce more (survival of the fittest), passing on those traits.
bio12-u2-q04|Darwin knew that offspring vary and that variation is inherited, but genes and mutations were unknown in his time, so he could not explain where new variation comes from or how it is passed on.
bio12-u2-q05|The human arm and bat wing have the same basic bone pattern (humerus, radius, ulna, carpals, digits) from a common ancestor, but different functions — they are homologous organs.
bio12-u3-q01|Photosynthesis removes CO₂ from the air and releases O₂; respiration releases CO₂ and uses O₂ — together they cycle carbon (and oxygen) between organisms and the atmosphere.
bio12-u3-q02|Bacteria do every key step of the nitrogen cycle: nitrogen fixation (Rhizobium, Azotobacter), nitrification (Nitrosomonas, Nitrobacter), denitrification, and decomposition to ammonium.
bio12-u3-q03|A habitat is the physical place where an organism lives, e.g. a pond or the bark of a tree; its niche is its role there.
bio12-u3-q04|Abiotic factors are the non-living parts of the environment: temperature, light, water, soil, wind, pH. Biotic factors are the living organisms.
bio12-u3-q05|The taiga (boreal forest) is a cold northern biome dominated by evergreen conifers such as pine, spruce and fir.
bio12-u3-q06|Tropical rainforests near the equator receive the most rain (often over 2 000 mm a year) and are warm all year.
bio12-u3-q07|Savanna is a tropical grassland with scattered trees and a long dry season, so it belongs to the grassland biome.
bio12-u3-q08|Taiga (and tundra) need long, very cold winters found only at high northern latitudes; Africa lies between about 37°N and 35°S, so it has no taiga.
bio12-u3-q09|Natural gas, coal and oil (fossil fuels) form over millions of years, so once used they cannot be replaced on a human time scale — they are non-renewable.
bio12-u3-q10|The ozone (O₃) layer in the stratosphere absorbs most of the Sun's harmful ultraviolet radiation.
bio12-u3-q11|Energy is lost at every trophic level (as heat), so each level always has less energy than the one below; a pyramid of numbers (one tree, many insects) or biomass can be inverted.
