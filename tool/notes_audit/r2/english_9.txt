@tip Decide the relationship between the two ideas first: addition, contrast, cause, result or time. Then pick the linking word for that relationship.

Q: She loved him; ____, she did not want to marry so young.
O: *however | therefore | because | so that
W: The two ideas contrast: love, but not wanting to marry young. | "however" signals contrast and is used after a semicolon with a comma. | "therefore" shows result, "because" reason, "so that" purpose.
S: Link with a contrast word: It was raining. We went out. => It was raining; however, we went out.
S: Link with a result word: He studied. He passed. => He studied; therefore, he passed.

Q: ____ he was very poor, he helped everyone in the village.

S: Join with although: She was ill. She came to school. => Although she was ill, she came to school.
S: Join with even though: It was late. They kept working. => Even though it was late, they kept working.

@tip In reported speech, move the tense one step back when the reporting verb is in the past, and change pronouns and time words to fit the new speaker.

Q: He said, "I am tired." → He said that he ____ tired.
O: *was | is | has been | will be
W: The reporting verb "said" is past. | Present simple "am" moves back to past simple "was". | The pronoun "I" becomes "he".
S: Report: She said, "I like tea." => She said that she liked tea.
S: Report: They said, "We are ready." => They said that they were ready.

Q: "Please close the window," the teacher said to me. → The teacher asked me ____ the window.

W: A polite request is reported with ask + object + to-infinitive. | So: asked me to close the window. | "please" disappears because "asked" already shows politeness.
