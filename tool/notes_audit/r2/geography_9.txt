@lesson geo9-u1-l1-2
C: Which branch of geography studies landforms, climate, soils and vegetation?
O: *Physical geography | Human geography | Economic geography only | Political geography
W: Geography has two main branches. | Physical geography studies natural features: landforms, climate, soils, water and vegetation. | Human geography studies people – population, settlement, economic activities.

@lesson geo9-u3-l3-4
C: Air temperature decreases with altitude at an average rate of about ____.
O: *6.5 °C per 1,000 m | 1 °C per 1,000 m | 15 °C per 100 m | 0 °C – it does not change
W: The normal (environmental) lapse rate is about 6.5 °C for every 1,000 m. | Air is heated mainly from the ground, and thinner air holds less heat. | So mountains are cooler than lowlands.

@lesson geo9-u3-l3-5
C: Air pressure is measured with a ____.
O: *barometer | thermometer | rain gauge | hygrometer
W: A barometer measures atmospheric pressure (in millibars or hectopascals). | A thermometer measures temperature; a rain gauge measures rainfall; a hygrometer measures humidity. | So the answer is barometer.

@lesson geo9-u4-l4-2
C: A region is best defined as ____.
O: *an area with one or more common characteristics that set it apart from surrounding areas | any country | a single city | a line of latitude
W: Geographers group places into regions by shared features. | These may be physical (climate, relief) or human (language, economy). | A region need not match a country's borders.

@lesson geo9-u4-l4-3
C: Which is a common characteristic of all regions?
O: *They have location, area and boundaries | They are all the same size | They never change | They are all political units
W: Every region has a location and covers an area. | It has boundaries, often transitional zones rather than sharp lines. | Regions vary in size and can change over time.

@lesson geo9-u6-l6-2
C: Two towns are 6 cm apart on a map and 3 km apart on the ground. What is the scale as an RF?
O: *1:50,000 | 1:5,000 | 1:500,000 | 1:20,000
W: Convert to the same unit: 3 km = 300,000 cm. | RF = map distance ÷ ground distance = 6 ÷ 300,000 = 1 ÷ 50,000. | Scale 1:50,000.

@lesson geo9-u6-l6-3
C: A map with scale 1:50,000 is enlarged two times (linear). What is the new scale?
O: *1:25,000 | 1:100,000 | 1:50,000 | 1:200,000
W: Enlarging makes map distances bigger, so the RF denominator gets smaller. | New denominator = 50,000 ÷ 2 = 25,000. | New scale 1:25,000.
