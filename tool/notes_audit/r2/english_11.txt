X: Linking verbs and sentence boundaries
B: A **copula** (linking verb) connects the subject to a description: *be, become, seem, look, feel*. Tigrinya can drop or attach the copula, but English always needs it: ✗ *She a nurse* → **She is a nurse**; ✗ *The soup hot* → **The soup is hot**.
B: A **clause boundary** is where one complete idea ends. Two complete ideas cannot be joined by a comma alone (a *comma splice*): ✗ *I was hungry, I ate*.

@lesson eng11-u3-l3-1
X: Choosing a modal by meaning
B: Modals add attitude to a verb. Always ask *what is the speaker's meaning?* – **obligation** (must, have to), **no obligation** (don't have to, needn't), **prohibition** (mustn't), **advice** (should, ought to), **ability** (can, could), **permission** (can, may), **possibility** (may, might, could).
B: **must** usually shows the speaker's own feeling or a rule; **have to** often shows an outside rule: *I must study tonight* vs *We have to wear uniform.* In the past both become **had to**.
B: Grammar of modals: no -s (*she can*), no to (*you should go*), questions by inversion (*Can you help?*), negatives with not (*mustn't, can't*).

X: Perfect modals in context
B: Perfect modals look back at the past: **modal + have + past participle**.
B: **must have** = I'm sure it happened (*The streets are wet – it must have rained*). **can't/couldn't have** = I'm sure it didn't happen. **may/might/could have** = perhaps it happened.
