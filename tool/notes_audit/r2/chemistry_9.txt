# Chemistry 9 round 2

@unit chem9-u4
@tip In practical questions, think safety first, then choose the apparatus whose precision matches the job; in review questions, write particles, formulas or balanced equations step by step.

Q: Which piece of apparatus measures 25.0 cm³ of a solution most accurately?
O: *Volumetric pipette | Beaker | Conical (Erlenmeyer) flask | Test tube
W: Beakers, flasks and test tubes have rough or no volume marks. | A volumetric pipette is calibrated to deliver one fixed volume very precisely. | So use a 25.0 cm³ volumetric pipette.
S: Which apparatus delivers variable volumes accurately in a titration? => A burette.
S: Is a graduated cylinder more or less accurate than a pipette? => Less accurate.

Q: How should you heat a flammable liquid such as ethanol in the laboratory?
O: *In a water bath or on a hot plate | Directly over a Bunsen flame | In a closed container over a flame | With a match held under the beaker
W: Flammable vapours can catch fire from an open flame. | A water bath or hot plate heats without a naked flame. | Never heat a closed container – pressure can make it burst.
S: Why must closed containers never be heated? => Pressure builds up and they can explode.
S: Name the flame used for strong heating. => The blue (non-luminous) Bunsen flame.

Q: True or false: You should look down into a test tube while heating it to see the reaction clearly.
TF: false
W: Heated liquids can spit or boil over suddenly. | Always point the mouth of the test tube away from yourself and others. | Observe from the side, wearing safety goggles.
S: Which way should a heated test tube point? => Away from people.
S: What protects your eyes in the lab? => Safety goggles.

Q: When reading a measuring cylinder containing water, you should read ____.
O: *the bottom of the meniscus at eye level | the top of the meniscus from above | the edge of the glass from below | any point on the curve
W: Water forms a curved surface (meniscus) that dips in the middle. | Reading from above or below gives a parallax error. | Put your eye level with the surface and read the bottom of the curve.
S: What error is caused by reading from an angle? => Parallax error.
S: For mercury, which part of the meniscus is read? => The top.

Q: A Bunsen burner gives a yellow, smoky flame. What should you do to get a hot blue flame?
O: *Open the air hole | Close the air hole | Turn off the gas completely | Add water to the burner
W: A yellow (luminous) flame means too little air: incomplete combustion. | Opening the air hole lets more air mix with the gas. | Complete combustion gives a hot, clean blue flame.
S: Which flame is safer when the burner is not in use? => The yellow flame, because it is easy to see.
S: Which flame is hotter? => The blue flame.

Q: What is the first thing to do if a chemical splashes into your eye?
O: *Rinse the eye with plenty of running water and tell the teacher | Rub the eye hard | Cover it and continue working | Add another chemical to neutralise it
W: Speed matters: flush the chemical out at once. | Rinse with plenty of clean running water (eyewash) for several minutes. | Report to the teacher; never add another chemical.
S: Why should you not rub the eye? => It spreads the chemical and can scratch the eye.
S: Name one piece of PPE for the body. => A lab coat.

Q: In the scientific method, a testable prediction that explains an observation is called a ____.
O: *hypothesis | conclusion | law | result
W: The method: observe → ask a question → form a hypothesis → experiment → analyse → conclude. | A hypothesis is a testable explanation or prediction. | Experiments then support or reject it.
S: What do we call the factor that is deliberately changed? => The independent variable.
S: Why do we keep other factors constant? => So the test is fair.

Q: Which method separates salt from salt solution?
O: *Evaporation | Filtration | Using a magnet | Decanting
W: Salt is dissolved, so it passes through filter paper. | Heating evaporates the water and leaves the salt behind. | Magnets and decanting do not separate a dissolved solid.
S: Which method separates sand from water? => Filtration.
S: Which method collects pure water from salt water? => Distillation.

Q: Which of these is a chemical change?
O: *Burning wood | Melting ice | Dissolving sugar | Crushing chalk
W: A chemical change makes a new substance and is hard to reverse. | Burning wood produces ash, carbon dioxide and water. | Melting, dissolving and crushing only change state or size.
S: Give one sign of a chemical change. => Gas given off / colour change / heat or light produced.
S: Is boiling water a chemical change? => No, it is a physical change.

Q: An atom has 11 protons, 12 neutrons and 11 electrons. What is its mass number?
O: *23 | 11 | 12 | 34
W: Mass number = protons + neutrons. | 11 + 12 = 23. | Electrons have almost no mass and are not counted.
S: What is its atomic number? => 11.
S: Which element is it? => Sodium (Na).

Q: Isotopes of an element have the same number of ____ but different numbers of ____.
O: *protons; neutrons | neutrons; protons | electrons; protons | protons; electrons
W: The number of protons fixes the element. | Isotopes differ only in neutrons, so their mass numbers differ. | Example: carbon-12 and carbon-14 both have 6 protons.
S: How many neutrons does carbon-14 have? => 8.
S: Do isotopes have the same chemical properties? => Yes.

Q: What is the electron arrangement of an atom with 17 electrons?
O: *2, 8, 7 | 2, 7, 8 | 8, 8, 1 | 2, 8, 8
W: Fill shells in order: first shell up to 2, second up to 8, then the third. | 2 + 8 = 10, leaving 17 − 10 = 7. | Arrangement 2, 8, 7 (chlorine).
S: Electron arrangement of magnesium (12 electrons)? => 2, 8, 2.
S: How many valence electrons does chlorine have? => 7.

Q: Which particle has a negative charge and is found in shells around the nucleus?
O: *Electron | Proton | Neutron | Nucleus
W: Protons (+) and neutrons (no charge) are in the nucleus. | Electrons (−1) move in shells around it. | So the answer is the electron.
S: Which particle has no charge? => The neutron.
S: Relative mass of a proton? => 1.

Q: What is the correct formula of aluminium oxide? (Al³⁺, O²⁻)
O: *Al₂O₃ | AlO | Al₃O₂ | AlO₃
W: Charges must balance: Al³⁺ and O²⁻. | Lowest common multiple of 3 and 2 is 6: 2 Al³⁺ = +6 and 3 O²⁻ = −6. | Formula Al₂O₃.
S: Formula of magnesium chloride (Mg²⁺, Cl⁻)? => MgCl₂.
S: Formula of sodium oxide (Na⁺, O²⁻)? => Na₂O.

Q: Which equation is balanced?
O: *2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O | H₂ + O₂ → H₂O | H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O | 2H₂ + 2O₂ → 2H₂O
W: Count atoms on each side. | 2H₂ + O₂: 4 H and 2 O; 2H₂O: 4 H and 2 O – equal. | The other equations have unequal numbers of H or O atoms.
S: Balance: Mg + O₂ → MgO. => 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO.
S: Balance: N₂ + H₂ → NH₃. => N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃.

Q: How many atoms are in one formula unit of CaCO₃?
O: *5 | 3 | 4 | 6
W: CaCO₃ contains 1 Ca, 1 C and 3 O. | 1 + 1 + 3 = 5 atoms. | Subscripts count the atom immediately before them.
S: How many oxygen atoms are in 2H₂SO₄? => 8.
S: How many atoms in H₂O? => 3.

Q: The symbol of potassium is ____.
O: *K | P | Po | Pt
W: Some symbols come from Latin names. | Potassium comes from "kalium", so its symbol is K. | P is phosphorus, Po polonium and Pt platinum.
S: Symbol of sodium and its Latin name? => Na, natrium.
S: Symbol of iron? => Fe (ferrum).

Q: True or false: A mixture can be separated by physical methods, but a compound cannot.
TF: true
W: In a mixture the substances are not chemically joined. | Filtration, evaporation or distillation can separate them. | A compound's elements are chemically bonded and need a chemical reaction to separate.
S: Is air a mixture or a compound? => A mixture.
S: Is water a mixture or a compound? => A compound.

Q: Which state of matter has a fixed volume but no fixed shape?
O: *Liquid | Solid | Gas | Plasma only
W: Solids keep both shape and volume. | Gases fill their container: no fixed shape or volume. | Liquids keep their volume but take the shape of the container.
S: Which state is easiest to compress? => Gas.
S: What is the change from gas to liquid called? => Condensation.

Q: Calculate the relative formula mass of CO₂. (C = 12, O = 16)
A: 44
W: Relative formula mass = sum of relative atomic masses. | CO₂ = 12 + 2 × 16. | = 12 + 32 = 44.
S: Relative formula mass of H₂O (H = 1, O = 16)? => 18.
S: Relative formula mass of NaCl (Na = 23, Cl = 35.5)? => 58.5.
