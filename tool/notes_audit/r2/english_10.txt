@tip Obligation: must / have to; no obligation: don't have to; prohibition: mustn't; advice: should / ought to. Inference: must (sure yes), can't (sure no), might/may/could (possible).

Q: You ____ wear a seat belt. It's the law.
O: *must | might | needn't | could
W: "It's the law" shows a strong rule. | Strong obligation is expressed with must (or have to). | "might" and "could" show possibility; "needn't" shows no obligation.
S: Complete: Students ____ wear uniform at our school. => must / have to.
S: Complete: Drivers ____ stop at a red light. => must.

Q: You ____ bring food; lunch will be provided.
O: *don't have to | mustn't | must | have to
W: Lunch is provided, so bringing food is not necessary. | "don't have to" means no obligation. | "mustn't" would mean it is forbidden, which is a different meaning.
S: Complete: It's Saturday, so I ____ get up early. => don't have to.
S: Complete: You ____ pay; the museum is free. => don't have to / needn't.

@tip A noun clause does the job of a noun (subject, object or complement). It begins with that, what, whether/if or a wh- word and uses statement word order.

Q: I don't know ____.
O: *where she lives | where does she live | where lives she | where she does live?
W: The clause is the object of "know", so it is a noun clause. | Noun clauses use statement order: wh-word + subject + verb. | So: where she lives.
S: Complete: Tell me what ____ (you / want). => you want.
S: Complete: I wonder why ____ (he / is) late. => he is.

Q: ____ he passed the exam surprised everyone.
O: *That | What | Who | Which
W: The whole clause "___ he passed the exam" is the subject of "surprised". | When the clause is a full statement, it is introduced by "That". | "What" would need a missing part inside the clause.
S: Complete: ____ she is honest is clear. => That.
S: Complete: ____ you said was true. => What.

Q: I'm not sure ____ the bus has left.
O: *whether | what | that's | which
W: The speaker is uncertain about a yes/no question (Has the bus left?). | Reported yes/no questions use whether or if. | So: whether the bus has left.
S: Complete: Ask him ____ he can come. => if / whether.
S: Complete: I don't know ____ to laugh or cry. => whether.

Q: Identify the noun clause: "The problem is that we have no water."
O: *that we have no water | The problem | is that | we have

S: Find the noun clause: "What he wants is a new bike." => What he wants.
S: Find the noun clause: "I believe that she is right." => that she is right.
