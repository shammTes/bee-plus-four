C: Which contraceptive method also protects against sexually transmitted infections such as HIV?
O: *Condom | Contraceptive pill | Intra-uterine device (IUD) | Vasectomy
W: Most methods only prevent pregnancy. | A condom is a physical barrier that stops semen and body fluids passing between partners. | So it reduces the risk of STIs as well as pregnancy.

@lesson bio11-u5-l5-4
C: When body temperature rises above normal, the skin responds by ____.
O: *vasodilation of skin arterioles and sweating | vasoconstriction and shivering | raising the hairs | reducing blood flow to the skin
W: The hypothalamus detects the rise and triggers cooling responses. | Arterioles in the skin widen (vasodilation), so more heat is lost by radiation. | Sweat glands secrete sweat, which cools the skin as it evaporates.

@lesson bio11-u6-l6-3
C: Which part of a root absorbs most of the water and mineral ions from the soil?
O: *Root hair cells | Root cap | Xylem in the stem | Cork cells
W: Root hairs are long, thin extensions of epidermal cells. | They give a very large surface area in contact with soil water. | Water enters by osmosis and ions by diffusion or active transport.

@lesson bio11-u6-l6-5
C: Sugars made in the leaves are carried to other parts of the plant through the ____.
O: *phloem | xylem | stomata | root hairs
W: Xylem carries water and minerals upward from the roots. | Phloem carries dissolved sugars (translocation) from leaves to roots, fruits and growing points. | So sugars move in the phloem.

@unit bio11-u4
@tip Compare the three muscle types by location, control (voluntary or involuntary), appearance (striated or not) and fatigue.

Q: Muscles that move bones work in antagonistic pairs. When the biceps contracts, the triceps ____.
O: *relaxes | contracts too | becomes a tendon | pushes the bone
W: Muscles can only pull, not push. | One muscle of the pair contracts while the other relaxes. | When the biceps contracts the arm bends and the triceps relaxes.
S: What happens when the arm straightens? => The triceps contracts and the biceps relaxes.
S: What joins muscle to bone? => A tendon.

Q: Which type of muscle is found only in the heart and never tires?
O: *Cardiac muscle | Skeletal muscle | Smooth muscle | Voluntary muscle
W: Cardiac muscle forms the heart wall. | It contracts rhythmically all through life without fatigue. | It is involuntary but striated.
S: Is cardiac muscle striated? => Yes.
S: Is it under conscious control? => No.

Q: Smooth (involuntary) muscle is found in the ____.
O: *walls of the gut and blood vessels | biceps | heart | tongue only
W: Smooth muscle lines hollow organs. | Examples: the alimentary canal (peristalsis), blood vessels, bladder and uterus. | The biceps is skeletal muscle and the heart is cardiac muscle.
S: Name the wave of contraction that moves food along the gut. => Peristalsis.
S: Is smooth muscle striated? => No.

Q: True or false: Skeletal muscle is under voluntary control and appears striped (striated) under a microscope.
TF: true
W: Skeletal muscle is attached to bones and moves them when we choose. | Its fibres show light and dark bands, so it is striated. | It tires more quickly than smooth or cardiac muscle.
S: Name the protein filaments that cause the stripes. => Actin and myosin.
S: What substance supplies energy for contraction? => ATP.

Q: What causes muscle fatigue and cramp during hard exercise?
A: Lactic acid builds up because of anaerobic respiration when oxygen supply is too low.
W: During hard exercise muscles need more energy than oxygen can supply. | They respire anaerobically: glucose → lactic acid. | Lactic acid builds up, causing fatigue and cramp (an oxygen debt).
S: How is the oxygen debt repaid? => By deep breathing after exercise to oxidise the lactic acid.
S: Which respiration releases more energy, aerobic or anaerobic? => Aerobic.

@unit bio11-u5
@tip For each homeostatic system, name the stimulus, the receptor, the control centre, the effector and the response, and show how negative feedback restores the normal level.

Q: Homeostasis is ____.
O: *keeping the internal environment of the body constant | the removal of undigested food | the growth of cells | the movement of muscles
W: Cells work best at stable temperature, water and glucose levels. | Homeostasis keeps these conditions within narrow limits. | It works mainly by negative feedback.
S: Name three conditions controlled by homeostasis. => Body temperature, water content, blood glucose.
S: What type of feedback does it mostly use? => Negative feedback.

Q: Which hormone lowers blood glucose?
O: *Insulin | Glucagon | Adrenaline | ADH
W: Insulin is made by the pancreas when blood glucose is high. | It makes liver and muscle cells take up glucose and store it as glycogen. | Glucagon has the opposite effect.
S: Which hormone raises blood glucose? => Glucagon.
S: Which disease results from too little insulin? => Diabetes (type 1).

Q: When you are dehydrated, the pituitary gland releases more ADH. What is the effect?
O: *The kidneys reabsorb more water and make a small volume of concentrated urine | The kidneys make a large volume of dilute urine | Sweating increases | Blood glucose falls
W: Dehydration raises the concentration of the blood. | More ADH makes the collecting ducts more permeable to water. | More water is reabsorbed, so less, more concentrated urine is made.
S: What happens to ADH after drinking a lot of water? => Less ADH is released.
S: Which organ detects the water level of the blood? => The hypothalamus.

Q: Which organ filters the blood and produces urine?
O: *Kidney | Liver | Pancreas | Lung
W: Each kidney contains about a million nephrons. | They filter blood, reabsorb useful substances and remove urea, extra water and salts. | The result is urine.
S: What is the functional unit of the kidney? => The nephron.
S: Where is urea made? => In the liver.

Q: True or false: Shivering helps warm the body because muscle contractions release heat.
TF: true
W: Shivering is rapid involuntary contraction of skeletal muscles. | Respiration in the contracting muscles releases heat. | This raises body temperature when it falls below normal.
S: What happens to skin arterioles in the cold? => They constrict (vasoconstriction).
S: Which part of the brain controls body temperature? => The hypothalamus.

Q: What is the normal core body temperature of a human?
O: *About 37 °C | About 30 °C | About 42 °C | About 25 °C
W: Human enzymes work best at about 37 °C. | The hypothalamus keeps the core temperature close to this. | Large changes can denature enzymes or slow reactions.
S: What is a raised body temperature called? => Fever (hyperthermia if dangerous).
S: What is a dangerously low body temperature called? => Hypothermia.

@lesson bio11-u4-l4-3
C: Peristalsis in the alimentary canal is produced by ____.
O: *smooth (involuntary) muscle in the gut wall | skeletal muscle attached to bones | cardiac muscle | tendons
W: The gut wall has circular and longitudinal layers of smooth muscle. | Their waves of contraction push food along. | This is involuntary – we do not control it consciously.
