C: A person with blood group O can donate red cells to people of all ABO groups because O red cells ____.
O: *have no A or B antigens | have both A and B antigens | have anti-A and anti-B antibodies only in the cells | contain no haemoglobin
W: Agglutination happens when donor antigens meet matching antibodies in the recipient. | Group O red cells carry neither A nor B antigen. | So they are not agglutinated by anti-A or anti-B – O is the universal donor.

C: During strenuous exercise, the breathing rate increases mainly to ____.
O: *supply more oxygen and remove the extra carbon dioxide produced by the muscles | cool the lungs | reduce the heart rate | store oxygen in the bones
W: Working muscles respire faster, using more oxygen and producing more CO₂. | A rise in blood CO₂ is detected by the brain, which speeds up breathing. | Faster, deeper breathing brings in O₂ and removes CO₂.

@lesson bio10-u7-l7-2
WK: Explain how internal receptors keep the body informed
P: A person stands up quickly and feels dizzy for a moment, then recovers. Explain the role of internal receptors.
ST: **Step 1: Name the change.** Standing quickly lowers blood pressure in the head for a moment. | **Step 2: Name the receptors.** Pressure receptors (baroreceptors – a kind of mechanoreceptor) in the aorta and carotid arteries detect the fall. | **Step 3: Message.** They send impulses to the brain (medulla). | **Step 4: Response.** The brain raises the heart rate and narrows blood vessels, so blood pressure returns to normal and the dizziness passes.
A: Baroreceptors (internal mechanoreceptors) detect the drop in blood pressure and signal the brain, which raises heart rate and constricts vessels to restore pressure.

@lesson bio10-u8-l8-3
C: A newly hatched gosling follows the first moving object it sees. This behaviour is called ____.
O: *imprinting | a reflex | habituation | conditioning
W: Imprinting is rapid learning that happens during a short sensitive period early in life. | The young animal forms an attachment, usually to its mother. | Reflexes are automatic responses; habituation is learning to ignore a repeated stimulus.
