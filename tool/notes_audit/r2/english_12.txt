@tip A finite clause has a verb that shows tense and agrees with a subject; a non-finite clause uses to-infinitive, -ing or -ed forms. In long sentences, find the main subject and main verb first.

Q: Which underlined part is a non-finite clause? "**Having lost his job**, Tesfay started his own business."
O: *Having lost his job | Tesfay started | his own business | started his own business
W: "Having lost his job" has no tense-marked verb and no subject of its own. | It uses a perfect participle, so it is non-finite. | "Tesfay started ..." is the finite main clause.
S: Find the non-finite clause: "To succeed, you must work hard." => To succeed.
S: Find the non-finite clause: "Shocked by the news, she sat down." => Shocked by the news.

Q: Which sentence contains only finite clauses?
O: *She failed the test because she was ill. | Feeling ill, she failed the test. | She tried to pass the test. | Having been ill, she failed.
W: Finite verbs show tense: failed, was. | Options B, C and D contain non-finite forms (feeling, to pass, having been). | So the first sentence has only finite clauses.
S: Finite or non-finite: "when she arrived" => finite (arrived shows past tense).
S: Finite or non-finite: "to arrive on time" => non-finite.

Q: ____ the difficulties, the farmers built terraces on the hills.
O: *Despite | Although | Because | Unless
W: The blank is followed by a noun phrase ("the difficulties"), not a clause. | "Despite" takes a noun phrase. | "Although" needs a finite clause.
S: Rewrite with although: Despite the cold, they swam. => Although it was cold, they swam.
S: Rewrite with despite: Although she was tired, she ran. => Despite being tired, she ran.

Q: Find the main verb: "The students who had worked hard throughout the year, despite many problems at home, finally passed the national exam."
O: *passed | had worked | problems | finally
W: Remove the relative clause (who had worked hard throughout the year) and the phrase (despite many problems at home). | The core sentence is "The students finally passed the national exam". | So the main verb is "passed".
S: Main verb: "The man standing near the gate, whom I met yesterday, is my uncle." => is.
S: Main subject: "The book that you lent me last week was excellent." => The book.

Q: Correct the error: "Walking to school, the rain started to fall."
A: Walking to school, I was caught in the rain. (or: As I was walking to school, the rain started to fall.)
W: A non-finite clause at the start must refer to the subject of the main clause. | Here it seems the rain was walking, which is illogical (a dangling participle). | Give the main clause a subject who can walk, or make the first clause finite.
S: Correct: Having finished dinner, the dishes were washed. => Having finished dinner, we washed the dishes.
S: Correct: While cooking, the phone rang. => While I was cooking, the phone rang.

Q: Read: "Facing drought, rising prices and a shortage of workers, the villagers, who had always depended on rain-fed farming, decided to build a small dam with their own hands." What did the villagers decide to do?
O: *build a small dam themselves | leave the village | stop farming | wait for rain
W: Strip away the opening non-finite clause (Facing drought ...) and the relative clause (who had always depended ...). | The core is "the villagers decided to build a small dam with their own hands". | "with their own hands" means themselves.
S: Find the non-finite clause at the beginning. => Facing drought, rising prices and a shortage of workers.
S: What had the villagers always depended on? => rain-fed farming.

Q: Which word means "a difficult task that tests your ability"?
O: *challenge | reward | routine | luxury
W: A challenge is something difficult that requires effort or skill. | "reward" is given for success; "routine" is usual; "luxury" is comfort. | Answer: challenge.
S: Adjective from "challenge". => challenging.
S: Meaning of "overcome". => To succeed in dealing with a difficulty.

Q: He wants ____ an engineer.
O: *to become | becoming | became | becomes
W: "want" is followed by a to-infinitive. | "to become" is a non-finite form. | "becoming" would follow verbs such as enjoy or consider.
S: Complete: She hopes ____ (study) abroad. => to study.
S: Complete: He considered ____ (leave). => leaving.

Q: Combine using a non-finite clause: "She was encouraged by her teacher. She entered the competition."

W: Both sentences have the same subject (she). | The first, passive sentence can become a past-participle clause: Encouraged by her teacher. | Then add the main clause.

Q: True or false: "To be honest, I didn't like the film" contains a non-finite clause.
TF: true
W: "To be honest" uses a to-infinitive. | It has no tense and no subject, so it is non-finite. | "I didn't like the film" is the finite main clause.
S: Non-finite or finite: "Being a doctor" => non-finite.
S: Non-finite or finite: "because she is a doctor" => finite.

Q: In the sentence "The bridge built by the Italians is still in use", what does "built by the Italians" do?

W: A subordinate clause begins with a subordinator and cannot stand alone. | "after it was read on the radio" depends on the main clause. | "The poem became famous" is the main clause.
S: Find the subordinate clause: "If you read the novel, you will cry." => If you read the novel.
S: Find the subordinate clause: "The author who wrote it is Eritrean." => who wrote it.

Q: Which is the correct collocation?
O: *make a decision | do a decision | take a decide | have a deciding
W: Collocations are fixed partners. | In English we "make a decision". | The other combinations are not natural.
S: Collocation: ____ homework (do / make). => do.
S: Collocation: ____ a mistake (do / make). => make.

Q: "There was ____ rain last night, so the river flooded."
O: *heavy | strong | big | thick
W: "heavy rain" is the natural collocation. | "strong" collocates with wind; "big" and "thick" do not go with rain. | So: heavy rain.

@tip A good paragraph has one topic sentence, supporting sentences that explain it, and a concluding sentence. Unity means every sentence supports the topic; coherence means the ideas flow in a logical order with linking words.

Q: Which sentence is the best topic sentence for a paragraph about Independence Day celebrations?
O: *Independence Day is the most joyful holiday in Eritrea. | People sing. | Some shops close. | I like oranges.
W: A topic sentence states the main idea of the whole paragraph. | "Independence Day is the most joyful holiday in Eritrea" introduces the topic and an opinion to support. | The others are details or unrelated.
S: Topic sentence for a paragraph about Timket? => Timket is one of the most colourful religious festivals in Eritrea.
S: What follows the topic sentence? => Supporting sentences with details and examples.

Q: Which sentence breaks the unity of this paragraph? "(1) Fenkil is celebrated in Massawa every February. (2) It remembers the liberation of the port in 1990. (3) People hold parades and concerts. (4) Massawa has a hot climate."
O: *Sentence 4 | Sentence 1 | Sentence 2 | Sentence 3
W: The paragraph is about the Fenkil celebration. | Sentences 1–3 describe the celebration. | Sentence 4 is about the climate, which is off-topic.
S: How do you fix a paragraph with an off-topic sentence? => Remove it or move it to another paragraph.
S: What is "unity" in a paragraph? => Every sentence supports the topic sentence.

Q: Choose the best linking word: "First, we packed our bags. ____, we caught the bus to Keren."
O: *Then | However | In contrast | For example
W: The sentences describe events in time order. | "Then" (or Next) shows sequence. | "However" and "In contrast" show contrast; "For example" introduces an example.
S: Linker for the last step. => Finally.
S: Linker for adding a point. => In addition / Moreover.

Q: What is the purpose of a concluding sentence?
O: *to summarise or restate the main idea | to introduce a new topic | to give the first example | to ask the reader a question about another subject
W: The concluding sentence closes the paragraph. | It summarises or restates the topic sentence in new words. | It should not introduce a new topic.
S: Write a concluding sentence for a paragraph about Independence Day. => For all these reasons, Independence Day is a day every Eritrean looks forward to.
S: Can a concluding sentence add new information? => No, it should round off the paragraph.

Q: Correct the coherence problem: "We visited Massawa. Finally, we arrived at the hotel. First, we left Asmara early."
A: First, we left Asmara early. Then we visited Massawa. Finally, we arrived at the hotel. (Order the sentences logically.)

S: Put in order: Then we ate. First we cooked. Finally we washed up. => First we cooked. Then we ate. Finally we washed up.
S: Which words help coherence? => Linking words and pronouns that refer back.

S: Place "always": He helps his mother. => He always helps his mother.

@tip Idioms have meanings that differ from their separate words. After negative adverbs (never, rarely, not only, hardly) at the start, invert the subject and auxiliary.

Q: Never ____ such a beautiful sunset.
O: *have I seen | I have seen | I saw | seen I have
W: When a negative adverb like "Never" begins the sentence, the subject and auxiliary swap places. | "have I seen" is the inverted form. | "I have seen" is normal order and is wrong here.
S: Invert: I have rarely heard such music. => Rarely have I heard such music.
S: Invert: I had hardly sat down when the phone rang. => Hardly had I sat down when the phone rang.

Q: Not only ____ the exam, but she also won a scholarship.
O: *did she pass | she passed | passed she | she did pass
W: "Not only" at the start causes inversion with an auxiliary. | Past simple uses "did" + subject + base verb. | So: Not only did she pass ...
S: Invert: He not only sings but also dances. => Not only does he sing, but he also dances.
S: Invert: They not only won but also broke the record. => Not only did they win, but they also broke the record.

Q: What does "break the ice" mean?
O: *to make people feel relaxed at the start of a meeting | to break something frozen | to end a friendship | to cause an accident
W: "break the ice" is an idiom. | It means to do or say something that makes people less shy at the beginning. | It has nothing to do with real ice.
S: Meaning of "a piece of cake". => something very easy.
S: Meaning of "under the weather". => feeling slightly ill.

Q: "My parents expect me to pass with flying colours." What does "with flying colours" mean?
O: *very successfully | with difficulty | by cheating | at the last moment
W: "pass with flying colours" is an idiom meaning to succeed easily and very well. | The context of parents' expectations supports a positive meaning. | So: very successfully.
S: Meaning of "hit the books". => to study hard.
S: Meaning of "burn the midnight oil". => to work or study late into the night.

Q: Correct the error: "Seldom we see such dedication."
A: Seldom do we see such dedication.
W: "Seldom" at the beginning is a negative adverb. | It requires inversion with an auxiliary: do + subject + verb. | So: Seldom do we see ...
S: Correct: Little he knew the truth. => Little did he know the truth.
S: Correct: Under no circumstances you should open this door. => Under no circumstances should you open this door.

Q: Read: "Ruth's family expected her to become a doctor like her mother. She worked hard, but her heart was set on teaching. After a long talk, her parents finally gave her the green light, and today she is a proud teacher in Keren." What does "gave her the green light" mean?
O: *gave her permission | gave her a lamp | refused her request | gave her money
W: The context: Ruth wanted to teach, and after a talk her parents agreed. | "give the green light" means give permission to go ahead. | The result (she is a teacher) confirms this.
S: Meaning of "her heart was set on". => She strongly wanted.
S: Did Ruth meet her family's first expectation? => No; they expected a doctor but she became a teacher.

Q: Choose the idiom that means "to feel very nervous".
O: *to have butterflies in your stomach | to be over the moon | to cost an arm and a leg | to hit the nail on the head
W: "butterflies in your stomach" describes nervousness. | "over the moon" = very happy; "cost an arm and a leg" = very expensive; "hit the nail on the head" = be exactly right. | Answer: the first idiom.
S: Meaning of "over the moon". => extremely happy.
S: Meaning of "cost an arm and a leg". => very expensive.

Q: Hardly ____ arrived when it started to rain.
O: *had we | we had | did we had | we have
W: "Hardly ... when" describes one action happening immediately after another. | "Hardly" at the start causes inversion: had + subject + past participle. | So: Hardly had we arrived ...
S: Complete: No sooner ____ (we / leave) than the bus came. => had we left.
S: Complete: Scarcely ____ (she / sit) when the bell rang. => had she sat.
