# Round 4 - history_9: expanding short lessons

@lesson hist9-u1-t1
T: History, myth and legend: how to tell them apart
B: **History** studies past events scientifically, using evidence such as documents, artifacts and artworks that can be checked. **Myths and legends** are stories about and from the past that are mostly not supported by evidence. | **Why myths still matter.** They show how past societies thought and what they believed, so historians use them carefully as clues, not as facts. | **Practice – sort these three.** (a) "The first king of the earliest state in East Africa was a serpent called Arwe" – a **myth** (a snake cannot be king). (b) "Eritrea was liberated in 1991" – **historical reality** (supported by records and witnesses). (c) "The Queen of Sheba had a son by King Solomon and later rulers of Abyssinia descended from him" – a **legend** (based on a real tradition but not proven). | **History is to society what memory is to a person.** Without it a society loses its identity. | **Exam tip:** If a question asks "history or myth?", look for evidence that can be checked – no evidence means myth or legend.
