# Round 4 - history_11: expanding short lessons (units 7-9)

@lesson hist11-u7-t1
T: The causes of WWI: long-term and immediate
B: **Long-term causes (remember "MAN").** **M**ilitarism – the great powers built huge armies and navies; **A**lliances – the Triple Alliance (Germany, Austria-Hungary, Italy, 1882) against the Triple Entente (France, Britain, Russia, 1907); **N**ationalism – each state put its national interest first and competed for colonies and trade. | **The Balkan spark.** Serbia, backed by Russia, wanted a large Slav state; Austria-Hungary annexed **Bosnia and Herzegovina in 1908**, which angered Serbs. | **Immediate cause.** On **28 June 1914** Gavrilo Princip, a 19-year-old Bosnian Serb linked to the Black Hand, shot Archduke Franz Ferdinand and his wife in **Sarajevo**. Austria-Hungary blamed Serbia and declared war; the alliances pulled the other powers in within weeks. | **Exam tip:** Always separate the long-term causes (MAN) from the immediate cause (Sarajevo).

@lesson hist11-u7-t4
T: How the war was fought on the two fronts
B: **Western Front.** Germany planned to reach Paris quickly through **Belgium** (war on Belgium 4 August 1914) and got within 40 km of Paris, but was stopped at the **Marne** – France even rushed troops to the front in 2,000 Paris taxis. | **Stalemate.** Both sides dug trenches from the English Channel to Switzerland, protected by barbed wire, mines and machine guns, with "no-man's land" between them. Generals sent masses of men across after artillery barrages, and millions died; at **Verdun (1916)** about 700,000 died for a few kilometres. | **Eastern Front.** More mobile: Russia was beaten in eastern Germany, but Russia and Serbia drove Austria-Hungary out of Serbia; German help then defeated Russia (2.5 million casualties) and knocked Serbia out. | **New weapons.** Aircraft (first scouting, then attacking), submarines, machine guns, tanks and poison gas. | **Memory trick:** "West = trenches and stalemate; East = movement and huge losses."

@lesson hist11-u7-t7
T: Why the USA entered the war (1917)
B: **Blockades.** Britain blockaded Germany; Germany answered with unrestricted submarine warfare. **Lusitania (May 1915).** About 1,100 civilians, over 100 of them Americans, were killed; Germany paused its attacks. **1917.** Germany restarted unrestricted submarine warfare; the USA declared war in **April 1917**, and by 1918 over a million American soldiers were in Europe, tipping the balance. | **Memory trick:** "Subs sank ships, so the States stepped in."

@lesson hist11-u7-t10
T: From bread riots to the tsar's abdication (March 1917)
B: **Step 1 – hunger.** Bread prices rose sharply and rationing began in Petrograd in February 1917. | **Step 2 – the women march.** On **8 March** (International Women's Day) about 10,000 women marched shouting "Peace and Bread" and "Down with the Czar"; other workers joined and factories closed. | **Step 3 – the army changes sides.** Soldiers ordered to fire refused and joined the protesters. | **Step 4 – new government.** On **12 March** the Duma set up a **provisional government**; Nicholas II abdicated on **15 March 1917**. | **Step 5 – dual power.** Kerensky's provisional government stayed in the war, while the **soviets** (workers' and soldiers' councils, strongest in Petrograd) opposed it – which gave Lenin his chance. | **Memory trick:** "Bread → Women → Soldiers → Duma → Abdication."

@lesson hist11-u7-t12
T: The Russian Civil War (1918–1921)
B: **Two sides.** The **Reds** (Communists) against the **Whites** – liberals, anti-Lenin socialists and royalists, backed by the Allies (at one point over 100,000 Allied troops were in Russia). | **Outcome.** The Red Army defeated most Whites by 1920 and controlled all Russia in 1921. | **The royal family.** Held in the Urals, the tsar, his wife and five children were killed by the local Soviet on the night of **16 July 1918**. | **Memory trick:** "Red beat White, 1918–21."

@lesson hist11-u7-t13
T: How WWI ended, step by step
B: **1917 – mixed year.** Allied attacks on the Western Front failed and Russia left the war after the November revolution, but US entry raised Allied morale. | **March 1918 – Germany's last gamble.** Free from the Eastern Front, Germany attacked in the west and by April was within about 80 km of Paris. | **The turn.** French, Moroccan and American troops with hundreds of tanks stopped the advance; with over a million Americans in France the Allies counter-attacked and pushed steadily towards Germany. | **Collapse at home.** Defeats and hardship led to a German army mutiny in November 1918; workers' and soldiers' councils took over in the north, and **Emperor William II** left the country on **9 November**. | **Armistice.** The new government signed it on **11 November 1918**; Germany's allies (Austria-Hungary, the Ottoman Empire, Bulgaria) had already given up. | **Cost.** Over 10 million soldiers and at least 1 million civilians died, and 20 million were wounded. | **Memory trick:** "11th hour, 11th day, 11th month, 1918."

@lesson hist11-u7-t15
T: The Paris Peace Conference and the main terms of Versailles
B: **The Big Four.** Wilson (USA), Clemenceau (France), Lloyd George (Britain) and Orlando (Italy). Wilson wanted a League of Nations and borders based on nationality; France wanted huge reparations and a buffer state on the Rhine; Britain wanted reparations. The result was a compromise. | **Five treaties.** With Germany, Austria, Hungary, Bulgaria and Turkey; Versailles (with Germany) was the most important. | **Four key terms.** (1) **War guilt** – Germany was responsible and must pay **reparations**; (2) army cut to **100,000** men, navy reduced, no air force; (3) **Alsace-Lorraine** returned to France; (4) the **Rhineland** demilitarized. | **Why it mattered.** Germans saw it as a harsh, humiliating dictated peace – one root of WWII. | **Memory trick:** "Guilt, Guns, Ground, Gold" – blame, disarmament, lost land, reparations.

@lesson hist11-u7-t16
T: The wider territorial changes after WWI
B: **Empires broken up.** Austria-Hungary disappeared; Germany and Russia lost land in Eastern Europe. **New states.** Finland, Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania, Poland, Czechoslovakia, Austria and Hungary. **Ottoman lands.** Despite promises to the Arabs, France took **Syria and Lebanon** and Britain took **Iraq and Palestine** as League of Nations **mandates**. | **Memory trick:** "Four empires fell, eight states rose."

@lesson hist11-u7-t17
T: Reparations: the numbers and the consequences
B: **The bill.** In April 1921 the Allied Reparations Commission set the total at **132 billion gold marks** (about 33 billion US dollars), paid in yearly instalments. Germany made its first payment in 1921. | **The crisis.** In 1922 Germany said it could not pay. In 1923 France occupied the **Ruhr**, Germany's industrial heart, to take payment in coal and goods. German workers went on strike and the government paid them by printing money. | **Hyperinflation.** In 1914 one dollar bought 4.2 marks; by November 1923 one dollar cost 4.2 trillion marks, and savings were wiped out. | **The fixes.** The **Dawes Plan (1924)** lowered yearly payments according to Germany's ability to pay and gave a $200 million US loan, bringing a short prosperity (1924–29). | **The long-term effect.** Reparations caused resentment that Hitler used against the "Versailles dictate". | **Memory trick:** "132 billion → Ruhr → Runaway prices → Rescue (Dawes)."

@lesson hist11-u8-t1
T: The Great Depression: the key event of the interwar years
B: **What it was.** The collapse of Western economies at the end of the 1920s and in the 1930s – low economic activity and mass unemployment. | **Causes.** Fundamental: uneven distribution of wealth and an unregulated free market. Immediate: economic decline in many Western countries in the late 1920s, and the financial crisis when US investors pulled money out of Germany for the booming stock market, which then **crashed in October 1929**. | **Effects.** In 1932 one British worker in four and about 6 million Germans (40 %) were unemployed; Americans suffered hunger and homelessness. Despair strengthened extreme parties, which paved the way for dictatorships and WWII. | **Memory trick:** "Crash 1929, Jobless 1932, Dictators 1933."

@lesson hist11-u9-t1
T: Two kinds of nationalism between the wars
B: **Anti-colonial nationalism.** In Africa, the Middle East and Asia national movements aimed at **independence** from European colonial rule. | **Anti-domination nationalism.** In Latin America the countries were already independent but suffered Western, especially US, economic and political domination, so movements aimed to free their economies. | **Key events.** **1916** – the governor of Mecca, encouraged by Britain, declared Arabia independent of the Ottomans; **1923** – the Turkish Republic under Kemal Atatürk replaced the Ottoman Empire; **1928** – Chiang Kai-shek formed a new Chinese government; **1930** – Gandhi's **Salt March** in India against British law; **1931** – Japan invaded Manchuria; **1932** – Saudi Arabia was founded. | **Why they mostly failed then.** Colonial powers were still strong, but these movements trained leaders and ideas that won independence after WWII. | **Memory trick:** "Colonies wanted freedom; Latin America wanted economic freedom."
