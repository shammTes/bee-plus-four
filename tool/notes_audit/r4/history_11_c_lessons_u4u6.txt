@lesson hist11-u4-t1
T: Direct rule versus indirect rule in Asia
B: **Indirect rule.** Local rulers kept their positions inside the colonial system. It was cheaper (fewer officials), gave easier access to resources and disturbed local culture less. | **Direct rule.** Used where rulers resisted: they were removed and replaced by officials from the colonizing country. Example: Britain abolished the Burmese monarchy and ruled Burma from India; France used both systems in Indochina. | **Colonial economies.** Colonies exported raw materials and bought manufactured goods; plantations paid peasants poverty wages. | **Memory trick:** "Indirect = use the king; Direct = remove the king."

@lesson hist11-u4-t4
T: Why the Tai Ping Rebellion matters
B: **Radical programme.** Land for all peasants, equality of women (women had their own army units), shared property, no alcohol or tobacco, and an end to foot-binding – goals similar to the later Communist revolution. | **Cost.** About **20 million** deaths in 14 years – one of the most destructive civil wars in history. | **Effect on the Qing.** The government relied on regional warlords' armies, so the warlords grew powerful, collected taxes and later sold railway and mining rights to foreigners. | **Memory trick:** "1850 rise, 1853 Nanjing, 1864 crushed."

@lesson hist11-u4-t5
T: The background to the Open Door Policy, step by step
B: **Step 1 – the Opium War (1839–42).** Britain sold Indian opium in China to stop silver flowing out of Britain; when China banned it, Britain went to war. The **Treaty of Nanjing (1842)** opened five ports, limited taxes on British goods, gave Britain **Hong Kong**, and let Europeans live under their own laws. | **Step 2 – more defeats.** The Second Opium War (1856–60) and the Treaty of Tianjin (1858) legalized opium and opened more ports. | **Step 3 – spheres of influence.** Warlords sold exclusive trading, railway and mining rights to Britain, France, Germany, Russia and Japan; Japan took **Taiwan** after the 1894 war. | **Step 4 – the US proposal (1899).** Equal trade access for all nations and the unity of China. | **Result.** Spheres of influence remained, but restrictions on other countries' trade were reduced and the powers' fear that one rival would monopolize China eased. | **Memory trick:** "Opium → Ports → Spheres → Open Door."

@lesson hist11-u4-t6
T: The Boxer Rebellion in steps
B: **Who.** A secret society angry at foreign occupation, missionaries and Chinese Christians. **When.** From early 1900 Boxer units killed missionaries and converts. **The response (August 1900).** An allied army of 20,000 British, French, German, Russian, American and Japanese troops took Beijing. **The result.** More concessions and a heavy **indemnity**, which further weakened the Qing. **Aftermath.** The dynasty tried reforms, but merchants and professionals grew impatient and Empress Dowager **Ci Xi** blocked real change, which helped bring the dynasty down in 1911. | **Memory trick:** "Boxers 1900 – eight nations – indemnity – Qing collapse."

@lesson hist11-u5-t1
T: How colonial Latin America worked
B: **Two empires.** From the 16th century Portugal ruled **Brazil**, and Spain ruled parts of North America, all of Central America and most of South America – for over **300 years**. | **A mixed society.** From 1501 Spain allowed intermarriage: children of Europeans and Native Americans were **mestizos**; millions of African slaves were brought for plantations, and children of Europeans and Africans were **mulattoes**. | **Wealth.** Gold and silver went to Europe, but farming on huge estates owned by Spanish and Portuguese landlords was the lasting source of wealth; Native Americans worked on the estates or farmed poor land. Sugar, tobacco, diamonds and hides were exported in return for manufactured goods, and no other nation was allowed to trade. | **The Church.** Rulers wanted to Christianize the natives, so the Catholic Church built cathedrals, hospitals, orphanages and schools – Catholicism is still dominant. | **The class system.** Peninsulares (officials from Europe) at the top; creoles (Europeans born in America) owned land but were second-class; mestizos were the largest group, working as labourers. | **Memory trick:** "Gold, Land, Church, Class."

@lesson hist11-u6-t3
T: Central Africa: from Livingstone to the Congo Free State
B: **Livingstone.** Arrived in **1841** and explored the interior for 30 years until his death in **1873**. | **Stanley.** Sent to find Livingstone, he stayed, explored the **Congo River** in the 1870s and sailed it to the Atlantic. Britain refused his settlement plan, so he turned to **King Leopold II** of Belgium, who hired him in **1876**. | **Scramble.** Leopold's claim to the Congo alarmed others; France quickly took the lands further north. | **Berlin decision.** The area along the Congo became the **Congo Free State**, run by Leopold with free trade and navigation – one of the harshest colonial regimes in Africa. | **Memory trick:** "Livingstone explored, Stanley sold, Leopold took."

@lesson hist11-u6-t4
T: East Africa and the Berlin Conference explained
B: **The rivals.** By 1885 Britain and Germany were the main rivals. Bismarck first saw colonies as unimportant but changed his mind as Germans demanded an empire. Britain wanted East Africa to link its territories from South Africa to Egypt. | **The Berlin Conference (1884–85).** Called by Bismarck: it recognized British and German claims in East Africa, gave Portugal **Mozambique**, created the **Congo Free State** under Leopold II, and in **Article 34** allowed powers to take African land if they notified the others. | **Why it matters.** Africans were not invited; the conference sped up the Scramble. | **Memory trick:** "Berlin: notify and take (Article 34)."

@lesson hist11-u6-t6
T: Comparing colonial rule in Africa
B: **By 1914** seven powers (Britain, France, Germany, Belgium, Italy, Spain, Portugal) had divided Africa; only **Liberia and Ethiopia** stayed independent. | **British indirect rule.** Local rulers enforced decisions made by British administrators; it was cheap but it hardened tribal divisions that caused conflict after independence. | **French and Italian direct rule.** A **governor-general** appointed from Europe ruled through a central bureaucracy. | **French assimilation.** Africans were expected to adopt French culture; a few could stand for office or sit in the French National Assembly. | **Nationalist reaction.** Western-educated Africans saw that colonizers preached democracy and equality but did not practise them in the colonies. | **Memory trick:** "Britain uses chiefs; France makes Frenchmen."
