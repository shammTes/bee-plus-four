@after hist9-u1-n02
T: Completing these points: why history matters, the prehistoric developments and dating time
B: **1. "History helps us to…" – the full list.** (a) learn about the politics, economy and culture of different peoples; (b) draw lessons from past experience so we do not repeat mistakes; (c) value the variety of our heritage and preserve the good parts; (d) be proud of our cultural and national identity. Like memory for a person, history tells a society who it is. | **2. The three key developments of prehistory.** (1) The first modern humans, **Homo sapiens**, evolved in **Africa**. (2) Humans **migrated out of Africa** and settled Eurasia, Australia and the Americas (from roughly 100,000 years ago). (3) Humans began to develop **culture**: tools, fire, language, art and burial customs. | **3. The two ways of dating.** **Relative dating** places events in order or in broad units (decade = 10 years, century = 100 years, millennium = 1,000 years) without an exact date. **Actual (absolute) dating** gives the precise year, using calendars or scientific methods. | **Worked example:** "The 5th century B.C.E." is relative (500–401 B.C.E.); "Adwa, 1 March 1896" is an actual date. | **Memory trick:** "Africa – Out – Culture" for prehistory; "Relative = Range, Actual = Exact."

@after hist9-u2-n01
T: Completing this point: the three stages of human evolution
B: **Stage 1 – the first hominids (Australopithecines).** Lived in Africa about **3–4 million years ago**, walked upright, made simple stone tools, but had small brains. | **Stage 2 – Homo erectus ("upright human").** Appeared about **1.5 million years ago**, had a bigger brain, used stone tools and **fire**, hunted in organized groups and spread beyond Africa. | **Stage 3 – Homo sapiens ("thinking human").** Appeared about **250,000 years ago** and split into two groups: the **Neanderthals** (Europe and Southwest Asia, 100,000–30,000 B.C.E., the first to bury their dead) and **Homo sapiens sapiens**, anatomically modern people like us, who spread from Africa over the whole world by 10,000 B.C.E. | **Memory trick:** "A-E-S: Australopithecus walked, Erectus burned, Sapiens thought." | **Exam tip:** Africa is the cradle of all three stages – a frequent true/false question.

@after hist9-u3-n10
T: Completing this point: the Phoenician economy
B: **Trade first.** Trade was the basis of Phoenician wealth. **Byblos, Tyre, Sidon and Carthage** were trading cities on the coasts of the **Mediterranean Sea**. | **What they sold.** Purple dye (their most famous product, made from sea snails), glass, and **cedar timber** from the forests of Lebanon. | **How far they sailed.** With better ships they traded across the Mediterranean and into the **Atlantic Ocean**, reaching Britain and sailing south along the west coast of Africa. | **Second activity.** Agriculture came second, because the narrow coastal strip had little farmland. | **Memory trick:** "Phoenicians sold P-G-C: Purple, Glass, Cedar." | **Exam tip:** Link geography to economy: a narrow coast with little land pushed the Phoenicians to the sea, which is why they were called "people of the sea".

@after hist9-u4-n04
T: Completing this point: the Four Noble Truths and the Eightfold Path
B: **The Four Noble Truths in plain words.** (1) Life is full of suffering. (2) Suffering is caused by desire (craving). (3) Suffering ends when desire ends. (4) The way to end desire is the **Eightfold Path**. | **The Eightfold Path (the "Middle Way").** Right understanding, right intention, right speech, right action, right livelihood, right effort, right mindfulness and right concentration – in daily terms: speak the truth, do not kill, do not steal, live honestly. | **The goal.** **Nirvana** – release from desire and from the cycle of rebirth. | **Memory trick:** "Suffering – Cause – End – Path" (S-C-E-P). | **Exam tip:** Buddhism rejected the caste system: anyone could reach enlightenment, which is one reason it arose in opposition to Hinduism.

@after hist9-u5-n12
T: Completing this point: the three great Greek philosophers
B: **Socrates (about 470–399 B.C.E.).** Taught by asking questions (the **Socratic method**), believed wrongdoing comes from ignorance and can be cured by education, wrote nothing himself, and was sentenced to death by poison for criticizing Athens. | **Plato (his student).** Wrote dialogues such as **The Republic**, arguing that philosophers should rule, and founded the **Academy** in Athens. | **Aristotle (Plato's student).** Studied at the Academy, wrote on politics, astronomy, biology and physics, compared the constitutions of 158 states, and named monarchy, aristocracy and constitutional government as good forms of government. He also taught Alexander the Great. | **Memory trick:** "S-P-A: Socrates taught Plato, Plato taught Aristotle" – teacher to student, in time order.

@after hist9-u5-n14
T: Completing this point: the two periods of Roman history
B: **Period 1 – the Roman Republic (509–27 B.C.E.).** In **509 B.C.E.** the Romans overthrew the Etruscan king and set up a **republic**: a state without a monarch, where some citizens elect their leaders (two consuls, the Senate and assemblies). Rome conquered Italy and, after the Punic Wars against Carthage, the whole Mediterranean. | **Period 2 – the Roman Empire (27 B.C.E.–476 C.E. in the West).** After civil wars, **Octavian (Augustus)** became the first emperor in **27 B.C.E.** The Empire brought the long **Pax Romana**. The Western Empire fell in **476 C.E.**, while the Eastern (Byzantine) Empire lasted until 1453. | **Memory trick:** "509 kings out, 27 emperor in, 476 West falls." | **Exam tip:** A republic is not a democracy for everyone – in Rome only some citizens could vote.

@after hist9-u6-n10
T: Completing this point: Sertse Dengil, Adi Qoro and the Naibs
B: **What happened in 1578.** Angered by Bahri Negassi Issak's changing alliances, King **Sertse Dengil** attacked the combined forces of Issak and the Turks. At the **Battle of Addi Qoro** (in Tigray) both **Issak and the Turkish Pasha were killed**. | **After the battle.** Sertse Dengil entered **Debarwa**, broke down its walls and destroyed its mosques, then tried to take the Turkish fortress at **Hirghigo** – but **failed** to defeat the Turks there. | **The Naib arrangement (1589).** The Turks handed **Hirghigo and Massawa** to local **Belew chiefs, the Naibs**, to rule on their behalf. This let the Turks keep control of the coast and its hinterland for about **three hundred years**. | **Memory trick:** "1557 Turks arrive, 1578 Addi Qoro, 1589 Naibs rule."
