TITLE: Introduction to climate
S: Climate is the average condition of temperature and precipitation of a place over a long period (about 30 years or more); the word comes from the Greek "klima" (slope, inclination of the Sun). | Its elements are what we measure: temperature, precipitation, humidity, air pressure, wind and cloud. | Its controls (factors) explain why climates differ: latitude, altitude, distance from the sea, ocean currents, relief and prevailing winds. | Climatologists group places with similar averages into climate regions (e.g. Köppen's tropical, dry, temperate, cold and polar groups). | Climate affects farming, settlement, clothing, housing and health, so it is a key link between physical and human geography.
E: Asmara and Massawa have almost the same latitude, but Asmara (≈2350 m) is cool and Massawa (sea level) is hot – altitude, a control, changes the elements temperature and rainfall.
M: "Elements are measured, factors are reasons" — E-M, F-R.
T: Never list latitude or altitude as an "element" of climate: they are factors (controls). Elements are temperature, rainfall, humidity, pressure, wind.

@lesson geo10-u2-l2-2
TITLE: Formation of soil
S: Soil forms when parent rock is broken down by weathering (physical and chemical) into small particles. | Organisms add organic matter: plants drop leaves, and worms, insects and bacteria decompose them into humus. | Climate controls the speed: heat and moisture speed up weathering and decay; dry or cold places form soil slowly. | Relief (slope) matters: steep slopes lose soil to erosion and have thin soils; gentle slopes and valleys collect deep soils. | Time: it can take hundreds to thousands of years to form a few centimetres of mature soil with clear horizons (O, A, B, C).
E: Basalt weathering on the gentle Eritrean plateau around Mendefera produces deep, dark fertile soil, while on the steep eastern escarpment slopes soil is thin because it is washed away.
M: Cl-O-R-P-T — CLimate, Organisms, Relief, Parent material, Time: the five soil-forming factors ("CLORPT").
T: For "explain how soil forms" questions, describe each of the five factors with its effect – a list of names alone earns only part of the marks.

@lesson geo10-u2-l2-4
TITLE: Properties of soil
S: Colour: dark/black soils are rich in humus; red and yellow soils contain iron oxides; grey or white soils are often poorly drained or leached. | Texture: the proportion of sand (gritty, coarse), silt (smooth) and clay (sticky, fine); a balanced mix is called loam. | Structure: how particles group into crumbs, blocks, plates or columns – crumb structure lets air and water move well. | Porosity and permeability: sandy soils drain fast and hold little water; clay soils hold water but drain slowly. | Soil reaction (pH): acidic (below 7), neutral (7) or alkaline (above 7); most crops grow best at about pH 6–7.5.
E: A farmer rubs moist soil between the fingers: it feels gritty and falls apart → sandy, low water-holding; it feels sticky and makes a ribbon → clay, poor drainage. Adding manure improves both.
M: "Can The Soil Please Plants?" — Colour, Texture, Structure, Porosity, pH.
T: Link each property to farming in your answer: e.g. "clay holds water, so it can waterlog crops in the rainy season".

@lesson geo10-u2-l2-6
TITLE: Soil erosion
S: Soil erosion is the removal of topsoil by water, wind or ice faster than new soil forms. | Water erosion goes in stages: splash (raindrops), sheet (thin layer washed off), rill (small channels) and gully (deep channels). | Wind erosion lifts and blows dry, loose particles in deserts and bare fields. | Human causes: deforestation, overgrazing, ploughing up and down slopes, and over-cultivation remove the plant cover that protects soil. | Effects: loss of fertility and crop yields, silting of dams and rivers, desertification and floods.
E: In parts of the Eritrean highlands cutting trees for fuel and building left slopes bare; heavy July–August rain then formed rills and gullies and carried soil into the dams.
M: S-S-R-G — Splash, Sheet, Rill, Gully: water erosion grows from a drop to a ditch.
T: Always tie erosion to removed vegetation: no plant cover = no protection. Then give one natural cause and one human cause.

@lesson geo10-u2-l2-7
TITLE: Soil conservation
S: Soil conservation means methods that protect soil from erosion and keep it fertile. | Biological methods: afforestation and reforestation, planting grass strips, cover crops, crop rotation, controlled grazing and enclosures (area closures). | Mechanical (physical) methods: terracing, contour ploughing, stone bunds, check dams in gullies, and micro-dams. | Fertility methods: manure, compost, fallowing, mixed cropping and correct use of fertilizer. | Community action and laws (e.g. bans on cutting trees, national tree-planting campaigns) make the methods last.
E: Eritrea's highland terracing, hillside closures and summer student work campaigns building bunds and planting trees slow down runoff, trap soil and let vegetation recover.
M: "Trees, Terraces, Tillage, Tending" — the 4 T's of conservation: plant trees, build terraces, plough along contours, feed the soil.
T: Match the method to the problem: steep slope → terraces/contour bunds; gullies → check dams; wind → shelterbelts; low fertility → manure and rotation.

@lesson geo10-u3-l3-2
TITLE: History of world population growth
S: For most of human history growth was very slow: birth and death rates were both high (hunting and gathering, then early farming). | The Agricultural Revolution (about 10 000 years ago) gave more reliable food, so population rose slowly to about 250 million by 1 AD. | From about 1750 the Industrial Revolution, better food supply, medicine and sanitation lowered death rates, so growth speeded up: 1 billion around 1800. | In the 20th century growth exploded (population explosion): 2 billion in 1927, 3 billion in 1960, 6 billion in 1999, 8 billion in 2022. | Today growth is slowing in developed countries but remains fast in many developing countries, including much of Africa.
E: Doubling time: at 2 % growth per year a population doubles in about 70 ÷ 2 = 35 years; at 1 % it doubles in about 70 years ("rule of 70").
M: "Hunters, Farmers, Factories, Explosion" — H-F-F-E: the four stages of world population history.
T: When explaining fast growth, say it was falling death rates (not rising birth rates) that caused the population explosion after 1750.
