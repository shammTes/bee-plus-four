C: Two cars move in the same direction on a straight road at 70 km/h and 50 km/h. The velocity of the faster car relative to the slower one is:
O: *20 km/h forward | 120 km/h forward | 20 km/h backward | 60 km/h forward
W: Relative velocity: v(A rel B) = vA − vB for motion in the same direction. | Substitute: 70 − 50 = 20 km/h. | Answer: 20 km/h forward (in their common direction). | Tip: For motion in opposite directions the speeds add: 70 + 50 = 120 km/h.

@lesson phys10-u2-l2-2
C: Heat reaches the Earth from the Sun mainly by:
O: *radiation | conduction | convection | evaporation
W: Conduction and convection need a material medium (particles). | Space between the Sun and the Earth is almost a vacuum. | Answer: only radiation (electromagnetic waves) can cross a vacuum. | Tip: Conduction – solids; convection – liquids and gases; radiation – no medium needed.

@lesson phys10-u3-l3-1
C: A glass rod rubbed with silk becomes positively charged because:
O: *electrons move from the glass to the silk | protons move from the silk to the glass | electrons are created in the silk | protons are destroyed in the glass
W: Only electrons can move between objects when they are rubbed; protons stay in the nuclei. | The glass loses electrons, so it is left with more protons than electrons. | Answer: electrons move from the glass to the silk (the silk becomes negative). | Tip: Charge is conserved – the silk gains exactly the charge the glass loses.

@lesson phys10-u3-l3-2
C: Which of the following is a semiconductor?
O: *Silicon | Copper | Rubber | Glass
W: Conductors (copper) have many free electrons; insulators (rubber, glass) have almost none. | Semiconductors have a conductivity between them that increases with temperature or doping. | Answer: silicon (germanium is another example). | Tip: Semiconductors are used to make diodes, transistors and solar cells.

@lesson phys10-u3-l3-4
C: Two point charges are 2 m apart. If the distance is reduced to 1 m, the electric force between them becomes:
O: *4 times larger | 2 times larger | 2 times smaller | 4 times smaller
W: Coulomb's law: F = kq₁q₂ / r², so F ∝ 1/r². | New distance = r/2 → F_new = F ÷ (1/2)² = 4F. | Answer: the force becomes 4 times larger. | Tip: Halving the distance quadruples the force; doubling it gives one quarter.

WK: Using Coulomb's law
P: Two charges of +2 μC and −3 μC are 0.3 m apart. Find the size of the force between them and say whether it is attractive or repulsive (k = 9 × 10⁹ N·m²/C²).
ST: Formula: F = k|q₁q₂| / r². | Convert: q₁ = 2 × 10⁻⁶ C, q₂ = 3 × 10⁻⁶ C, r = 0.3 m. | Substitute: F = 9 × 10⁹ × (2 × 10⁻⁶ × 3 × 10⁻⁶) / 0.3² = 9 × 10⁹ × 6 × 10⁻¹² / 0.09. | Calculate: F = 0.054 / 0.09 = 0.6 N. | Opposite signs attract.
A: F = 0.6 N, attractive.

@lesson phys10-u4-l4-4
C: A current of 0.5 A flows through a resistor when the potential difference across it is 6 V. The resistance is:
O: *12 Ω | 3 Ω | 6.5 Ω | 0.083 Ω
W: Ohm's law: V = IR, so R = V ÷ I. | Substitute: R = 6 V ÷ 0.5 A. | Answer: R = 12 Ω. | Tip: Cover the quantity you want in the V-I-R triangle: V on top, I and R below.

@lesson phys10-u4-l4-7
C: In house wiring, the fuse or circuit breaker must be connected in the:
O: *live wire | neutral wire | earth wire | any of the three wires
W: The live wire carries the high potential into the appliance. | A fuse in the live wire cuts off this potential when it melts, so the appliance becomes safe. | Answer: the live wire. | Tip: Switches are also placed in the live wire for the same safety reason.

@lesson phys10-u5-l5-1
C: Which of the following is a luminous object?
O: *The Sun | The Moon | A mirror | A white wall
W: A luminous object produces its own light. | The Moon, a mirror and a wall only reflect light that falls on them (non-luminous). | Answer: the Sun. | Tip: Other luminous sources: a candle flame, a glowing bulb filament, a firefly.

@lesson phys10-u5-l5-2
C: Frosted glass, which lets light through but does not allow objects to be seen clearly, is described as:
O: *translucent | transparent | opaque | luminous
W: Transparent materials let almost all light through and you see clearly (clear glass, water). | Opaque materials let no light through (wood, metal). | Translucent materials let some light through but scatter it, so objects look blurred. | Tip: Frosted glass and oiled paper are classic translucent examples.

@lesson phys10-u5-l5-3
C: The formation of shadows is evidence that light:
O: *travels in straight lines | is a wave | travels faster than sound | can be reflected
W: An opaque object blocks the rays that hit it. | Because light cannot bend around the object, a dark region forms behind it. | Answer: shadows show rectilinear propagation (light travels in straight lines). | Tip: Eclipses and the pinhole camera are also explained by rectilinear propagation.
