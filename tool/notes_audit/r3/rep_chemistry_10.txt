@lesson chem10-u2-l2-1
C: Which substance has hydrogen bonding between its molecules?
O: *NH₃ | CH₄ | H₂S | HCl
W: Hydrogen bonds form when H is bonded directly to a very electronegative atom: N, O or F. | NH₃ has N–H bonds → hydrogen bonding. | CH₄ (C–H), H₂S (S–H) and HCl (Cl–H) do not meet the F-O-N condition. | Tip: Hydrogen bonding explains the high boiling points of H₂O, NH₃ and HF.

@lesson chem10-u4-l4-2
C: According to the Brønsted–Lowry theory, a base is a substance that:
O: *accepts a proton (H⁺) | donates a proton (H⁺) | produces H⁺ ions in water | accepts an electron pair only from metals
W: Brønsted–Lowry acids are proton donors; bases are proton acceptors. | Example: NH₃ + H₂O ⇌ NH₄⁺ + OH⁻ – NH₃ accepts H⁺ from water. | Answer: a base accepts a proton. | Tip: The Arrhenius definition (produces OH⁻ in water) is narrower than the Brønsted–Lowry definition.

@lesson chem10-u4-l4-5
C: A solution has [H⁺] = 1 × 10⁻⁹ M. Its pH and nature are:
O: *pH 9, basic | pH 9, acidic | pH 5, acidic | pH −9, basic
W: pH = −log[H⁺] = −log(10⁻⁹). | pH = 9. | pH above 7 → basic. | Tip: Each pH unit is a tenfold change in [H⁺].
