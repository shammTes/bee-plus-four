TITLE: Banks and banking activities
S: A bank is a financial institution that accepts deposits from savers and lends money to borrowers, earning profit from the difference between the interest it charges and the interest it pays. | Central bank (Bank of Eritrea): issues currency, is banker to the government and to commercial banks, controls the money supply and interest rates, and keeps foreign-exchange reserves. | Commercial banks (e.g. Commercial Bank of Eritrea): accept current, savings and fixed deposits; give loans and overdrafts; transfer money (cheques, transfers); exchange foreign currency; keep valuables safe. | Specialized banks lend for particular purposes, e.g. the Housing and Commerce Bank (housing, business) and development or agricultural banks. | Credit creation: because only part of each deposit must be kept as reserve, banks lend the rest, and deposits multiply through the system (money multiplier = 1 ÷ reserve ratio).
E: Simple interest: a loan of 10 000 Nakfa at 8 % per year for 3 years → I = P × r × t = 10 000 × 0.08 × 3 = 2 400 Nakfa; total to repay = 12 400 Nakfa. Multiplier: with a 20 % reserve ratio, 1 ÷ 0.20 = 5, so a new deposit of 1 000 Nakfa can support up to 5 000 Nakfa of total deposits.
M: "Deposit, Lend, Transfer, Exchange, Safe" — D-L-T-E-S, the main jobs of a commercial bank.
T: Keep central and commercial banks apart: only the central bank issues currency and controls monetary policy; commercial banks serve the public for profit.
