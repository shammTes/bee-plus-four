@lesson bio9-u1-l1-1
C: Biology is best defined as the study of:
O: *living organisms and their life processes | rocks and minerals | matter and energy only | the weather and climate
W: The word comes from Greek "bios" (life) and "logos" (study). | Biology studies living things – their structure, functions, growth, origin and distribution. | Answer: living organisms and their life processes. | Tip: Branches include botany (plants), zoology (animals) and microbiology (microorganisms).

C: Which branch of biology deals with the study of plants?
O: *Botany | Zoology | Ecology | Genetics
W: Botany = plants; zoology = animals; ecology = organisms and their environment; genetics = heredity. | Answer: botany. | Tip: Learn the branch names in pairs with their subjects – they are frequent one-mark questions.

C: Which characteristic is shared by ALL living things?
O: *They respond to stimuli | They can move from place to place | They make their own food | They have a backbone
W: Characteristics of life: movement, respiration, sensitivity (response to stimuli), growth, reproduction, excretion and nutrition (MRS GREN). | Not all living things move from place to place (plants), make their own food (animals) or have backbones. | Answer: responding to stimuli. | Tip: MRS GREN lists the seven characteristics of living things.

@lesson bio9-u1-l1-3
C: The movement of water molecules across a partially permeable membrane from a dilute to a concentrated solution is called:
O: *osmosis | diffusion | active transport | phagocytosis
W: Diffusion is the movement of any particles from high to low concentration. | Osmosis is the special case of WATER moving through a partially permeable membrane, from a dilute solution (more water) to a concentrated one. | Answer: osmosis. | Tip: Active transport moves substances against the concentration gradient and needs energy (ATP).

@lesson bio9-u3-l3-3
C: In a food chain, only about what percentage of the energy at one trophic level is passed on to the next level?
O: *10% | 50% | 90% | 100%
W: Most energy at each level is used for respiration, lost as heat or left in undigested waste. | Only about 10 % is stored in new body tissue that the next level can eat. | Answer: about 10 %. | Tip: This energy loss is why food chains rarely have more than four or five links.

@lesson bio9-u4-l4-1
C: Adaptation is best described as:
O: *a feature that helps an organism survive and reproduce in its environment | any change an animal makes during its lifetime | migration to a new habitat | the death of weak organisms
W: Adaptations are inherited structural, physiological or behavioural features. | They increase an organism's chance of survival and reproduction in its habitat. | Answer: a feature that helps survival and reproduction. | Tip: Give examples by type: structural (camel's hump), physiological (concentrated urine), behavioural (nocturnal activity).

@lesson bio9-u4-l4-3
C: Which adaptation helps a camel survive in the desert?
O: *It produces very concentrated urine and dry faeces | It has a thick layer of fat all over its body | It sweats heavily at low temperatures | It has small, narrow feet
W: Deserts are hot and dry, so saving water is vital. | Camels lose very little water in urine and faeces and can tolerate large rises in body temperature before sweating. | The hump stores fat in one place, so the rest of the body loses heat easily; wide feet stop sinking in sand. | Tip: Explain each adaptation with its benefit ("…so that less water is lost").

C: Fish are adapted to life in water mainly by having:
O: *gills and a streamlined body | lungs and fur | dry scaly skin and claws | a long neck and hooves
W: Gills absorb dissolved oxygen from water. | A streamlined body reduces water resistance; fins help balance and steering. | Answer: gills and a streamlined body. | Tip: In exam answers link each structure to the problem of living in water (getting O₂, moving easily).

WK: Explaining an animal adaptation
P: Explain three ways in which a polar bear is adapted to the cold Arctic environment.
ST: Identify the problem: very low temperatures cause heat loss. | Adaptation 1: thick fur and a thick layer of fat (blubber) under the skin insulate the body and reduce heat loss. | Adaptation 2: small ears and a large body give a small surface area to volume ratio, so less heat is lost. | Adaptation 3: white fur camouflages it against snow for hunting; wide, furry paws spread its weight on ice.
A: Thick fur and blubber (insulation), small surface area to volume ratio (less heat loss), and white fur / wide paws (camouflage, walking on ice).
