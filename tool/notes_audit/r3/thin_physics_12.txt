TITLE: Principle of flotation
S: Archimedes' principle: a body in a fluid is pushed up by a buoyant force equal to the weight of the fluid it displaces: F_b = ρ_fluid × V_displaced × g. | Principle of flotation: a floating body displaces a weight of fluid equal to its own weight (F_b = W). | Compare densities: if the body's average density is less than the fluid's, it floats; if greater, it sinks; if equal, it stays suspended. | For a floating body the fraction submerged = ρ_body ÷ ρ_fluid. | Applications: steel ships float because their hollow shape makes their average density smaller than water; submarines change their ballast; hydrometers measure liquid density; balloons float in air.
E: A wooden block of density 600 kg/m³ floats in water (1000 kg/m³). Fraction submerged = 600 ÷ 1000 = 0.6, so 60 % of the block is under water and 40 % above.
M: "Float = Weight = Displaced weight" — F-W-D: buoyant force equals the body's weight equals the weight of displaced fluid.
T: Ships float higher in sea water than in fresh water because sea water is denser, so less volume must be displaced to balance the same weight.
