C: Land degradation is best defined as:
O: *a process that lowers the current or potential capability of land to produce goods and services | the natural formation of soil from rocks | the building of terraces on slopes | an increase in soil fertility
W: The lesson defines land degradation as a process that lowers the present and/or potential ability of land to produce goods and services. | Continued degradation permanently reduces biological potential and living conditions. | Answer: lowering of land's productive capability. | Tip: Its causes are a combination of biophysical, social, economic and political factors.

C: Which of the following is a human cause of land degradation?
O: *Overgrazing | Low annual rainfall | Steep natural slopes | Strong natural winds
W: Human causes: overgrazing, deforestation, poor farming methods (ploughing up and down slopes), over-cultivation. | Low rainfall, steep slopes and wind are natural (biophysical) factors. | Answer: overgrazing. | Tip: In essay answers separate natural and human causes.

WK: Explaining the chain of land degradation
P: Explain how overgrazing on a hillside can lead to land degradation and suggest two remedies.
ST: Too many animals eat the grass faster than it regrows, leaving bare soil. | Hooves compact and loosen the soil; bare soil is exposed to raindrop splash and runoff. | Topsoil and nutrients are washed away → rills and gullies form → lower productivity (land degradation). | Remedies: reduce stocking rates / rotational grazing, and area closures with tree and grass planting, plus terraces or bunds to slow runoff.
A: Overgrazing removes plant cover → erosion of topsoil → loss of productivity. Remedies: controlled grazing and area closure/reforestation with soil-conservation structures.
