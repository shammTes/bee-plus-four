@lesson bio10-u3-l3-2
TITLE: Lymphatic vs circulatory system
S: The circulatory system is a closed system: the heart pumps blood through arteries, capillaries and veins in a complete circuit. | The lymphatic system is an open, one-way system: it starts with blind-ended lymph capillaries in the tissues and ends by emptying into veins near the heart (subclavian veins). | Blood has red cells, white cells, platelets and plasma; lymph is a clear fluid with white cells (lymphocytes) and no red cells. | Blood moves by the heart's pumping; lymph moves by squeezing of skeletal muscles and one-way valves (no pump). | Functions: blood transports O₂, nutrients, hormones and wastes; lymph returns tissue fluid to the blood, absorbs fats from the small intestine (lacteals) and fights infection in lymph nodes.
E: After a long day standing, ankles may swell slightly: tissue fluid collects because leg muscles moved little, so less lymph was squeezed back. Walking pumps the lymph and the swelling goes down.
M: "Blood has a Pump, Lymph has Muscles" — closed with a heart vs open with muscle squeeze.
T: Present comparisons in a two-column table (feature | blood system | lymph system) – it is clear and examiners mark each row.

@lesson bio10-u5-l5-2
TITLE: Mechanisms of breathing
S: Inhalation: the diaphragm contracts and flattens; the external intercostal muscles contract, moving the ribs up and out. | This increases the volume of the chest cavity, so pressure in the lungs falls below atmospheric pressure, and air rushes in. | Exhalation: the diaphragm relaxes and becomes dome-shaped; the intercostal muscles relax and the ribs move down and in. | Chest volume decreases, lung pressure rises above atmospheric pressure, and air is pushed out. | In the alveoli, O₂ diffuses into the blood and CO₂ diffuses out; alveoli are adapted by a huge surface area, thin walls (one cell), moisture and a rich blood supply.
E: Bell-jar model: pulling the rubber sheet (diaphragm) down increases the volume inside the jar, the pressure drops and the balloons (lungs) inflate – exactly like inhalation.
M: "Volume Up, Pressure Down, Air In" — V-U, P-D, A-I (reverse everything for exhalation).
T: Always link the three steps: muscle action → volume change → pressure change → air movement. Missing the pressure step is the commonest error.

@lesson bio10-u7-l7-2
TITLE: Internal receptors
S: Receptors detect stimuli; internal receptors (interoceptors) detect changes inside the body. | Chemoreceptors in the aorta, carotid arteries and brain detect CO₂, O₂ and pH of the blood – they control breathing rate. | Baroreceptors (pressure receptors) in the aorta and carotid arteries detect blood pressure. | Osmoreceptors in the hypothalamus detect water concentration of the blood (thirst and ADH). | Proprioceptors in muscles, tendons and joints tell the brain the position and stretch of body parts; hunger, pain and fullness receptors are also internal.
E: During a race CO₂ in the blood rises; chemoreceptors detect the fall in pH and signal the breathing centre in the medulla → breathing becomes faster and deeper.
M: "Chemical, Pressure, Water, Position" — CPWP: the four internal receptor types and what they sense.
T: Name the receptor, what it detects and the response it triggers – a three-part answer earns full marks.
