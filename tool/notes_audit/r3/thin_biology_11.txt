@lesson bio11-u1-l1-2
TITLE: Malfunctioning of the endocrine glands
S: Each gland can produce too much (hyper-) or too little (hypo-) hormone, and either one disturbs the body. | Pituitary – growth hormone: too little in childhood → dwarfism; too much in childhood → gigantism; too much in adults → acromegaly (enlarged hands, feet, jaw). | Thyroid – thyroxine: too little → goitre (with iodine shortage), tiredness, weight gain, and cretinism in children; too much → hyperthyroidism (fast heartbeat, weight loss, bulging eyes). | Pancreas – insulin: too little or cells not responding → diabetes mellitus (high blood sugar, sugar in urine, thirst, frequent urination). | Adrenal glands: too little cortisol/aldosterone → Addison's disease; too much cortisol → Cushing's syndrome.
E: A person living far from the sea with little iodine in the diet develops a swelling in the neck. Iodine is needed to make thyroxine; the thyroid enlarges trying to make more → goitre. Prevention: iodized salt.
M: "Pit-Grow, Thy-Iodine, Pan-Sugar" — pituitary controls growth, thyroid needs iodine, pancreas controls blood sugar.
T: Answer disorder questions in three parts: gland → hormone (too much or too little) → symptom. Missing the hormone name loses marks.

@lesson bio11-u2-l2-2
TITLE: The male reproductive system
S: Testes (in the scrotum) produce sperm and the hormone testosterone; the scrotum keeps them about 2–3 °C below body temperature, which sperm production needs. | Sperm mature and are stored in the epididymis. | During ejaculation sperm travel through the vas deferens (sperm duct) to the urethra. | Glands add seminal fluid: seminal vesicles (fructose for energy), prostate gland (alkaline fluid that neutralizes vaginal acidity) and Cowper's glands (lubricating fluid); sperm + fluid = semen. | The urethra in the penis carries semen out (and urine at other times, never both together).
E: Path of a sperm cell: testis → epididymis → vas deferens → (seminal vesicle and prostate add fluid) → urethra → out of the penis.
M: "SEVEN UP" — Seminiferous tubules, Epididymis, Vas deferens, Ejaculatory duct, (Nothing), Urethra, Penis.
T: When asked for functions, give a reason too: e.g. "the scrotum is outside the body to keep the testes cooler, because sperm are made best below body temperature".

@lesson bio11-u3-l3-2
TITLE: Joints
S: A joint is where two or more bones meet. | Immovable (fibrous) joints: bones are fixed by fibrous tissue – sutures of the skull. | Slightly movable (cartilaginous) joints: bones joined by cartilage pads – between vertebrae, pubic symphysis. | Freely movable (synovial) joints: a capsule with synovial fluid (lubricant), cartilage on bone ends (reduces friction) and ligaments (join bone to bone). | Types of synovial joint: ball-and-socket (shoulder, hip – movement in all directions), hinge (elbow, knee – one plane), pivot (atlas–axis, turning the head), gliding (wrist, ankle bones), saddle (thumb).
E: Shoulder vs elbow: the shoulder (ball-and-socket) lets you swing the arm in a full circle; the elbow (hinge) only bends and straightens the forearm like a door.
M: "Ball Has Pivot Glide Saddle" — B-H-P-G-S, the five synovial joints.
T: Ligament joins bone to bone; tendon joins muscle to bone. Do not mix them up in joint diagrams.

@lesson bio11-u4-l4-2
TITLE: Voluntary (skeletal) muscles
S: Voluntary muscles are attached to bones by tendons and are controlled consciously by the nervous system; under a microscope they are striated (striped) and multinucleated. | A muscle can only pull (contract); it cannot push. | So muscles work in antagonistic pairs: when one contracts, the other relaxes. | The origin is the end attached to a fixed bone; the insertion is the end attached to the bone that moves. | The flexor bends a joint (biceps at the elbow); the extensor straightens it (triceps).
E: Bending the arm: biceps contracts (shortens), triceps relaxes, the forearm is pulled up. Straightening the arm: triceps contracts, biceps relaxes, the forearm is pulled down.
M: "Biceps Bends, Triceps sTraightens" — B-B, T-T.
T: Always describe both muscles of the pair and the movement – "the biceps contracts" alone is half an answer.

@lesson bio11-u5-l5-2
TITLE: Control of water balance (osmoregulation)
S: Osmoregulation keeps the water content and solute concentration of the blood constant. | Each kidney has about a million nephrons: blood is filtered at the glomerulus into Bowman's capsule (ultrafiltration). | Useful substances (all glucose, most water and salts) are reabsorbed in the tubule; water is reabsorbed especially in the collecting duct. | The hypothalamus detects blood concentration; the pituitary gland releases ADH (antidiuretic hormone). ADH makes the collecting duct more permeable, so more water returns to the blood. | Negative feedback: when blood becomes normal again, ADH secretion falls back.
E: On a hot day in Massawa you sweat a lot and drink little: blood becomes concentrated → more ADH → more water reabsorbed → small volume of dark, concentrated urine. After drinking lots of water: less ADH → large volume of pale, dilute urine.
M: "ADH = Add Water Back" — high ADH, more water kept.
T: In ADH questions follow the chain: detector (hypothalamus) → gland (pituitary) → hormone (ADH) → target (collecting duct) → effect (urine volume).

@lesson bio11-u6-l6-2
TITLE: Plant tissues
S: Meristematic tissue: small, dividing cells at root and shoot tips (apical meristems – growth in length) and in the cambium (lateral meristem – growth in thickness). | Permanent simple tissues: parenchyma (thin-walled living cells for storage and photosynthesis), collenchyma (unevenly thickened living cells for flexible support in young stems) and sclerenchyma (dead cells with lignified walls for rigid support – fibres and stone cells). | Complex tissues: xylem (vessels and tracheids – carry water and minerals up, dead, lignified) and phloem (sieve tubes and companion cells – carry sugars both ways, living). | Dermal tissue: epidermis with a waxy cuticle, stomata and root hairs. | These tissues form three tissue systems: dermal, vascular and ground.
E: Why can a young sunflower stem bend in the wind without breaking? Collenchyma gives flexible support; later, as the stem thickens, sclerenchyma and xylem give rigid support.
M: "Xylem carries Xtra water up; Phloem carries Food" — X-water, P-food.
T: Remember which tissues are dead: xylem vessels and sclerenchyma. Phloem sieve tubes are living (but have no nucleus).

@lesson bio11-u2-l2-4
TITLE: Contraception
S: Contraception means methods used to prevent pregnancy by stopping sperm from meeting the egg or stopping implantation. | Natural methods: abstinence (100 % effective) and the rhythm/calendar method (avoiding intercourse around ovulation – unreliable). | Barrier methods: male and female condoms and the diaphragm stop sperm reaching the egg; condoms also protect against STIs including HIV. | Hormonal methods: the pill, injections and implants contain hormones that stop ovulation; the IUD prevents implantation. | Surgical (permanent) methods: vasectomy (cutting the sperm ducts) and tubal ligation (cutting/tying the oviducts).
E: A couple wants to delay children and also avoid any risk of infection: the condom is the best single choice because it is a barrier to sperm and to infections; the pill alone would not protect against STIs.
M: "No sex, Natural, Barrier, Hormone, Surgery" — the five groups of contraception, from abstinence to permanent.
T: For each method state HOW it works (stops sperm, stops ovulation, stops implantation) and whether it protects against STIs.
