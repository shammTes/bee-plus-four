WK: Model answer: primary and secondary sources
P: (Exam question) Distinguish between primary and secondary sources of history and give two examples of each.
ST: Define a source: the material out of which history is written – no history without a source. | Primary sources come directly from the period itself: letters, diaries, treaties, tools, graves, photographs, eyewitness accounts (also memoirs and oral traditions). | Secondary sources are written later by people who did not take part: books, articles, theses and dissertations. | Conclude: a diary of a soldier is primary; a modern history book chapter about the war is secondary.
A: Primary sources date from the time of the event (e.g. letters, treaties, artifacts, eyewitness accounts); secondary sources are later accounts by non-participants (e.g. books, articles, theses).

O: *Adulis | Meroe | Napata | Memphis

W: The lesson says the Periplus of the Erythrean Sea, a Greek guidebook, mentioned Aksum for the first time around 1 C.E. | The Book of the Dead is Egyptian; the Code of Hammurabi is Babylonian.

Q: Why are Aksumite coins important to historians?
O: *They give the names of rulers and the chronology of events | They show the shape of the pyramids | They were the first paper money | They prove trade with China
W: The lesson says Aksumite coins of gold, silver and bronze carried the names of the rulers. | So they are important artifacts that provide names and the chronology of events. | Coinage began about 270 C.E.

Q: Who introduced Christianity into the Aksumite kingdom in 330–340 C.E.?
O: *Syrian missionaries | Portuguese priests | Egyptian pharaohs | Ottoman officials
W: The lesson says Christianity was introduced by Syrian missionaries in 330–340 C.E. | Before that, Aksumites were polytheistic and worshipped gods such as the moon god. | Portuguese and Ottomans arrived in the 16th century.

Q: What did the Egyptians call the Land of Punt, and why?
O: *"Land of the gods", because it was rich in gold, ivory and slaves | "Land of the rivers", because of the Nile | "Land of iron", because of its mines | "Land of salt", because of the Danakil
W: The lesson says the area was rich in gold, ivory and slaves, so the Egyptians called it Punt, meaning the land of the gods. | Punt emerged around 2500 B.C. and included the Eritrean Red Sea coast.
