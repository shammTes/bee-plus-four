@lesson geo11-u1-l1-5
C: The point on the Earth's surface directly above the focus of an earthquake is called the:
O: *epicentre | focus (hypocentre) | fault | seismograph
W: The focus (hypocentre) is the point underground where the rocks break. | The epicentre is the point on the surface directly above it, where shaking is usually strongest. | A seismograph records the waves; earthquake strength is given on the Richter scale. | Tip: Draw a simple diagram: focus below, epicentre above, waves spreading out.

@lesson geo11-u2-l2-1
C: Gradational forces are those that:
O: *wear down and level the Earth's surface, e.g. weathering, erosion and deposition | build up the land, e.g. folding and faulting | come only from inside the Earth | form only volcanoes
W: Internal (endogenic) forces such as folding, faulting and volcanism build up relief. | External (exogenic) gradational forces – weathering, mass wasting, erosion and deposition by water, wind, ice and waves – wear it down and fill low places. | Answer: wearing down and levelling. | Tip: "Degradation" lowers land (erosion); "aggradation" builds it up by deposition – both are gradation.

@lesson geo11-u3-l3-1
C: Most sea waves are formed by:
O: *wind blowing over the water surface | the rotation of the Earth | the gravitational pull of the Moon | underwater rivers
W: Wind transfers energy to the sea surface by friction. | The size of a wave depends on wind speed, how long it blows and the fetch (distance of open water). | Tides (not ordinary waves) are caused by the Moon and Sun; tsunamis by earthquakes. | Tip: Longer fetch + stronger wind + longer duration = bigger waves.

C: Waves that remove material from a beach because they are steep and frequent with a strong backwash are called:
O: *destructive waves | constructive waves | tidal waves | swash waves
W: Destructive waves are high, steep and frequent; their backwash is stronger than their swash, so they carry material away. | Constructive waves are low and gentle with a strong swash, so they build beaches. | Answer: destructive waves. | Tip: Swash = water moving up the beach; backwash = water moving back down.
