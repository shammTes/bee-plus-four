@lesson eng9-u3-l3-2
TITLE: Punctuation (1) – full stop, question mark, comma, capital letters
S: Begin every sentence and every proper noun (names of people, places, days, months, languages) with a capital letter: "On Monday Senait went to Keren." | End a statement or a polite command with a full stop (.), a direct question with a question mark (?), and a strong feeling with an exclamation mark (!). | Use commas to separate items in a list: "We bought bread, milk, eggs and sugar." | Use a comma after an introductory word or clause: "However, …", "When the bell rang, we left." | Use a comma before "and/but/so" when they join two complete sentences, and to mark off a name you speak to: "Abel, come here."
E: Wrong: "my brother lives in asmara he works in a bank" → Right: "My brother lives in Asmara. He works in a bank." (capital letters for the sentence start and the place name, a full stop between two sentences).
M: "Capital Starts, Stop Ends, Commas Separate" — C-S-S.
T: Read your sentence aloud: where you pause slightly put a comma; where your voice stops and drops put a full stop; where it rises in a question put a question mark.

@lesson eng9-u5-l5-2
TITLE: Punctuation (2) – direct and reported speech
S: Direct speech copies the exact words inside quotation marks: Selam said, "I am tired." | Put a comma (or ? or !) before the closing quotation mark, and start the spoken words with a capital letter. | Start a new line (paragraph) each time a new person speaks. | Reported speech has no quotation marks: Selam said (that) she was tired – the tense moves one step back after a past reporting verb. | A reported question is a statement, so it ends with a full stop, not a question mark: He asked me where I lived.
E: Direct: "Where is the library?" asked Yonas. → Reported: Yonas asked where the library was. (no quotation marks, statement word order, full stop).
M: "Quotes for exact words, no quotes for reported words" — and the punctuation mark goes INSIDE the closing quotes.
T: The most common error is a question mark at the end of a reported question ("He asked what my name was?"). Remove it and use statement order.

@lesson eng9-u6-l6-2
TITLE: Parts of speech (2) – making the verb agree
S: Find the subject (a noun or pronoun) and decide if it is singular or plural. | A singular subject in the present simple takes verb + -s: "The girl reads." A plural subject takes the base verb: "The girls read." | Ignore phrases between the subject and the verb: "The list of names is long." | Words like everybody, someone, each, nobody are singular: "Everybody has arrived." | With either…or / neither…nor the verb agrees with the nearer subject; with "together with / as well as" the verb agrees with the first subject.
E: "The teacher, together with her students, (visit/visits) the museum." First subject = the teacher (singular) → visits. "Either the boys or their father (is/are) at home." Nearer subject = father → is.
M: "One subject, one -s" — the -s goes on the noun OR on the verb, not both (the dog barks / the dogs bark).
T: Underline the subject and draw an arrow to the verb before choosing – it stops you being misled by the noun closest to the verb.
