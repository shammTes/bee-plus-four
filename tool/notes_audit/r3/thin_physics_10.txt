TITLE: Transmission of light
S: When light meets a material, it can be transmitted (pass through), reflected (bounce off) or absorbed (turned into heat). | Transparent materials (clear glass, clean water, air) transmit almost all light, so objects behind them are seen clearly. | Translucent materials (frosted glass, tracing paper, thin cloth) transmit some light but scatter it, so objects look blurred. | Opaque materials (wood, metal, stone) transmit no light; they reflect or absorb it and cast shadows. | Light travels at 3 × 10⁸ m/s in a vacuum and slower in materials (about 2 × 10⁸ m/s in glass).
E: A bathroom window is made of frosted glass: it is translucent, so daylight still enters but people outside cannot see clearly inside.
M: "Clear, Cloudy, Closed" — transparent, translucent, opaque.
T: When classifying a material, ask two questions: does light pass? (no → opaque) – can I see clearly through it? (yes → transparent, no → translucent).
