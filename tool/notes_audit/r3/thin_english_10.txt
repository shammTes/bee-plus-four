@lesson eng10-u2-l2-2
TITLE: Future continuous
S: Form: will + be + verb-ing: "I will be studying." Negative: will not (won't) be + -ing; question: Will you be + -ing? | Use 1: an action in progress at a particular time in the future: "At 8 pm tomorrow I will be watching the match." | Use 2: an action that will interrupt or be interrupted by a shorter action (future simple): "When you arrive, we will be having dinner." | Use 3: polite questions about someone's plans: "Will you be using the computer this evening?" | Time expressions: at this time tomorrow, at 6 o'clock next Monday, this time next week; do not use it with stative verbs (know, like, own).
E: "This time next year, Aman ___ (study) at college." → will be studying. "Don't phone at 7; I ___ (cook)." → will be cooking.
M: "WILL BE + ING = a film paused in the future" — picture the action in the middle at that future time.
T: Look for "at this time tomorrow / at (time) next…" in the question – these phrases almost always signal the future continuous.

@lesson eng10-u7-l7-2
TITLE: Present perfect and past perfect passive
S: Present perfect passive = has/have + been + V3: "The work has been finished." | Past perfect passive = had + been + V3: "The work had been finished before the manager came." | To convert: take the active object as the new subject, keep the perfect tense in has/have/had, add "been" and the V3. | Use has with a singular subject and have with a plural subject; "had" is used for all subjects. | Add "by + agent" only when it is important who did it.
E: Active: "They have repaired the road." → Passive: "The road has been repaired." Active: "Somebody had stolen the money before the police arrived." → "The money had been stolen before the police arrived."
M: "Perfect passive = HAVE + BEEN + V3" (H-B-3); past perfect just turns HAVE into HAD.
T: Do not forget "been": "The road has repaired" is wrong because roads cannot repair. Check that the new subject cannot do the action itself.

@lesson eng10-u8-l8-2
TITLE: Confusing verbs
S: lie – lay – lain (intransitive, no object: rest flat): "I lie down after lunch." lay – laid – laid (transitive, needs an object: put down): "She laid the book on the table." | rise – rose – risen (no object: go up): "The sun rises." raise – raised – raised (with an object: lift): "Raise your hand." | sit – sat – sat (no object) vs set – set – set (put something). | say vs tell: say something (to someone); tell someone something: "He said that…", "He told me that…". | borrow (take from) vs lend (give to); bring (towards the speaker) vs take (away from the speaker).
E: "Yesterday the price of sugar (raised/rose)." → rose (no object). "Can you (borrow/lend) me your pen?" → lend. "She (said/told) me the answer." → told (there is a person object).
M: "rAIse, lAY, sET take An object" — the verbs with the "a/e" sound before the object are the transitive ones.
T: Ask "does the verb have an object right after it?" If yes, choose lay / raise / set / tell / lend; if no, choose lie / rise / sit / say.
