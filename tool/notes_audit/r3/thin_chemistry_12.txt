TITLE: Half-life calculations
S: Half-life (t½) is the time for half of the radioactive nuclei in a sample to decay; it is constant for each isotope and not changed by temperature or pressure. | Number of half-lives: n = total time ÷ t½. | Amount left = initial amount × (½)ⁿ. | Fraction decayed = 1 − (½)ⁿ. | To find t½ from data, find how long it takes for the amount (or activity) to fall to half.
E: Iodine-131 has t½ = 8 days. From 80 g, after 24 days: n = 24 ÷ 8 = 3; left = 80 × (½)³ = 80 ÷ 8 = 10 g; decayed = 70 g.
M: "Halve, Halve, Halve" — 1 → ½ → ¼ → ⅛ → 1/16: count the halvings.
T: For whole numbers of half-lives draw a halving chain (80 → 40 → 20 → 10); it is faster and avoids power mistakes.

TITLE: Nuclear reactions and energy
S: In a nuclear equation both mass numbers (top) and atomic numbers (bottom) must balance. | Alpha decay emits ⁴₂He (mass −4, atomic number −2); beta decay emits ⁰₋₁e (atomic number +1, mass unchanged); gamma rays change neither. | Fission: a heavy nucleus (U-235) absorbs a neutron and splits into two smaller nuclei plus 2–3 neutrons, which can cause a chain reaction (nuclear reactors, atomic bombs). | Fusion: light nuclei (hydrogen isotopes) join to form heavier ones (helium) – the energy source of the Sun. | The energy comes from the mass defect: E = mc² (c = 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s), so a tiny loss of mass releases huge energy.
E: ²³⁸₉₂U → ⁴₂He + X. Mass: 238 − 4 = 234; atomic number: 92 − 2 = 90 → X = ²³⁴₉₀Th (thorium). Energy from 0.001 kg mass loss: E = 0.001 × (3 × 10⁸)² = 9 × 10¹³ J.
M: "Fission Fragments, Fusion Fuses" — fission splits heavy nuclei, fusion joins light ones.
T: Balance the top numbers and the bottom numbers separately and write both sums in your working.
