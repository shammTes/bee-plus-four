C: In an axiomatic system, a statement that is accepted as true without proof is called:
O: *an axiom (postulate) | a theorem | a corollary | a definition of a term
W: An axiomatic system starts from undefined terms, definitions and axioms. | Axioms (postulates) are accepted without proof. | Theorems are statements proved from the axioms; corollaries follow directly from theorems. | Tip: "Point", "line" and "plane" are undefined terms in Euclidean geometry.

@lesson math10-u4-l4-1
C: A cube has how many faces, edges and vertices respectively?
O: *6, 12, 8 | 8, 12, 6 | 6, 8, 12 | 12, 6, 8
W: A cube has 6 square faces, 12 edges and 8 vertices. | Check with Euler's formula: F + V − E = 6 + 8 − 12 = 2. | Answer: 6, 12, 8. | Tip: Euler's formula F + V − E = 2 holds for every convex polyhedron – use it to check counts.

@lesson math10-u5-l5-1
C: Which of the following is NOT a valid condition for proving two triangles congruent?
O: *AAA | SSS | SAS | ASA
W: SSS, SAS, ASA (and AAS, RHS) guarantee congruence. | AAA only guarantees that the triangles have the same shape (similar), not the same size. | Answer: AAA. | Tip: SSA is also not a valid test in general.

C: In triangles ABC and PQR, AB = PQ, ∠B = ∠Q and BC = QR. The triangles are congruent by:
O: *SAS | SSS | ASA | RHS
W: Two sides are equal (AB = PQ, BC = QR). | The equal angle ∠B = ∠Q lies between those two sides (included angle). | Answer: SAS. | Tip: In SAS the angle must be the one between the two given sides.
