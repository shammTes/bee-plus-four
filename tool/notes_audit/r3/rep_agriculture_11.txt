C: Lines on a map joining places with the same mean temperature are called:
O: *isotherms | isohyets | isobars | contours
W: Isotherms join equal temperature; isohyets join equal rainfall; isobars join equal pressure; contours join equal height. | Answer: isotherms. | Tip: Relative humidity – the amount of moisture in the air compared with saturated air – falls as temperature rises.

C: Why is genetic diversity important for agriculture?
O: *It provides genes for breeding high-yielding and drought- or pest-resistant varieties | It reduces the number of species on farms | It makes all crops identical | It prevents any change in crop varieties
W: Genetic diversity is the variety of genes within a species (e.g. many local sorghum or barley varieties). | Breeders use these genes to develop varieties that resist drought and pests and give high yields. | Losing local varieties narrows these options. | Tip: Biodiversity is described at genetic, species and ecosystem levels.

O: *climate (rainfall, temperature), altitude, soils and the crops and livestock they support | political boundaries | population density only | the location of markets
W: An agro-ecological zone is an area with similar climate, altitude, soils and vegetation, so the same crops and livestock suit it. | The lesson notes that the agro-ecological map of Eritrea shows six major zones. | Answer: climate, altitude, soils and suitable crops/livestock. | Tip: Link each zone to its typical crops, e.g. barley, wheat and taff in the cooler highlands.

C: Which parts of the light spectrum does chlorophyll absorb most strongly for photosynthesis?
O: *Violet-blue and red | Green only | Yellow and orange | Infrared only
W: Chlorophyll absorbs mainly violet-blue and red light. | It reflects green light, which is why leaves look green. | Answer: violet-blue and red. | Tip: Monocots have one cotyledon (maize, sorghum); dicots have two (beans, peas).

@lesson agri11-u3-l3-3
C: Why is nitrogen fertilizer often applied in split doses rather than all at once?
O: *To reduce losses by leaching in heavy rain and supply N when the crop needs it | Because nitrogen is not needed by cereals | To make the soil more acidic | Because nitrogen only works in dry soil
W: Nitrate is very soluble and is easily washed (leached) below the roots by heavy rain. | Applying it in parts – at sowing and again at an active growth stage – matches the crop's demand and reduces losses. | Answer: less leaching, better timing. | Tip: Organic manure feeds soil life and improves structure; mineral fertilizer corrects a measured nutrient shortage.
